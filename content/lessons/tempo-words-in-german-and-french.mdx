Italian is the default language of tempo, but not the only one. Bach, Beethoven, Schumann,
Brahms and Mahler often marked tempo in **German**; Couperin, Debussy, Ravel and Fauré in
**French**. Some wanted the precision of their own tongue; some wanted the national colour. A
few — Beethoven, Mahler — printed both.

This is a reference card. You don't need to memorise it — recognise the word, find it here.

## German

| German | Literally | Italian it sits near |
|---|---|---|
| Langsam | slow | Adagio / Lento |
| Getragen | "carried", sustained | Largo / Grave |
| Ruhig | calm, at rest | Andante tranquillo |
| Mäßig | moderate | Moderato |
| Bewegt | "moved", agitated | Con moto / Allegretto |
| Lebhaft | lively | Vivace |
| Rasch | quick | Allegro |
| Schnell | fast | Presto |

Modifiers travel too: **nicht zu** = "not too" (*nicht zu schnell*, like *ma non troppo*),
**sehr** = very, **etwas** = somewhat, **immer** = always.

## French

| French | Literally | Italian it sits near |
|---|---|---|
| Lent | slow | Lento / Adagio |
| Modéré | moderate | Moderato |
| Animé | animated | Allegro |
| Vif / Vite | lively / fast | Vivace / Presto |
| Retenu | held back | Ritenuto |
| Cédez | "yield" — give way, slow | Rit. |
| En pressant | pressing on | Stringendo |
| Au mouvement | back in tempo | A tempo |
