Before vibrato touches a string, the **motion itself** has to become easy — a small, light,
quick flick of the hand that you can do without thinking. Teachers call it the **pulse**: a
forward-and-rebound, forward-and-rebound, like a gentle bounce. Get the pulse free and loose
first, away from the pressure of playing an actual note, and everything after it goes faster.

## The rhythm of it

A pulse is one forward-and-back per tap. Start with one per beat:

## Two ways to feel it, both silent

**Off the instrument.** Hold your left hand up as if the violin were there. Let the hand flick
gently forward from the wrist (or from the forearm — try both) and spring back, over and over,
loose. The fingers hang soft. It should feel like shaking water off your fingertips, only
smaller and more regular.

**The knock.** Bring the hand to the violin neck in playing shape, thumb light. Now rock the
hand *backward* toward the pegs and let it come forward again — and let a knuckle or the heel
of the hand tap gently against the pegs or the side of the scroll at the back of each rock. The
tap gives the motion a rhythm and a clear end point, and it proves the hand is actually moving
rather than the arm tensing.

<TryThis>
  One minute of the off-the-instrument flick, then one minute of the knock against the pegs, at
  60 on the metronome, one pulse per click. Then stop. Do this at the start of every practice
  session for a week before you try to make a note out of it — the pulse becoming automatic is
  the whole foundation.
</TryThis>

## Hear it — you can't, yet

There's no sound here on purpose. The pulse is pure motion. When it goes onto the string in
Movement C you'll finally hear the wobble; for now, the only feedback is how *loose* it feels.
A pulse that feels effortful is wrong no matter how it looks.

## The usual mistake

Making it big and driven — pumping from the shoulder or the elbow with force. The pulse is
small and it *rebounds* rather than being pushed each way; the muscles mostly let go and let it
bounce back. The other mistake is gripping the neck with the thumb "for stability" — a gripped
thumb kills the pulse instantly. Thumb light, or off the neck entirely while you practise the
motion.
