The finish line. Mozart wrote his **Violin Concerto No. 3 in G major, K.216** in Salzburg in
1775, when he was nineteen — one of five violin concertos he finished that year. It runs about
twenty-five minutes, and by the time you can read and play it, the distance you've come from
your first open string *is* this whole course.

This lesson is the front door of the K.216 module. The pages themselves come one section at a
time, each rebuilt as our annotated line — every note named, with its string and finger, a
how-to-play note per line, and playback.

## The three movements

- **I. Allegro** — G major, sonata form. The orchestra lays out the whole main theme first;
  then you enter and take it over, and the movement is a conversation between you and them.
- **II. Adagio** — D major. A song without words, muted strings, the solo line floating over
  a soft cushion. The heart of the piece.
- **III. Rondeau: Allegro** — G major. A dancing refrain that keeps returning, with a slower
  musette episode in the middle — a folk tune Mozart borrowed, which is why the whole concerto
  got the nickname the **"Strassburg" concerto**.

## What "Tutti" and "Solo" mean on the page

A concerto is a soloist *and* an orchestra. In the first movement the orchestra plays the
entire main theme on its own — about forty bars — before you play a note. Your part still
prints those bars, small, so you can follow along and count; where you actually play, the
notes are full size and marked **Solo**.

This module shows you both:

- **Your part** — full annotation, the thing you practise.
- **Around you** — the orchestra's line for that stretch, as a listening reference, so you
  know what you're resting through and what you're coming in against.

## Where this sits

K.216 is upper-intermediate to early-advanced for the hands: first position through about
fifth, clean string crossings, trills, and a cadenza where you're briefly alone. Musically
it's harder than it is technically — Classical phrasing is all taste and restraint, nothing
to hide behind. The milestone this course aims at is: **you can read the whole score and play
it slowly and honestly.** Performing it is a further climb, and it's yours to make.
