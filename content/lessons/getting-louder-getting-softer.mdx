A single letter sets a level. To change the level *while you play*, the page gives you two
tools: a **word** — *cresc.*, *dim.* — or a **shape**, the hairpin.

## The word and the shape

- **cresc.** (*crescendo*, "growing") — get louder. Often trailed by a dashed line:
  *cresc. \_ \_ \_ \_* — showing how far the growth runs.
- **dim.** (*diminuendo*) or **decresc.** (*decrescendo*) — get softer, the same way.
- The **hairpin**: two lines that **open** `<` toward the loud end and **close** `>` toward the
  soft end. It sits under the staff, stretched across exactly the notes it applies to.

The hairpin carries more information than the word: **its length** tells you over how many
notes to spread the change, and **where it ends** tells you where you're aiming — the loudest
point of a `<`, the quietest of a `>`.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play the four-note rise D–E–F♯–G on the D string. Give each note a hair more bow speed than
  the one before — same weight, just faster travel. Four small increases add up to a
  crescendo you *built*, evenly, rather than a loudness you flipped on at the top.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Saving the whole change for the last note — three quiet notes and then a loud one is not a
crescendo, it's a step. Spread it. Second: ignoring where the hairpin **ends** — that point is
the target, and if you peak early you've nowhere to go. Third: reading a long closing hairpin
`>` as an **accent**. An accent `>` sits on one note; a diminuendo hairpin stretches across
several. Check how wide it is.

</Cite>
