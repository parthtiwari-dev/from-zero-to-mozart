Back in Stage 1 you learned the staff has a few **landmark notes** you know on sight, and that
you read *from* them rather than counting up from the bottom line every time. That idea is the
foundation of fluent reading, and now it becomes the *only* way you read.

## The anchors

On the treble staff, a handful of notes should be as instant as your own name:

- **The lines, bottom to top: E – G – B – D – F.** Of these, **G (line 2)**, **B (the middle
  line)** and **D (line 4)** are the strongest anchors — evenly spaced, easy to spot.
- **The open strings, written**: G below the staff, D in the bottom space, A in the second
  space, E in the top space (Stage 1, `open-strings-on-the-staff`). These double as landmarks
  *and* as "that's an open string, no finger."
- **Middle C**, one ledger line below — the edge of your low range.

Everything else you read as *"a step / a skip / a leap from the nearest anchor."* A note
sitting just above the middle line? That's a step above B — it's C. A note on the next line up
from B? A skip — B to D. You find the anchor, then move.

## Mnemonics were a crutch — let them go

"*Every Good Boy Deserves Fudge*" and "FACE" got you started. But a reader who recites a
mnemonic for every note is far too slow, and worse, they have no idea how the line *sounds*
until they've decoded it all. Landmark-plus-interval reading is faster and it keeps the music
audible as you go. If you still catch yourself reciting, that's the sign to drill
`reading-notes-fast` until the anchors are automatic.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Open any first-position tune. Cover everything but the first note of each bar. For each one,
  name it by *anchor* — "that's on the middle line, it's B" — not by counting. Then uncover and
  read the rest of each bar as steps and skips *from* that first note. This is reading.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Having *too many* anchors, or the wrong ones. You need about three rock-solid ones (G, B, D
lines) plus the open strings — not all nine line-and-space notes memorised as separate facts.
Three anchors and the ability to step/skip from them covers the whole staff.
