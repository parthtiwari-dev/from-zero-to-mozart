The ear is built the way sight-reading is built — **a little, every day, for a long time.**
This is the daily routine that ties Movements D through H together.

## The ten-minute menu

At the start of a practice session, before your ear tires:

| Minutes | What |
|---|---|
| 2 | **Sing & match.** Drone on. Sing up and down the scale in numbers; match single pitches to the string until they lock. |
| 3 | **Scale-degree gym.** Your current wave (`the-scale-degree-drills`) — random degrees, then pairs, then short fragments. Name them. |
| 2 | **Harmony.** A few chords: major or minor? home, leaving, or pulling? (`major-or-minor`, `home-leaving-pulling`). |
| 2 | **Dictation.** One short rhythm and one short melody, taken down and checked (`the-dictation-drills`). |
| — | **A new tune by ear** every few days (`learning-a-tune-by-ear`) — not daily, but a standing project. |

Ten minutes. Every degree named, every chord judged, every phrase sung. That's the whole
thing.

## Two rules that matter more than the menu

- **Distributed, not massed.** Ten minutes daily beats seventy minutes on Sunday, by a wide
  margin — the ear consolidates what it learned *overnight*. Same rule as scales
  (`how-to-practise-a-scale`) and sight-reading (`the-sight-reading-habit`).
- **Always sing.** Every drill has a sing step. If you're only listening and naming, you're
  doing half the exercise — the voice is how the ear checks itself
  (`singing-is-not-optional`).

## The tools

- **The drone** in the practice bar — running under every drill.
- **The drill pages** — `the-scale-degree-drills`, `the-dictation-drills`, and the harmony
  drills inside the Movement F lessons.
- **A metronome** for the rhythm work.
- **Recordings** — for the "learn a tune by ear" project, any song you like. Later, an ear
  trainer app (Functional Ear Trainer, Teoria, Toned Ear) for extra reps between sessions.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Do the full ten-minute menu tomorrow morning, first thing in your practice. Set a timer for
  each block so you don't overrun one and skip another. Do it every day for two weeks, then
  judge: which block is easy now, which is still hard? Spend an extra minute on the hard one.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Long, occasional ear-training binges — an hour every week or two — which build almost
nothing, because the gains come from *frequency*, not session length. The other mistake:
dropping the singing to "save time." That's not a shorter workout; it's a different, weaker
one.
