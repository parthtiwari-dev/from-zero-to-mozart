This is the lesson the whole stage — and a good part of the whole course — has been building
toward. Your stated goal, in your own words: *"if I hear something, I should be able to play
it on my own."* Here is the method.

## The six steps

1. **Listen to the whole thing — violin down.** Several times. Don't touch the instrument.
   You're building an *audiation* of the tune (`hearing-it-first`) — hear it clearly in your
   head, all the way through, before you play a note.
2. **Rhythm and phrases.** Tap the pulse, find the metre, hear where the phrases break. Get
   the *shape of time* first (`taking-a-rhythm-down`).
3. **Find home.** Hum the tune; let it end; the note it wants to rest on is ^1. Set a drone
   there (`finding-home`).
4. **Degree by degree.** For each note: **sing it** → **name the degree** (against the drone)
   → **put the finger where that degree lives** (`degrees-on-the-fingerboard`) → **play it.**
5. **Guess, hear the error, correct.** When a note's wrong, your ear tells you *which way* —
   too high, too low. Nudge and re-check (the loop from Stage 5's `finding-a-tune-by-ear`).
   Every wrong guess teaches your hand the fingerboard.
6. **Chunk and join.** Work one phrase at a time. Get it solid, then join it to the next.
   Don't grind the whole tune end to end.

## The graded ladder

| Level | Target |
|---|---|
| 1 | a nursery tune you **know cold** — Twinkle, Mary Had a Little Lamb. You're practising the *method*, not straining the ear. |
| 2 | a folk tune you **don't** know well — get it from a recording, by ear only. |
| 3 | the **first phrase** of a pop or film hook you like (`REPERTOIRE.md`'s viral-melody list). |
| 4 | a **whole short tune**, played along with the track (`play-along`). |

Targets at `the-tunes-to-find-by-ear`.

## The usual mistake

Grabbing the violin immediately and hunting note by note with no plan — no audiation, no
home, no degrees. It sometimes works for three notes and then collapses. Do the listening and
the singing first; the violin is the *last* step, not the first. The other mistake: refusing
to sing because it feels awkward. Singing is the step that makes the rest possible.
