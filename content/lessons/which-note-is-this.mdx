The musical alphabet only has seven letters, so every letter turns up more than once on the
staff — a lower one and a higher one, an octave apart. "B" isn't an instruction until you know
*which* B.

## The usual mistake

Seeing "B" and playing the first B your hand knows, ignoring the octave the staff is showing.
The letter tells you the note's *name*; the height tells you which one.

## About "C4" and "C5"

Later you'll see notes written as "C4" or "C5" — just a precise way of saying *which* C (C4 is
middle C). For now, "the lower one" and "the higher one", read from the staff, is enough.
