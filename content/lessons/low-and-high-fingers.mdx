The violin has no frets, so a finger isn't tied to one spot. It can land a little lower or a
little higher — a **half step** apart — and that's two different notes from the same finger.

The one you meet first and most is the **second finger**, which has a **low** position and a
**high** position roughly a centimetre apart. On the D string:

Same idea for the first finger and the third finger on some strings. You don't need to memorise
every case tonight — you learn the low/high placements one key at a time, as the music you're
playing needs them. For now, just hold the concept: **one finger, two possible notes, a
half step apart.** When a lesson says "low 2" or "high 2", that's what it means.
