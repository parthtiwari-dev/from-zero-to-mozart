Stage 3 taught you to *read* two notes stacked together. Stage 8's `reading-two-notes-at-once`
had you *play* the easy cases. Here's the whole playing ladder, and the one genuinely new step
at the top of it.

## The ladder you're already most of the way up

1. **Two open strings** — no left hand involved at all. Purely a bow-control exercise: even
   weight on both strings, a clean simultaneous attack.
2. **Open + one stopped note**, on the adjacent string — the left hand only has one job; the
   open string takes care of itself.
3. **Both notes stopped** — the genuinely new skill. Two fingers down at once, on adjacent
   strings, tuned **to each other**, not each in isolation against an open string.

You've been doing steps 1 and 2 since Stage 3 and Stage 8. This lesson, and the three after it,
are step 3.

Most real double stops — thirds, sixths, octaves, all coming next — use **two different
fingers**, one per string, which is harder: the hand has to hold both in tune *relative to each
other*, with no open string to lean on for either one.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play the 5th above, holding it for a slow four counts, listening for beating. Then lift and
  replace the finger a few times, checking the interval stays clean each time — that repeatable
  accuracy, not the first lucky attempt, is what the next three lessons build on.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Checking each note of a double stop against an open string separately, then assuming the pair
is in tune. It isn't the same test — two notes can each be individually correct and still clash
against each other. Tune the *interval*, by listening to the two notes together, every time.
