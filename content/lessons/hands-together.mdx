Your two hands do completely different jobs, and they run on **separate tracks**. The bow arm
draws a steady, even stroke; the left hand changes notes underneath it. The bow doesn't know
the fingers moved — and it mustn't.

## The problem

The beginner's bow **hitches** — it slows or stops or jerks every time a finger drops. Or the
opposite: the fingers wait for the bow. Either way the two hands are fighting, and the sound
stutters.

## The fix: apart, then together

Every method teaches this. Practise the two hands **separately** first, then combine them
**slowly**:

- **Air-bow** the line — the right arm alone, drawing even strokes in the air or on an open
  string, no fingers. Feel the bow speed stay constant.
- **Finger** the line **silently** — the left hand alone, dropping and lifting the notes, no
  bow.
- **Together**, slow — and the one rule is: *the bow's speed does not change at a finger
  drop.*

## Now one bow per note

The other coordination: the bow changes direction *exactly* when the finger drops — not
before, not after. Down on the first note, up on the second, and so on.

## The usual mistake

The bow **braking** at every finger change — a little dip in the sound on each new note. Or a
gap, because the finger lands after the bow has already moved past. Slow right down until the
bow speed is genuinely unbroken, then inch the tempo up.
