The four notes you just learned — C, G, D, A — aren't random. They're notes you already know
by ear: the tonic and dominant of the G-major scale (Stage 4's `the-twelve-keys` territory),
just reached from a new place on the neck. This lesson is the bridge from "I can find 3rd
position" to "I can use it in something musical."

## The usual mistake

Treating 3rd position as a drill that only exists in exercises. The moment you can reliably
land on these four notes, you can already use them musically — waiting for "real repertoire"
before playing anything in a new position just delays the ear-and-hand trust that makes the
position actually useful later.

<Cite source="This course's own verified G-major material (Stage 4); Suzuki-tradition practice of using known material to consolidate a new position">
  Method books consistently introduce a new position through material the student already
  knows by ear, precisely so the *position* is the only new variable — everything else about
  the music stays familiar.
</Cite>
