When a *D.C.* or *D.S.* sends you back through a section that has its **own** repeat signs, do
you take those repeats again? Almost always: **no.** And almost never is that written down
anywhere — it's a convention you're simply expected to know.

## The minuet is the classic case

A Classical minuet movement is really three things:

| Section | How you play it |
|---|---|
| **Minuet** — `‖: a :‖ ‖: b :‖` | with both repeats |
| **Trio** — `‖: c :‖ ‖: d :‖` | with both repeats |
| **Minuet da capo** | straight through — **a then b, no repeats** |

The whole movement is a big **A – B – A**: Minuet, Trio, Minuet. The third part is a return,
and a return doesn't need to linger.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take a minuet you can play — even eight bars of one. Play the first strain **with** its

  through**. Notice how the return feels like "we're wrapping up," not "here it is again."

## The usual mistake

Taking every repeat on the da capo — the movement swells to twice its intended length and
sags. The over-correction is dropping the repeats the **first** time too. The rule is simple:
**first time, take them; on the da capo, don't** — unless the score actually prints *con
replica*.

  dropped and *con replica* when they wanted them kept — proof that the default was already

</Cite>
