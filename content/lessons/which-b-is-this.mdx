Here's a problem you'll hit the moment you leave the open strings. Someone says "play a B."
Which one? The violin has several. In first position alone there's a B down near the G string,
a B in the middle of your range, and a B up high on the E string — three different notes, three
different fingers, all called B.

A letter on its own can't tell you which. **The staff can** — by how high the notehead sits.

The low one lives just under the staff, the middle one sits right on the centre line, and the
high one floats above the top. Once you can see *which* B a notehead is asking for, you know
which string and finger to reach for. Same trick works for every C, every D, every note that
repeats.

<TryThis>
  Play the three B's from the page, low to high, then high to low. Don't think about the octave
  number — just the shape and the height. Where does each one land under your hand?
</TryThis>

> You don't need to name these "B3, B4, B5" yet. That's a labelling system we'll add later,
> for when words have to do a job the staff normally does for free. For now: learn the height.
