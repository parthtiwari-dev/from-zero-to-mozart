On the violin, a duration is a **distance and a speed of bow**. Reading a note's length is
only half the job — the other half is planning how much bow it gets, so you don't run out
halfway through a long note or scrub a short one.

Rough guide, on a full-length bow:

| Note | Bow |
|---|---|
| whole note | the whole bow, frog to tip — slow and even |
| half note | about half the bow |
| quarter note | a modest length, around the middle |
| eighth notes | short and quicker, upper half |

The bow is a **ruler for time**: match its travel to the rhythm and everything fits.

## The usual mistake

Using the same amount of bow for every value. Then the long notes die early — the bow ran
out — and the short notes are scratchy, because there's too much bow moving too fast for such
a small note. Match the bow to the length.
