Vibrato sits on top of two things it must not disturb: the **pitch** underneath it, and the
**bow** drawing the sound. When vibrato is new, it tends to shove both around. This lesson is
how to keep them still.

## The pitch: the centre must not move

The wobble has an average, and your ear hears that average as *the note*. If the average
drifts — usually sharp, because the "reach up" habit sneaks back in — the note goes out of tune
even though every individual instant of the wobble passed through the right pitch. Check it
against a drone:

Turn on the drone in the tuner bar, set it to D, and vibrate this note against it. Listen only
to the *centre* of your wobble — is it sitting on the drone an octave up, or has it crept
above?

## The bow: it stays independent

If the vibrato pulses into your bow arm, the tone scratches or throbs in time with the shimmer.
The two hands have to be independent, and that's built by practising them apart:

- **Bow only:** a long, dead-even bow, left hand still. Perfect the tone.
- **Vibrato only:** the left hand shimmering, no bow (or the lightest possible bow). Perfect the
  motion.
- **Together, slowly:** add them, watching that the bow stays as even as it was alone.

<TryThis>
  One long note against the drone: 10 seconds bow-only (even tone), 10 seconds vibrato-only
  (loose motion), then 20 seconds both — with the single job of keeping the bow exactly as
  smooth as it was on its own. If the tone starts throbbing, drop the vibrato, reset the bow,
  add it back.
</TryThis>

## Hear it

You'll hear the *failure* easily — a beginner recording where the tone pulses with the vibrato.
The success is silent: in a good recording the bow is glassy-smooth and the vibrato rides on
top of it without a ripple.

## The usual mistake

Letting the vibrato pull the pitch sharp (the reaching-up habit — the pitch is a ceiling), and
letting the wobble leak into the bow arm. Both come from the same root: the vibrato isn't yet
*isolated* to the left hand. Slow, separated practice fixes both.
