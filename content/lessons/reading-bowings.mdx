Putting the reading skills together: a short line with a **bowing** written into it — bow
directions, a slur, and articulation. Work out the plan before you play. Which notes share a
bow? Where does each bow start and stop?

**The plan:** D and E are slurred on **one down-bow** — two notes, one stroke, so use about half
the bow and don't stop between them. Then F and G are separate **up-bows** with staccato dots —
short and stopped, so save bow, use small strokes near where the previous stroke ended. Bar two
is two long half notes, down then up, using more bow each.

Reading a bowing is its own small skill: before playing a phrase, your eye should group the
notes into bow-strokes the same way you already group them into rhythms and pitches. Get in the
habit now — it's exactly what you'll do with the first line of Vivaldi and Mozart.
