You learned this tune in Stage 1 as eight even phrases. Here it is again, the same notes —
now with a **dynamic scheme** on top. Beethoven's theme starts almost inaudibly and grows,
statement by statement, into the whole orchestra and choir. Our version does that in
miniature: `p` → `mp` → `mf` → `f`, then softens and slows for home.

In A major, first position, all on the A string.

## The one thing this tune is for

**Reading and playing a dynamic scheme** — treating `p` … `mf` … `f` as one connected shape
across a whole tune, not as four separate settings. The notes never change; the arc is
everything.

## The usual mistake

Playing each dynamic as a flat step and jumping between them — the scheme should feel like one
long swell and release. And spending the whole crescendo early, so phrase 4 has nowhere to go:
keep phrases 1–3 held back, and let the top of the last rise be the only real forte.

<Cite source="Beethoven, Symphony No. 9 (1824), 4th movement — 'Ode an die Freude', public domain">
  In the symphony the theme is first given out by cellos and basses alone, very quiet, then
  built through repetition until the full forces have it. Our small arc is that same idea:
  a melody that grows.
</Cite>
