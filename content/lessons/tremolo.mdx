Slashes drawn **through a note's stem** mean: repeat this note, fast. The number of slashes
tells you how fast — and it's the fourth thing that can happen near a stem, after a beam, a
stack of noteheads, and a vibrato wave (Stage 2, *two in a row, two at once, or a wobble*).

## Reading the slashes

| Slashes | Play the note as | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | eighth notes | measured |
| **2** | sixteenth notes | measured |
| **3** (or the word **trem.**) | **as fast as possible** | unmeasured — a texture, not a rhythm |

On a whole note, which has no stem, the slashes float where the stem would be.

## The four things near a stem

| You see | It means |
|---|---|
| a **beam** joining noteheads | different notes, played in sequence (Stage 2) |
| **noteheads stacked** on one stem | different notes, played **together** — a double stop (Stage 2) |
| a **wavy line** beside a note | vibrato — a pitch shimmer, no rhythm (Stage 2) |
| **slashes through the stem** | tremolo — one note repeated fast (this lesson) |

## Try it

<TryThis>
  On the open A, play a half note's worth of very fast, very small bows near the tip — no
  accents, no rhythm you could count, just a haze of sound. That's a bowed tremolo. Now try
  the fingered kind: bow one steady note on the A string and rock your 1st and 2nd fingers
  quickly between B and C.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Counting a three-slash tremolo — it's **unmeasured**, a shimmer, not a rhythm. Accenting the
repeats, so it sounds like a machine gun instead of a haze — keep it dead even. And confusing
the two kinds: slashes **through one stem** repeat a single note; slashes **between two
noteheads** alternate two.
