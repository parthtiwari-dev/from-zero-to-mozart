Sometimes the part gives you a full bowing. Often it gives you almost nothing — just notes,
slurs and dynamics — and **you** decide the rest: directions, where in the bow, retakes. This
lesson is the method for doing that, and it ends where the course has been pointing all along.

## The checklist

1. **Find the strong beats.** They want down-bows (`the-rule-of-the-down-bow`).
2. **Count the notes** between the strong beats. Work out which direction each group has to
   start so the strong beat lands **⊓**.
3. **Fix the clashes.** Where the count would put a strong beat on an up-bow, use a **retake**,
   a **hooked bowing**, or **choose a slur** that re-groups the notes.
4. **Plan the distribution** (`planning-the-bow`). Will you run out of bow? Where does each
   phrase start — frog, middle? Where do you retake to reset before the next one?
5. **Mark it in, play it slow, adjust.**

## Where this has been going — the first phrase of Mozart

The Stage 4 lesson `the-first-note-of-mozart` decoded *what* the K.216 solo opening is: a
**rolled G-major chord** landing on a high G, then the leading tone F♯ leaning back to G. Now
you can decode **how it's bowed.**

That's the whole arc: Stage 4 taught you to **read** the first note of Mozart; Stage 5 taught
you to **bow** it. "I can't even play the first note" is no longer true.

## The usual mistake

Bowing a passage note-by-note as you sight-read it, so you're constantly running out of bow or
landing strong beats on weak up-bows and having to scramble. Plan the phrase as a whole,
backwards from its end, *before* you play. The other mistake: assuming there's one "correct"
bowing — often several work, and the choice is a musical one.

<Cite source="the studio tradition of 'working out the bowing'; Mozart, Violin Concerto No. 3 in G, K.216 — the solo entrance">
  Every professional part a violinist plays has been bowed — by the player, a section leader,
  or an editor — before the first rehearsal. Learning to do it yourself is the point where you
  stop just executing a part and start interpreting one.
</Cite>
