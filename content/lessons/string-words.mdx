A handful of Italian words are specific to string instruments — you won't see these in a piano
part.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| arco | use the bow (returning after a pizzicato passage) |
| pizz. | pluck the string, no bow |
| con sordino | mute on |
| senza sordino | mute off |
| sul G, sul D, sul A, sul E | play this passage on the named string, even if it's reachable elsewhere |
| sul ponticello | bow very near the bridge — a glassy, edgy sound |
| sul tasto | bow over the fingerboard — soft and flute-like |

You met **pizz.** and **arco** briefly when we talked about bow strokes; here they are as the
words you'll actually see printed in a score. "Sul G" is a favourite composer's trick for tone
colour — the same pitch sounds noticeably different, warmer and darker, played up high on a
lower string than in its normal spot.
