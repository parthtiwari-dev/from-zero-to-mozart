**Détaché** (French for "detached") is the default stroke: **one separate bow per note**,
smooth, generally using the full written value of the note, with a clean change of direction
between notes. When a passage has no other marking, this is what you play.

**Notation:** none needed — the *absence* of any articulation mark is what tells you it's
détaché.

**How to practise:** start on an open string, four repetitions of a simple rhythm, listening
for a consistent attack and an even sound on every stroke regardless of direction. Then apply it
to a scale. Stop the moment your shoulder or bow hand stiffens — a tense détaché just teaches
tension.

**Don't confuse it with:** *staccato* (which stops the sound between notes) or *legato* (which
connects several notes in one bow). Détaché is separate bows but continuous sound.
