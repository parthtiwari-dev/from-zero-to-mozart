**Pizzicato** — "plucked" — is the one time in this stage the bow isn't the point. You met the
word `pizz.` in Stage 3 and learned to read it; here's how to actually do it, because a clean
pluck is its own small skill.

## The basic pluck

- **Anchor the thumb** on the corner of the fingerboard (the side nearest you), so your hand
  has a base.
- **Pluck with the side or tip of the index finger** — pull the string *across*, toward the
  next string, not straight up. Straight up gives a thin snap; across gives a round, ringing
  note.
- Pluck a little **over the end of the fingerboard**, not up near the bridge — you get more
  tone there.

## The single stray pizzicato

If a bowed passage has just **one** pizz note in the middle of it, you don't have time to
re-anchor the hand. Keep the bow in your hand and pluck with the **thumb** instead, or with
the index finger while the bow stays held. Composers who know the instrument leave you a beat
to make the switch; ones who don't, you fake.

## `pizz.` and `arco` — and the time it costs

`pizz.` means start plucking; **`arco`** means go back to the bow. Switching between them takes
a real half-second — getting the bow settled back on the string, or getting the hand into
plucking position. Look ahead for the next `arco` so you're not caught out.

## Left-hand pizzicato

Written with a **+** above the note: you pluck with a **left-hand** finger (usually while the
bow plays something else, or between bowed notes). It's a virtuoso trick — Paganini built
whole variations on it. **Recognise the + ; don't practise it now.**

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Pluck a one-octave G major scale, up and down, thumb anchored, index finger pulling across,
  each note ringing fully. Then play a tune you know entirely pizzicato — *Ode to Joy* works.
  Then bow the same tune. Feel how different the two right-hand jobs are.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Plucking **straight up** and near the bridge — a thin, buzzy, quiet note with no ring. Pull
the string *sideways*, over the fingerboard, and let it settle back onto its neighbour. The
other mistake: a stiff, clawed hand — the pluck is a relaxed flick of one finger, thumb
resting, everything else loose.
