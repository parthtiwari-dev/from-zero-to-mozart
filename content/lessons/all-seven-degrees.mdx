Wave 3. Now the stable three and the restless four go into **one bag** and come out in random
order. A note sounds over the drone; you name it, 1 to 7, straight away. This is the drill
that, once it's solid, means you can hear a melody as a row of numbers — and that's playing
by ear.

## Naming any degree

You already have the tools:

- **Stable or restless?** If it just sits, it's ^1, ^3 or ^5. If it pulls, it's ^2, ^4, ^6
  or ^7.
- **Stable — which?** ^1 = fully home. ^5 = bright, standing. ^3 = soft, coloured.
- **Restless — which?** By the pull: ^7 aches up a half step to ^1; ^4 sags a half step to
  ^3; ^2 leans a whole step down to ^1; ^6 falls a whole step to ^5.

With a drone under it, every degree has a fixed, learnable character. The work is just reps
until the name arrives without the reasoning.

## The sargam bridge — carefully

Your sargam ear does this exact job. Worth being precise about how the two systems line up:

- The **seven shuddha (natural) swaras** — Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni — have the **same
  step-pattern as the Western major scale**. That pitch-set, in Hindustani theory, is
  **Bilawal thaat**.
- So **^1↔Sa, ^2↔Re, ^3↔Ga, ^4↔Ma, ^5↔Pa, ^6↔Dha, ^7↔Ni**, and the *functions* carry
  straight across: Pa is bright and standing just as ^5 is; Ni pulls up to Sa just as ^7
  pulls to ^1.
- What does **not** transfer: "sargam = the major scale." Sargam is a **solmisation system**
  for singing any thaat (Kafi, Bhairav, Todi… each with its own komal/tivra swaras), and a
  **raga** is not a scale at all — it's a grammar (which phrases, which emphasis, ascent vs
  descent, mood). Bilawal thaat happens to share Western major's *notes*; that's the bridge,
  and it stops there.

| ^ | Solfège | Swara (shuddha) |
|---|---|---|
| ^1 | do | **Sa** |
| ^2 | re | Re |
| ^3 | mi | Ga |
| ^4 | fa | Ma |
| ^5 | sol | **Pa** |
| ^6 | la | Dha |
| ^7 | ti | Ni |

Use whichever names come fastest. The ear skill is identical either way.

## The usual mistake

Practising only in scale order (^1 ^2 ^3 ^4…), which lets you count instead of hear — you're
just tracking "the next one up." Real melodies jump around, so drill in **random order** from
the start of Wave 3. The other mistake: no drone. Without home sounding, the degrees have no
anchor and you're back to guessing intervals.
