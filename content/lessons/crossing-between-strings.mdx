Tunes don't stay on one string. Moving the bow from one string to the next — a **string
crossing** — is a whole-arm move, and it should barely disturb the sound.

## It's the arm, not the wrist

To reach a different string, the **whole arm changes level** — the elbow leads, hinging from
the shoulder, like Stage 0's "stopping a lift on a different floor." The **left-hand frame
keeps its shape**; the left elbow just swings a little further under for the lower strings.

Arm and bow stay in **one flat plane** — imagine a pane of glass under your whole arm, tilting
as a unit. The arm is **high for the G string**, dropping for D, then A, then E.

## Find the level first

The trick that makes it clean: before you cross, know where the new level *is*.

- Play the two strings **together**, as a double stop — feel the elbow settle at the balanced
  height *between* them.
- Now play the **crossing** — one string, then the other — **without changing that elbow
  height and without changing the bow speed.** The bow just tips across.
- Where you can, **put the next finger down early**, while the bow is still on the old string.

## The usual mistake

Twisting the **wrist** or reshaping the hand to reach a string, instead of moving the arm. And
**catching both strings** at the crossing — a scrape — because the arm over- or under-shoots
the new level.
