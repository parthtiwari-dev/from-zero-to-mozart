Of the three controls, one carries more of the load than the others, and getting this the
wrong way round is the single most common reason a beginner's tone sounds harsh. The principle:
**draw the sound out with bow speed, not down with pressure.**

## Why pressing works against you

The string's job is to swing **sideways**. Pressing the bow **down** into the string fights
that swing — squash it hard enough and the string can barely move, so you get a loud *effort*
and a small, rough *sound*. Fischer's picture: pressing is like trying to drive the sound down
toward your feet. Using speed instead feels like **lifting the sound out** of the string.

This holds even when you want a big, powerful tone. A strong note near the bridge is still
made with the bow **travelling** — more bow, moving deep in the string — not with a downward
shove.

## The two sounds that mean you've gone wrong

Your bow tells you which way it's out of balance:

- **A whistle, or a glassy, skating sound** — too *little* weight, or too *fast*, or too far
  from the bridge. The hair is sliding over the string without catching it. Fix: a hair more
  weight, or slow down, or move toward the bridge.
- **A crunch, a scrape, a torn sound** — too *much* weight, or too *slow*, or too near the
  bridge. The string can't complete its swing. Fix: less weight, or a faster bow, or move
  toward the fingerboard.

## The one test that matters — listen for the ring

Play a note, then add the tiniest bit of weight. Did the string ring *wider* and fuller? Good
— keep going. Did it choke or dull? Back off a fraction. Home in on the balance where the
string sings the widest. That's the whole method, and it's just listening.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Open D, medium volume, medium sounding point. Play a long note and very slowly add weight
  until it just starts to crunch — then back off until it clears. That's your maximum for that
  bow speed. Now speed the bow up and find the new maximum — it's higher. Slow the bow down —
  it's lower. The right weight always depends on the speed.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Reaching for weight whenever you want more sound. It feels like it should work and it doesn't —
you get a pressed, choked tone that tires your arm. Reach for **speed** first (more bow), and
add only as much weight as the speed needs.

<Cite source="Simon Fischer, Basics (1997), 'Tone production'">
  "Speed of bow is always the first consideration in drawing sound and expression from the
  violin." Even in the heaviest playing, Fischer writes, you draw the tone out with horizontal
  movement of the bow, not with downward pressure.
</Cite>
