The staff only has room for so many notes. When a pitch goes above or below it, we draw short
extra lines just for that note — **ledger lines**.

Violinists meet the ones *below* the staff straight away, because the G string lives down
there. The open G string is two ledger lines below the staff; the note you'll call "middle C"
(third finger on the G string) sits on the **first ledger line below**.

Don't let ledger lines scare you — they follow the exact same alphabet rule, just extended.
Count up or down from a landmark: the bottom line is E, so the space just below it is D, the
first ledger line below is C, and so on. Above the staff you'll see them too, once you're
reading E-string notes.
