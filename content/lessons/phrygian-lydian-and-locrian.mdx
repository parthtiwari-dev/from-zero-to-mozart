The three remaining modes. You'll meet them less often on the violin, but each has a
strong, instantly recognisable colour.

## Phrygian — minor with a flat 2nd

Natural minor, but the **2nd degree is a half step lower.** That ♭2 sits right against the
tonic and gives Phrygian its dark, tense, unmistakably **Spanish** flavour — flamenco is
built on it.

## Lydian — major with a sharp 4th

Major, but the **4th degree is a half step higher.** That ♯4 makes the scale sound like it's
lifting off the ground — bright, wondrous, unresolved-in-a-good-way. Film composers reach for
it constantly (the leap at the start of *The Simpsons* theme is a Lydian ♯4).

## Locrian — the one with no home

Minor with **both a ♭2 and a ♭5.** The flattened 5th means the tonic chord is *diminished* —
there's no stable home to rest on. Locrian is a curiosity; you'll almost never meet a whole
tune in it.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play a C major scale on the D and A strings. Now play the same seven notes starting from F
  (F G A B c d e f) — that's **Lydian**, and the B natural is the ♯4. Then start from E (E F G
  A B c d e) — that's **Phrygian**, and the F right by the E is the ♭2. Same notes, two
  completely different characters, just by choosing a different home.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Fixing the "wrong-sounding" note by ear. In Phrygian your hand will want to raise the ♭2; in
Lydian it will want to lower the ♯4. Those notes are the *point* of the mode — leave them
where they are and let the mode do its work.
