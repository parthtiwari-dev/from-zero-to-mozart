Three small signs change a note by a **half step** — the smallest move in this system. On the
violin that's almost always the *same finger*, slid a little.

| Sign | Name | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| ♯ | sharp | raises the note a half step |
| ♭ | flat | lowers the note a half step |
| ♮ | natural | cancels a sharp or flat — back to the plain note |

The sign sits **just before** the note it changes.

## The finger slides — it doesn't change

The direction never changes: **sharp = forward = higher, flat = back = lower.** And a half
step is the two fingers almost touching — back to the finger patterns.

## The usual mistake

Thinking a sharp or flat means a *different finger*. It's the same finger, moved. The 2nd
finger plays F and F♯; the 2nd finger plays B and B♭.

<TryThis>
  Drone on D. Play F♮ (low 2) against it, then F♯ (high 2), then F♮. One sits in D major, one
  fights it — and it's the *same finger* both times, just shifted a few millimetres.
</TryThis>
