Three small symbols change a note by a **half step** — the smallest step in this system, and on
the violin, usually a small slide of one finger.

| Sign | Name | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| ♯ | sharp | raises the note a half step |
| ♭ | flat | lowers the note a half step |
| ♮ | natural | cancels a sharp or flat — back to the plain note |

On the page the sign sits **just before** the note it changes. On the violin it usually means
**the same finger, moved**: F is a low 2, F sharp is a high 2. C is a low 2 on the A string,
C sharp is a high 2. The direction is always the same — sharp moves the finger toward the
bridge (higher), flat moves it toward the scroll (lower).

Which sharps and flats you'll actually meet depends on the **key** — that's the next page.
