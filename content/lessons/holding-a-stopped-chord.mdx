Stage 5's `rolling-a-chord-with-the-bow` solved the **bow** side of a 3-/4-note chord: roll
bottom to top, land and hold the top note, because the bridge's curve makes true simultaneity
impossible. This lesson is the **left hand's** job — holding the shape that makes such a chord
reachable at all.

## The same rule as reading a chord, now applied to playing one

`RULES.md`'s chord rule already governs every notated chord in this course: a playable chord
needs its notes spread across **3 or 4 different strings**. Now it matters even more, because
you're not just reading it — you're **holding** it, which means at least one, sometimes two, of
those notes has to be **fingered**, not open, while the bow rolls through all of them.

## Where 3rd position helps

Some chord voicings are cramped or flatly impossible to hold in 1st position but open up once
you shift the whole shape into 3rd position — the hand-frame you already know, just relocated.
If a chord feels like it's fighting your hand, try it a position higher before assuming it
can't be played as written.

## A real teacher's eye catches what this can't

This is the first genuinely new **hand shape** this stage asks of you — holding two fingers
down across two different strings while a third string rings open, all at once. A teacher
watching your hand can spot tension, a collapsed knuckle, or a finger that's flattening the
wrong string in a way no self-check here can. If a chord like this feels physically wrong, that
instinct is worth listening to.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Set the two fingers down first, silently, and check by eye that both are cleanly on their own
  string. Then bow the roll slowly, one string at a time, holding the last note. Only once that
  feels stable should you try rolling it at a real tempo.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Rushing the roll before the hand shape is secure. A chord that's rolled fast but lands on a
half-formed hand shape will sound smeared no matter how good the bow arm is — the left hand has
to be ready *before* the bow starts moving, every time.
