Written music sits on a **staff**: five lines with four spaces between them. A note's job is
split in two — its **shape** tells you how long it lasts (that's Stage 2), and its **height**
on the staff tells you the pitch.

Higher on the staff means higher in pitch. Every step up — from a line to the next space, or a
space to the next line — moves one letter along the musical alphabet: **A B C D E F G**, then
it starts over.

The four **spaces**, bottom to top, spell **F, A, C, E**.

You'll learn to recognise these positions by sight, the way you recognise letters without
sounding them out. Mnemonics ("Every Good Boy…", "FACE") get you started, but the fastest
readers lean on a few fixed **landmarks** and count intervals from them — and your best
landmarks are the open strings, because they connect straight to the instrument. That's the
next few pages.
