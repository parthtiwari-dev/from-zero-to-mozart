The staff is five lines and four spaces. A note is a dot on a line or in a space, and its
**height is its pitch** — higher on the staff, higher in sound. That's the whole foundation.
Reading is just working out *which* line or space.

## The shape comes first

When the dots climb, the tune goes up; when they fall, it goes down. Before you name a single
note you can see the *shape* of a melody — and the shape carries most of the music.

## Position is pitch; shape is length

A note also has a *shape* — filled or open, with a stem or a flag — and that tells you how
**long** it lasts. That's Stage 2. For now, one job: **position on the staff = pitch.**

## The usual mistake

Reading a stack of notes as separate puzzles instead of one moving line. Your eye should follow
a melody like a path, not stop dead at every note.
