Two different kinds of numbers show up near notes, and mixing them up causes real confusion
later.

## Finger numbers

The small **1, 2, 3, 4** you've been reading since Stage 1 are **editorial suggestions** in
most printed music — someone (an editor, sometimes the composer) is telling you a workable
fingering, not the only legal one. A bare **0** means an open string. More advanced editions
often print *fewer* numbers, because choosing your own fingering is part of musicianship —
don't read "no number" as "no finger needed."

## Position numbers

Text like **"III"** (a roman numeral) refers to **position** — where your whole left hand sits
on the neck — not a finger. You'll meet positions properly in Stage 6, but the reading rule
matters now: a finger number alone is never a complete instruction, because the same finger
plays a different pitch in first position versus third position versus fifth. **"III, 1st
finger"** and **"1st finger"** in first position are two entirely different notes.

The takeaway for now: when you see a number in a score, check whether it's small and sits right
next to a notehead (finger) or is a roman numeral floating above a passage (position). They
answer different questions.
