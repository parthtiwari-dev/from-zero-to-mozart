Everything from here to the end of Stage 8 builds one thing: the ear that lets you **hear a
tune and play it back**. This lesson is the map of that work — what "a good ear" actually is,
in what order it's built, and why you are starting from further ahead than you think.

## It is not perfect pitch

Perfect pitch — naming an isolated note with no reference — is rare, mostly fixed by early
childhood, and **not what playing by ear needs**. What you need is **functional pitch**:
given a key, you hear where each note sits *inside* it. That's **relative** hearing, it's
**trainable at any age**, and it's the whole game. Nobody plays a melody back by identifying
absolute frequencies; they hear "home, then up to the fifth, then the note that leans on
home."

## You already own the core of this

Your sargam training *is* functional hearing. Choose a Sa; every other swara is heard as a
distance and a flavour *from that Sa*, over a drone. That is exactly — mechanism for
mechanism — what Western movable-do solfège and scale-degree training do. You are not
building this ear from nothing. You are **pointing an ear you already have at Western
tunes**, and learning the Western names for what it already does.

## The skill stack, in order

| Layer | What it is | Where it's built |
|---|---|---|
| **Pitch memory & matching** | hold a pitch in your head; sing it back in tune | Movement D |
| **Functional hearing** | hear a note as a scale degree against home | Movement E — *the core* |
| **Harmonic hearing** | major/minor, the bass, the chord under the tune, cadences | Movement F |
| **Rhythmic hearing** | take a rhythm down accurately (you start strong here) | Movement G |
| **Transcription** | put rhythm + degrees together — a whole tune, by ear | Movements G–H |

Each layer leans on the one above it. That's the build order, and it's also the daily
practice order (`the-ear-gym-routine`).

## The shorthand

Through the whole gym, a scale note is written **^1 ^2 ^3 ^4 ^5 ^6 ^7** — plain degree
numbers. Solfège (*do re mi…*) and sargam (*Sa Re Ga…*) sit alongside as the same idea in
other alphabets; use whichever names stick. Numbers are the default here because they never
get ambiguous the way "do" does between major and minor.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Set a G drone. Sing — quietly, alone is fine — up the scale on numbers: "one, two, three…"
  up to eight and back down. Then sing just "one", then "five", then "one" again, checking each
  against the drone. If you can do that roughly in tune, you have everything you need to start
  the gym.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Thinking you need perfect pitch, deciding you're "not musical" because you don't have it, and
never starting. The ear that plays music back is relative, functional, and ordinary — and you
have a head start on it. The other mistake: trying to train all five layers at once instead
of building pitch-matching and degrees first.
