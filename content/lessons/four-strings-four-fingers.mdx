Four strings, and four fingers to press them — the whole left hand, at this stage, is those
sixteen combinations. Before any of it means a note on the page, get clear on what a finger
number *is*.

## The strings

Low to high: **G, D, A, E** (you tuned them in Stage 0). Under the left hand, the G string is
farthest from you and the E string nearest. To reach the low strings your **elbow swings
under the violin**; for the E string it swings back out. The hand shape doesn't change — only
the elbow.

## A finger number is a finger, not a note

The fingers are numbered **0, 1, 2, 3, 4**:

| Number | Finger |
|---|---|
| **0** | open — no finger, the string alone |
| **1** | index |
| **2** | middle |
| **3** | ring |
| **4** | little finger |

The **1st finger** is roughly a whole step up from the nut on *every* string — but that's a
different **note** on each one:

So **"2nd finger" on its own means nothing.** It's B on the G string, F♯ on the D string, C♯
on the A string, G♯ on the E string. A finger number is only an instruction once it's paired
with a string.

## The usual mistake

Reading a fingering as if it were a pitch — thinking "2 = C" because that's where you first
met the 2nd finger. It isn't; C is one *place* the 2nd finger goes. Always say the string and
the finger together: "A string, 2."

<TryThis>
  No bow, no reading. Place fingers 1-2-3-4 gently on the **A string**, one at a time, then
  lift all four at once and shake the hand out. Do it five times. Then the same on the D
  string, feeling the elbow swing a little further under.
</TryThis>
