Reading music on the violin is really a translation job: a mark on the page tells you a
**pitch**, and you have to know which **string** and which **finger** produces it. This lesson
sets up the two halves of that.

## The four strings

Lowest to highest: **G, D, A, E**. You met them tuning. They're your reference points — the
whole fingerboard is learned as distances from an open string.

## The four fingers

Your left-hand fingers are numbered **1, 2, 3, 4** — index to pinky. **0 means open string**,
no finger at all.

Here's the rule that saves you months of confusion: **a finger number is a finger, not a
note.** The 2nd finger plays a different pitch on every string, and even on one string it can
play two different pitches depending on where it lands. So a finger number is only ever half an
instruction. The useful form is always **string + finger**, and better still **string + finger
+ note name**:

> Not "second finger." Say "A string, high second finger — that's C-sharp."

Everything in this stage is about connecting the written mark to that full address.
