A real violin part doesn't just tell you the notes — it often tells you **which part of the
bow** to play them in, and **which direction**. These marks are a vocabulary, and the whole
point of this short lesson is that you can **recognise every one** when you meet it in
Vivaldi, or Mozart, or a study.

## Direction

| Mark | Means |
|---|---|
| **⊓** (a bracket, open at the bottom) | **down-bow** — pull the bow, frog toward tip. Naturally the heavier, stronger stroke. |
| **V** | **up-bow** — push the bow, tip toward frog. Naturally the lighter stroke. |

You met these in Stage 3. The strong beats of a bar tend to fall on ⊓ — there's a whole
lesson on why (`the-rule-of-the-down-bow`).

## Which part of the bow

| Abbreviation | In full | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **WB** | whole bow | frog to tip |
| **LH** (also **½L**, **Lo. ½**) | lower half | middle → frog |
| **UH** (also **½U**, **Up. ½**) | upper half | middle → tip |
| **M** / **MB** | middle | the balance point (rarely marked — it's the default) |
| **frog** / **at the frog** / **au talon** / **talon** / **nut** | — | the heel end, by your hand — most weight available |
| **tip** / **point** / **punta d'arco** / **Sp.** (*Spitze*, German) / **Pt.** (*punta*, Italian) | — | the far end — least weight, needs the most arm |

Different editions and countries use different words for the same places — `au talon`
(French), `Sp.` (German), `punta` (Italian) all just mean "frog" or "tip". The K.216 edition
uses `Sp.` and `au talon`, so those are worth knowing by sight.

## The retake

A small comma, an apostrophe, a half-circle, or the written word **"lift"** — all mean
**retake the bow**: lift it off the string and reset it (you practised this in
`hooked-bowing-and-retakes`). It's a silent gap, not a played note.

## The usual mistake

Reading a position number (roman `III`, `IV`) or a fingering (`1 2 3 4`) as a *bowing*
instruction. They're not — positions and fingers are the **left** hand; bowings are the
**right** arm. This is the same numeral tangle you met in Stage 3 (`finger-string-or-position`)
and Stage 4 (`the-first-note-of-mozart`). A `⊓`, a `V`, `Sp.`, `WB` — those are bowings. A
`3` or a `IV` is not.
