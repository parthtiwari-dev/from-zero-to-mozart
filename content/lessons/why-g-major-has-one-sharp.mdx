Apply the W-W-H-W-W-W-H recipe starting on G, one step at a time:

- G → A: whole step
- A → B: whole step
- B → C: **half** step (that's naturally close — no adjustment needed)
- C → D: whole step
- D → E: whole step
- E → F♯: whole step — but E → F is only a *half* step naturally, so F has to be **raised** to
  F♯ to make it a whole step
- F♯ → G: half step (which checks out — F♯ to G naturally is a half step)

That one raised note is the whole story: **G major needs exactly one sharp, F♯, and nothing
else**, because that's the only place the natural notes didn't already fit the pattern.

This is the derivation behind the key signature you met back in Stage 1 — it's not an arbitrary
fact to memorise, it falls straight out of the major-scale formula applied to G.

One thing major does **not** mean: "happy." It describes this specific interval structure.
Whether a piece in G major sounds bright, sad, tense or calm depends on melody, harmony,
tempo, register and how it's played — the key alone doesn't decide the mood.
