A scale moves step by step. An **arpeggio** plays a chord's notes one at a time instead of all
together — the same triad from two pages back, spread out melodically.

Instead of drilling every scale degree, an arpeggio trains **string crossings, hand geography,
and your ear's sense of harmony** — three things a plain scale doesn't isolate as directly.
It's also a preview of shifting: as your positions expand in Stage 6, arpeggios are usually the
first place you'll use a new position, because the hand shape maps so directly onto the chord.
