Octaves behave differently from thirds and sixths: instead of the low/high finger juggling,
they're a **fixed frame** — 1st and 4th finger, one string apart, that moves up and down the
neck as a single shape.

## Tuning octaves against each other, not against opens

The same wiggle-to-lock principle applies, but the target is different: an in-tune octave rings
completely still, with no beating at all — arguably the easiest interval to *hear* when it's
right, even though the frame itself takes practice to hold steady.

## The usual mistake

Placing the 4th finger independently of the 1st, checking each against an open string
separately, and hoping they line up. Octaves are a **frame**, not two independent notes — treat
the interval between the fingers as fixed, and place the whole shape at once.
