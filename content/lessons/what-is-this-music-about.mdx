Before you choose a fingering, before you plan a bowing, before you decide where the vibrato
goes — ask one question: **what is this music?** A march? A lament? A dance? A lullaby? A joke?
A prayer? A storm? That single answer decides everything downstream.

## The 18th-century word for it: *Affekt*

Classical-era musicians talked about the **affect** of a piece — the one governing feeling it
expresses. C.P.E. Bach wrote that a player "must himself be moved" to move the listener; Quantz
and Leopold Mozart said the same. You can't play a lament convincingly while thinking about
your fourth finger — you have to know it's a lament and let that colour every decision.

## Character → choices

| If it's a… | tempo | articulation | vibrato | colour | dynamic shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **march** | steady, firm | detached, marked | narrow | core, near the bridge | level, decisive |
| **lament** | slow | legato, sustained | wide, slow | covered, veiled | ebbing away |
| **dance** | lively, with lift | light, springing | light | bright, clear | buoyant |
| **lullaby** | gentle, unhurried | smooth | narrow, quiet | warm, soft | very soft, still |
| **hymn / prayer** | broad, even | sustained | slow, even | full, warm | arch to a climax |
| **scherzo / joke** | quick | crisp, pointed | little or none | bright | sudden contrasts |

The table isn't a rulebook — it's a starting point. But notice: *every* other decision in this
stage (vibrato width, rubato, colour, the agogic accent) has a column here. Character is the
thing they all serve.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take any tune you play. Say its character out loud in one word. Then make three concrete
  choices that serve that word — a tempo, a colour, a vibrato width — and play it that way.
  Then pick the *opposite* character and do it again. The tune should sound like two different
  pieces.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Skipping the question entirely — going straight to the notes and the fingering — so everything
you play comes out the same, "correct" and characterless. Or picking a character and then not
following through, so the *idea* is "lament" but the playing is still a neutral run of notes.

<Cite source="C. P. E. Bach, Versuch über die wahre Art das Clavier zu spielen (1753); Johann Joachim Quantz, On Playing the Flute (1752); Leopold Mozart, Violinschule (1756)">
  All three of the great Classical-era treatises say a version of the same thing: the player
  must feel the affect of the music, because a listener is only moved by a performer who is
  moved. Deciding what the music is *about* is the first act of interpretation.
</Cite>
