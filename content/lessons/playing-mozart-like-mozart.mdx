Everything in this stage — vibrato, rubato, the agogic accent, tone colour — plus Stage 6's
slides and Stage 3's articulation, comes down to one question when you sit down with a real
piece: **how much of each?** And the answer depends almost entirely on **when the music was
written.**

## The two settings

| Dial | Classical (Mozart, Haydn) | Romantic (Bruch, Tchaikovsky, Kreisler) |
|---|---|---|
| Vibrato | narrow, selective — an ornament on chosen notes | wide, warm, continuous |
| Slides / portamento | essentially off | expressive, frequent |
| Rubato | tiny, structural — a breath, never a wilful slowing | generous, audible ebb and flow |
| Articulation | non-legato is the *default*; slurs are the marked exception | long legato lines are the default |
| Dynamics | contained, speech-like, terraced; the Classical *Allegro* is restrained | broad swells, extremes |
| Tone | clear, transparent, light | thick, rich, projected |

Play a Mozart phrase with the right-hand column and it will sound wrong even though every note
is correct — too heavy, too slow, too smeared. This is the same rule Stage 3 gave you for every
symbol on the page: **the mark tells you *what*; the era tells you *how*.** It's just that here
the "marks" are your own expressive choices.

## Restraint is not the absence of expression

The Classical column is *restrained*, not *dead*. A Mozart slow movement is deeply expressive
— but its expression is in the shape of the line, the placement of a single agogic lean, one
perfectly-judged narrow vibrato on an arrival note. It's a whisper doing the work of a shout.
Playing Mozart dry and clipped, with no shaping at all, is as wrong as drowning him in vibrato.

## The usual mistake

Applying one modern "expressive default" — continuous wide vibrato, generous rubato, everything
slurred — to every piece regardless of era, so Mozart comes out sounding like a film score.
The over-correction is just as wrong: playing Classical music so austere and metronomic that
it has no life. The target is *shaped restraint*.
