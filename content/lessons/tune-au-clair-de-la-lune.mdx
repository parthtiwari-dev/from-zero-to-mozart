Possibly the simplest real tune there is — three notes for most of it, all steps, no leaps.
It's an 18th-century French song, and it was the very first sound ever recorded, in 1860.

Those two lines are the **A section**, and it repeats once ("Prête-moi ta plume…"). Then comes
the contrasting middle.

## Then the A section once more

"Ouvre-moi ta porte, pour l'amour de Dieu" — the opening line again, exactly as at the start,
to finish. The whole tune is **A – A – B – A**.

## The one thing this tune is for

A **completely even bow** on repeated notes, and a clean skip (D up to F♯, missing E). The
middle section adds your first **string crossing inside a tune** — the leap up to B.

## The usual mistake

On the repeated notes, letting the bow bump each time it changes direction — the left hand
isn't doing anything, so any unevenness is pure bow. And in the middle section, rushing the
string crossing to B: lift the whole arm deliberately, land clean, no scrape.
