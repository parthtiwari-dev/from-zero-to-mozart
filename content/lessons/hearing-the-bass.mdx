In a chord, the note that tells you the most about *what the harmony is doing* is the
**lowest** one — the bass. And it's the note a violinist most often needs to hear, because in
an ensemble you're usually playing the top of the texture or, occasionally, the bottom —
rarely the middle.

## Hum the bottom

Play or hear a chord and **hum its lowest note**. That's the whole drill. It sounds simple;
it's genuinely hard at first, because the ear is drawn to the top note (the melody) and the
bass has to be actively reached for.

Why it matters:

- The bass line is a melody in its own right — following it is how you hear a progression
  move.
- **Root position vs inversion:** the same three notes sound different depending on which is
  in the bass. C–E–G with C on the bottom is settled; with E on the bottom it's lighter,
  more passing. Your ear learns that from the bass note.
- When you *are* asked to play the bass or a low pedal, you need to find it by ear fast.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Set a G drone. Play a rolled G major chord (open G, open D, B on the A string). Hum down to
  the open G — the bass. Now play a rolled C chord (C on the G string, G on the D string, E on
  top on the E string) and hum its bass, C. Go back and forth, humming only the bottom note
  each time. The drone gives your voice a reference so it doesn't get pulled up to the melody.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Humming the note that's easiest to hear — which is almost always the top. The bass takes
deliberate attention; you have to decide to listen down. The other mistake: assuming the
bass is always the root of the chord. Often it is, but the moment a piece uses inversions,
the bass and the root are different notes — and hearing that difference is the point.

<Cite source="harmonic dictation practice (bass-line-first method); Stage 4 'inversions-and-spelling'; figured-bass tradition">
  Classical harmonic training has always started from the bass — figured bass notated the
  chord *as* a bass note plus intervals above it. Dictation courses still teach students to
  take the bass line down first, then fill in the upper voices.
</Cite>
