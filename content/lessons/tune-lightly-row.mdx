A German folk tune — *Hänschen klein* ("Little Hans") — that became the English nursery song
"Lightly Row". It's Suzuki's second piece, and it's built to teach one thing: dividing the
bow.

Those two lines together are the **A section**. The whole tune is **A – A′ – B – A′** — the
form is repetitive, so once A is solid you're most of the way there.

## The one thing this tune is for

**Bow division** — deciding, before you play, which part of the bow each note gets, so you
never run out of bow or pile up at the frog. Short notes: less bow. Long notes: more.

## The usual mistake

Running out of bow on the half notes because the quarters before them used too much. Plan it:
the two quarters take the *upper* half, the half note takes a whole bow from there. And on the
repeated notes (the B section), letting the bow lurch even though the left hand is doing
nothing — keep it dead even.
