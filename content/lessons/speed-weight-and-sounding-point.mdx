Everything the right arm does to the *sound* comes down to three controls. Every violin
teacher from Flesch to Galamian to Fischer names the same three, and every one of them says
the same thing about them: **they work together.** Change one and you have to adjust at least
one of the others, or the tone breaks.

## The three controls

- **Bow speed** — how fast the hair travels along the string, which is the same as *how much
  bow* you spend on a note. This is your **volume** control and your **expression** control.
  Faster bow → wider swing → louder.
- **Bow weight** — how much of the arm's weight you let sink into the string (through a loose
  hand — you *lean*, you don't press). This is mostly a **colour** control: more weight makes
  the tone brighter and more focused, up to a limit. Past the limit it chokes.
- **Sounding point** — *where* between the **bridge** and the **fingerboard** the hair meets
  the string. Near the bridge: bright, strong, but fussy. Near the fingerboard: soft, veiled,
  easy. (The next lesson, `the-five-lanes`, is all about this one.)

## How they trade

A few rules you'll use constantly:

- **Nearer the bridge** → the string is stiffer there, so it needs a **slower** bow and
  **more** weight to speak.
- **Faster bow** → needs **more** weight to keep the string caught (or move toward the
  fingerboard, where less weight is fine).
- **The thick low strings** (G, D) don't like the bow as close to the bridge as the thin E
  string does — their best sounding point sits a little further out.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  On the open D string, play a steady medium note. Now, keeping the bow speed and the weight
  exactly the same, slide the sounding point slowly from near the fingerboard toward the
  bridge. At some point it starts to scratch — that's the weight now being too little for that
  spot. Add a little weight and it clears up. You just felt the three controls trading.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Trying to fix a bad sound by leaning on **one** control — usually weight. If the tone is
rough, "press less" is only one of four fixes: you could also **speed the bow up**, **move
toward the bridge**, or **tilt the bow**. Knowing they're a set means you always have options.

  say the same in their own words — it's the one thing the whole tradition agrees on.
</Cite>
