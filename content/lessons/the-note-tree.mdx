Every note value is the one above it, **cut in half**. That single rule builds the whole
system:

- a **whole note** = two **half notes**
- a half note = two **quarter notes**
- a quarter note = two **eighth notes**
- an eighth note = two **sixteenth notes**

So one whole note lasts exactly as long as two halves, or four quarters, or eight eighths, or
sixteen sixteenths. Same span of time, divided finer and finer.

## It's a *ratio*, not a stopwatch

A quarter note is not "one second." It's **one beat** — and a beat is however long the tempo
makes it. Speed the tempo up and every value gets shorter together; the *relationships* stay
exactly the same. A half note is always twice a quarter, whatever the tempo.

## How the counts work

- **Whole note** — hold it four beats: count "1 – 2 – 3 – 4 –", keep the count running.
- **Half note** — two beats: "1 – " then "3 – ".
- **Quarter note** — one beat: "1", "2", "3", "4".
- **Eighth notes** — two per beat: "1 **and** 2 **and**".
- **Sixteenth notes** — four per beat: "1‑e‑&‑a 2‑e‑&‑a". (You'll drill these in Movement H.)

(There is one more level down — **thirty-second notes**, a third beam — that turns up in fast
Classical and Romantic music. Recognise the extra beam; you won't need to play them in this
stage.)

## The usual mistake

Trying to give each value a fixed length in your head — "a quarter note is about this fast."
It isn't a fixed anything. Count the beat, and let every value be its fraction of that beat.
When the tempo changes, you don't relearn the note; you just count faster or slower.

<TryThis>
  Clap one long note for four steady beats. Then the same four beats as four claps. Then as
  eight. Keep your counting voice at the exact same speed the whole time — only your hands
  speed up.
</TryThis>
