The violin has no frets, so the space between two fingers is something you *feel*, not
something you land on. In first position there are really only **four hand shapes** — four
patterns of whole steps and half steps between the four fingers. Every scale you'll play in
first position is one of them.

## Where the half step is

A **whole step** is a finger's-width gap. A **half step** is two fingers almost *touching*.
The pattern is just: *which two fingers are close together?*

| Pattern | Close fingers | Sound | Example (D string) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1–23–4** | 2 & 3 | major | D E **F♯ G** A |
| **12–3–4** | 1 & 2 | minor | D **E F** G A |
| **1–2–34** | 3 & 4 | major, high 3rd | D E F♯ **G♯ A** |
| **1–2–3–4** | none (all apart) | whole-tone | D E F♯ G♯ A♯ *(rare — a reference)* |

Only the **2nd finger moved** — a half step back. Same hand, one finger slides.

## The usual mistake

Spacing the fingers **evenly** — dropping the 2nd finger halfway between 1 and 3 by default.
The gap is never in the middle; it's at one specific place, and it *moves* depending on the
pattern. The hand is not a fixed grid.

<TryThis>
  On the D string, no bow: place the **major** pattern (D–E–F♯–G), fingers down. Now slide
  just the 2nd finger back to touch the 1st — that's the **minor** pattern. Slide it forward
  again. Back and forth, ten times, feeling the two shapes. Then bow each one slowly.
</TryThis>
