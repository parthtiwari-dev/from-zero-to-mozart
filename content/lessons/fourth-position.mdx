4th position sits between 3rd and 5th the same way 2nd sits between 1st and 3rd — no landmark
of its own, found by ear or by shifting partway between two positions you already trust.

## Why it's last in the teaching order

4th position is genuinely useful — but like 2nd, it's usually substitutable. A passage that
seems to want 4th position can often be played just as well by staying in 3rd and using an
extension (`reaching-one-more-note`), or by shifting straight to 5th a little early. That's why
this course places it after 5th rather than before: by the time you meet it, you already have
two solid neighbours to find it *between*.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Shift from 3rd position toward 5th on the A string, but stop partway — check your 1st finger
  against E (4th position). Then try arriving at the same note by shifting *down* from 5th
  instead. Both directions should land on the same pitch; if they don't, one of your two
  landmark positions has drifted.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Treating a landmark-free position as "less real" than 3rd or 5th and rushing past it. 2nd and
4th ask more of your ear precisely *because* they have no physical shortcut — that's more
practice value, not less, even though they show up in less repertoire.
