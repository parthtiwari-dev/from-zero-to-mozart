A small dot **directly after** a notehead adds **half of that note's value** on top. One
rule, and it works on any value.

- A dotted **half note** = 2 + 1 = **3 beats**.
- A dotted **quarter note** = 1 + ½ = **1½ beats**.
- A dotted **whole note** = 4 + 2 = **6 beats** (only in bars long enough to hold it).

The dot is exactly half again — never a vague "a bit longer."

## The dotted-quarter-and-eighth pair

This is the one you'll meet most — the "long–short" limp of a march or a carol. The dotted
quarter holds through beat 1 and half of beat 2; the eighth falls on the "**and**".

## The usual mistake

Two versions of it. Playing the dotted quarter as a full **two** beats — ignoring that the
dot is only *half* again. Or evening it out to "long–long" and losing the limp. Say the
count out loud with the "&" in it and the timing fixes itself.

<Margin>
  A **double dot** (two dots) adds half, then half again — a double-dotted quarter is
  1 + ½ + ¼ = 1¾ beats. Rare, mostly older music; recognise it, you won't drill it here.
</Margin>
