Sixteenth notes — four to a beat, "1 e & a" — are where beginners rush hardest. On the D
string, same routine: clap, say, air-bow, play, then reveal. Keep the metronome slow and
speed up only when it's clean.

## If the sixteenths run away

Play the whole line at half the tempo, subdividing every beat into four out loud, even the
plain quarters. Then take the metronome up five clicks at a time. Rushing is almost always a
subdivision that stopped — keep "e & a" running under everything.

<Cite source="the original PDF course, §27; violinist.com on rushing">
  "Clap four even taps per metronome beat at 50–60 BPM before asking your fingers to do it."
  Rushing sixteenths is the most common rhythm fault there is.
</Cite>
