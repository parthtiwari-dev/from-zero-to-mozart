Normally you draw the bow across the string. The word **pizz.** tells you to set the bow
aside — keep hold of it — and **pluck** the string with a right-hand fingertip. The word
**arco** tells you to pick the bow back up.

## The marks

| Mark | Means |
|---|---|
| **pizz.** (pizzicato) | pluck with a right-hand finger |
| **arco** | back to the bow (cancels a `pizz.` passage) |
| **`+`** above a note | **left-hand pizzicato** — pluck with a left-hand finger (usually 4th or 3rd), so you can pluck without putting the bow down |
| a small **circle with a line** rising from it | **snap / Bartók pizzicato** — pluck so hard the string slaps the fingerboard, a percussive crack (20th-century — you'll see it, you won't meet it in Mozart) |

A plucked passage usually gets **pizz.** once where it starts and **arco** once where it ends —
not on every note.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Keep the bow in your hand — thumb and the last two fingers holding it out of the way. With
  the fleshy pad (not the nail) of your right index finger, pluck the open D string just over
  the **end of the fingerboard**. Aim for a round, warm "boom". Too near the bridge it's
  glassy; too far up it flops.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Plucking with the **fingernail** — thin and harsh. Use the pad. Plucking in the wrong spot —
over the end of the fingerboard is the sweet spot. And forgetting to actually **re-take the
bow** at `arco`, so the first bowed note comes out as a pluck.

<Cite source="Grove, on pizzicato; standard performance history">
  Monteverdi asked string players to pluck as early as 1624. Left-hand pizzicato is a
  Paganini signature; the hard, string-slapping "snap" pizzicato is named for Bartók, who
  wrote it into his string quartets.
</Cite>
