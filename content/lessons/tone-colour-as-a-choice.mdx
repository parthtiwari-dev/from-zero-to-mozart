Stage 3 taught you the colour *words* — *sul tasto*, *sul ponticello*, *flautando*, *con
sordino*. Stage 5 taught you the five lanes between the bridge and the fingerboard. This lesson
is about using them as a **palette** — picking a colour because it fits the character, not
because the page told you to.

## The colours you have

| Where the bow is | The colour | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Lane 1–2, near the bridge | focused, bright, full of core | a fanfare, an arrival, intensity |
| Lane 3, the default | plain, direct, "normal" | most things |
| Lane 4–5, near the fingerboard (*sul tasto*) | soft, veiled, hollow, distant | a memory, a lament, a *pianissimo* |
| Right against the bridge (*sul ponticello*) | glassy, thin, metallic, eerie | tension, the sinister, a special effect |
| Light and fast, over the fingerboard (*flautando*) | flute-like, weightless | a shimmer, a halo |
| Mute on (*con sordino*) | hushed, grey, a different instrument | a nocturne, something withheld |

And the strings themselves are colours: the same written G is **dark and thick** on the G
string, **bright and clear** on the D string. Choosing which string to play a note on is
choosing its colour.

## The choice comes from the character

You don't reach for *sul tasto* because it's an interesting effect. You reach for it because
the phrase is a whispered memory and a covered colour is what a whispered memory sounds like.
The character decides (next lesson); the colour serves it.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  One phrase you know, three colours: normal (lane 3), *sul tasto* (veiled), *sul ponticello*
  (glassy). Then decide: which colour would you give a lullaby? A ghost story? The triumphant
  last line of a hymn? Play the phrase in the colour you chose for each.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Living your whole playing life in lane 3 — one colour, forever — so every piece sounds like the
same voice reading different words. The opposite mistake is treating the colour words as
effects to show off, dropping a *sul ponticello* into a tender phrase because you can. Colour
is a choice in service of a character, not a trick.

<Cite source="this course's Stage 3 'the colour words' and Stage 5 'the five lanes'; Ivan Galamian, Principles (1962), on the sounding point">
  Galamian's model of the sounding point is usually taught as a loudness control — nearer the
  bridge is louder. It is equally a *colour* control, and the great players use it that way:
  the bow's distance from the bridge is one of the violin's main expressive dials.
</Cite>
