The daily bow routine, gathered in one place to play. None of it is a piece — it's the
warm-up and workout that makes the pieces possible. Five or ten focused minutes of this at the
start of a session is worth more than an hour of grinding through repertoire with a rough bow.

## How to use this page

Not all of it every day. Pick: **always** the son filé; then one of the détaché / slurred /
martelé scales; then two minutes of bowing variations. Rotate the keys through the ones you
know (G, D, A, C). Stop the moment the bow hand tightens.

<Cite source="the standard warm-up routine — Simon Fischer (Basics, Scales); Ivan Galamian's scale method; Yuri Yankelevich's bowing-variation practice">
  Every one of these is centuries old and still done daily by professionals. The son filé
  levels the bow; the scales groove the strokes; the bowing variations, as Yankelevich showed
  with the Kreutzer studies, cover every bow-control problem there is in a few minutes.
</Cite>
