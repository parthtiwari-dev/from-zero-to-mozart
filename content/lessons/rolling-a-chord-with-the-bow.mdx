Stage 4 told you *why* a violin chord is rolled: the bridge is **curved**, so the bow can't
press three or four strings flat at once — it has to move across them, bottom to top. This
lesson is the **right-arm half** of that: the actual bow gesture. And you can do it now, on
open strings — it's the exact motion the Mozart concerto opens with.

## The gesture: drop low, roll up

- **Land the bow on the lowest string** of the chord, with weight.
- As the bow moves (usually a **down-bow**), let the arm **drop through the string levels** —
  the weight rolls from the bottom string up to the top, the same arm-level move as a string
  crossing (`string-crossings`), just done as one continuous sweep.
- **Arrive on the top note(s)** and let them ring — that's where the melody usually is, so
  that's what should sing.

It's quick — a chord isn't spread out slowly like a harp; it's a fast *bottom-to-top brush*
that lands on top, all inside one bow.

## With a finger on top — a real G-major chord

Add one left-hand finger and it's a G-major chord you can play: **open G, open D, and B on the
A string** (1st finger). Three different strings, so it rolls cleanly.

## This is the first note of Mozart

The K.216 solo entrance (Stage 4, `the-first-note-of-mozart`) is a **G–B–D chord rolled
bottom to top**, landing on a ringing G, on a down-bow. Everything you just did on open
strings is that gesture. The left-hand shape for the real chord is a Stage 6 job (stopped
notes on three strings at once), but the **bow move is yours right now.**

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Roll the three open strings G–D–A as one chord, down-bow, eight times, with a retake between
  each. Aim for: a clear start on the G, a smooth fast roll, and a ringing sustained A on top,
  every time. Then try G–D–B (with 1st finger). That's the Mozart gesture in your arm.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Rolling too **slowly**, so the chord sounds like a harp arpeggio instead of a chord — or
crushing all three strings at once trying to make them sound together (the curved bridge won't
let you, and you get a crunch). It's a *fast* brush that arrives on top. The other mistake:
letting the top note arrive weak because you spent all the bow on the roll — save bow for the
note that sings.
