Scores and teachers talk about *where* in the bow you are and *which way* it's going, constantly.
Learn the words now so those instructions mean something.

## Along the bow

From your hand outward: the **frog** (also "the heel"), the **lower half**, the **middle**, the
**upper half**, the **tip** (also "the point"). Different parts feel different — the frog is
heavy and powerful, the tip is light and needs help.

## Which direction

- **Down-bow** — written **⊓** — you *pull*, moving the bow from frog toward tip. It naturally
  gets a little heavier.
- **Up-bow** — written **V** — you *push*, tip back toward frog. It naturally gets lighter.

Strong beats often fall on down-bows; that's a default, not a law.

## Where it crosses the string

The bow travels in a straight lane **parallel to the bridge**. Where the hair actually meets
the string — the **contact point**, or sounding point — is something you steer. Galamian
described roughly five lanes between the bridge and the fingerboard, each with its own colour:
near the bridge is loud and intense and hard to control; near the fingerboard is soft and
flute-like. For now, aim for the middle lane and keep the bow straight.
