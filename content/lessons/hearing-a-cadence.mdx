A cadence is how a phrase punctuates — a full stop, a comma, a question mark, a surprise.
Stage 4's `what-a-cadence-is` and `the-other-three-cadences` taught the four kinds on paper.
Hearing which one just went past is a **gate skill** — it's on the Intermediate check — because
it proves you're following the harmony, not just the tune.

## The four endings

| Cadence | Chords | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Perfect** (authentic) | **V → I** | a firm full stop. Done. The phrase has landed and settled. |
| **Plagal** | **IV → I** | the "a-men" ending. Also a stop, but softer, rounder, less driven. |
| **Half** | anything **→ V** | a comma, or a raised eyebrow. It stops on tension — the phrase is unfinished, expecting an answer. |
| **Deceptive** | **V → vi** | the swerve. You brace for I and get vi instead — a minor chord where home should be. A surprise, a "not yet." |

Two of them end *resolved* (perfect, plagal); two end *unresolved* (half — on the dominant;
deceptive — on the wrong resting chord). That's the first split to hear.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  In G, play these pairs slowly as broken chords (one note at a time) and label each by ear:
  D–F♯–A then G–B–D (perfect); C–E–G then G–B–D (plagal); G–B–D then D–F♯–A (half). Then close
  your eyes, play one at random, and name it. The perfect/plagal pair both *resolve* — tell
  them apart by how hard they land. The half one just doesn't finish.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Only listening to the last chord. A cadence is a *move* — two chords — and the same
destination chord means different things depending on where it came from (V→I is perfect,
IV→I is plagal). Hear the pair. The other mistake: missing a deceptive cadence because you
"heard a resolution" — you heard a landing, but on vi, not I. If home suddenly went minor,
you were deceived.
