**Messa di voce** — Italian, "the placing of the voice" — is a long crescendo followed by a
long diminuendo **on a single note**, with *nothing changing but the volume*. Not the pitch,
not the tone colour, not (for you, yet) any vibrato. It's the oldest tone exercise there is,
and it's the graduation test of everything in this stage's first half.

## What it is

A note starts soft, swells to full, and fades back to soft — a smooth arch of sound. On the
page it's a **hairpin pair on one note**: `< >`.

## How the controls move

On **one** note you can't crescendo with bow speed alone — a whole bow only lasts so long. So
messa di voce is mostly **weight and sounding point**:

- **Growing:** add arm weight, slide toward the bridge, the bow speed rising only slightly
  toward the middle.
- **The peak** sits around the middle of the bow.
- **Fading:** release the weight, slide back toward the fingerboard, ease the speed.

Do it on a **down-bow** and an **up-bow**. Do it at the **frog** (harder — the bow is heavy
there) and at the **tip** (harder — the bow is light there). A player who owns messa di voce is
ready to shape *any* note, anywhere in the bow.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  One note — open A — one very slow down-bow. Four counts growing, four counts fading, the peak
  at the middle of the bow. Then the up-bow. Then move to the D string, the G string, the E
  string. Two minutes of this a day, alongside your son filé, and your control over a single
  note gets noticeably better within a week.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Letting the **pitch sag or the tone go rough** at the loud peak — the crescendo is *only*
volume; everything else stays put. And rushing: messa di voce is slow by nature, and the
value is in sustaining evenly through the whole arch. The third mistake: making the swell with
bow speed and running out of bow at the peak — use weight and lane.
