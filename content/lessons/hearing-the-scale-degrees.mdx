This is the most important ear lesson in the course, and it's built on something you already
do: **listen against a drone.**

The idea is simple. Turn on a held tonic note underneath, play any note of the scale, and
learn to recognise *which degree it is* by the way it sits against the drone — its flavour,
its pull. Not "that's a major third above the last note" (an interval, floating), but "that's
**^3**" (a degree, anchored). Real music always has a key around it, so this is the ear that
actually transfers.

## Each degree has a flavour

Open the tuner **·** drone **·** metronome bar and hold a **D** drone. Play the D major scale
slowly, and *say each degree number* as you go. Listen to how each one leans:

## The drill, in levels

Do a few minutes of this most days. The drone stays on the whole time.

1. **Sing/play the scale on solfège** — Do Re Mi Fa Sol La Ti Do — up and down, slowly.
2. **One note.** Play a single random degree. Name it before you check by playing the scale up
   to it.
3. **Two notes.** Play two degrees in a row. Name both.
4. **A fragment.** Play the first three or four notes of a tune you know. Name the degrees
   (Twinkle opens ^1 ^1 ^5 ^5 ^6 ^6 ^5).
5. **Tell them apart.** "Was that ^3 or ^5?" "Major 3rd or minor 3rd from home?"

## Anchor the intervals to tunes you've played

Once the degrees are steady, interval names attach easily — and the best anchors are the
course's own tunes, because you already know how they go:

| Interval | Anchor — first notes of… | Degrees |
|---|---|---|
| Perfect 5th, up | **Twinkle** ("Twin-kle") | ^1 → ^5 |
| Major 3rd, up | **When the Saints** ("Oh (when the) *saints*") | ^1 → ^3 |
| Perfect 4th, up | **Amazing Grace** ("A-*ma*-zing") | ^5 → ^1 |
| Minor 2nd | the top of any scale, ^7 → ^8 | ^7 → ^8 |

## The usual mistake

Drilling intervals as bare pairs of notes with no key around them — "name this leap." Research
on how musicians actually hear is clear: we process notes by their **place in the key**, not as
free-floating intervals, and interval-only training doesn't transfer well to reading or
playing. Learn the **degrees** first, against the drone. The intervals then come almost for
free.
