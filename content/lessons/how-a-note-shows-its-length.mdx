In Stage 1 a note's **height** on the staff told you its pitch. Its **shape** tells you its
length. Four parts of the drawing carry that:

- **The head** — the oval. **Filled in** (a solid dot) = a short note. **Open** (a hollow
  ring) = a long one.
- **The stem** — the vertical line off the head. A whole note has none; every shorter value
  has one.
- **The flag** — the little curved tail off the end of a stem, on a note standing on its own.
  One flag halves the length; two flags halve it again.
- **The beam** — a thick bar joining the stems of a group of short notes. Same job as flags,
  drawn across several notes at once.

Stem *direction* — pointing up or down — is only neatness. It never changes the length.

## The usual mistake

Reading the head's height (the pitch) and not registering the fill (the length). An open head
and a filled head on the very same line are the same note held for completely different
times — four beats versus one. Two questions on every note: which line, and filled or open.

<Cite source="Western notation, settled around 1600">
  Modern noteheads come from medieval mensural notation, where open versus filled shapes were
  already how scribes marked long against short. Stems and flags were added later, as music
  needed finer and finer divisions.
</Cite>
