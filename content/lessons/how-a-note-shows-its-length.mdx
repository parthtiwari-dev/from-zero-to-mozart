On the staff, a note's **height** is its pitch — that was Stage 1. Its **shape** is its
length. Four parts of the drawing carry that information:

- **The head** — the oval. **Filled in** (a solid dot) means a short note; **open** (a hollow
  ring) means a long one.
- **The stem** — the vertical line off the head. A whole note has none; everything shorter
  has a stem.
- **The flag** — the little tail off the end of a stem, on a note standing alone. One flag,
  two flags — each one halves the length again.
- **The beam** — a thick bar joining the stems of short notes into a group. A beam does the
  same job as flags, just drawn across several notes at once.

Stem *direction* — up or down — is only tidiness. It never changes the length.

## The usual mistake

Reading the head's *height* and skipping its *fill*. An open head and a filled head sitting
on the very same line are the same pitch and completely different lengths — one held for four
beats, the other for one. Check both every time: which line (pitch), and filled or open
(length).

<Cite source="Western notation, settled around 1600">
  Modern noteheads come from medieval mensural notation, where open versus filled shapes were
  already how scribes marked long against short. The stems and flags were added as the music
  needed finer divisions.
</Cite>
