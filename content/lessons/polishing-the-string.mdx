Vibrato is a **small** version of a motion your hand already knows: the loose lengthwise slide
along the string. If you make that slide big and obvious first — "polishing" the string — you
can feel the exact release in the fingertip and the finger joints that a good vibrato needs,
where a tiny motion would hide it.

## The drill

Put your 3rd finger down on the D string in first position — that's G. Now slide the whole hand
smoothly up to third position and back, keeping the 3rd finger lightly on the string the whole
way, **letting the first joint of the finger flex and release** as you go. Don't lift the
finger; don't grip. It's a windscreen-wiper motion — smooth, loose, repeated.

## Now shrink it

Once the big slide feels genuinely loose, make it smaller. And smaller. Keep the same releasing
feel in the finger, but let the hand travel less and less, until it's barely moving off one
spot — and now it's a vibrato. The pitch stops sliding a whole third and starts just
shimmering. Same motion, same release, tiny.

<TryThis>
  Big polish, ten times, loose. Then medium — half the distance. Then small — a couple of
  centimetres. Then tiny — you're vibrating. Do the whole shrink in one go, slowly, feeling
  that nothing in the hand changed except how far it travels. If it tightens up as it gets
  smaller, go back to big and start again.
</TryThis>

## Hear it

The big slide you *can* hear — a slow glissando. The tiny version, the vibrato, you can't play
here. Listen for a slow, wide vibrato on a long note in a recording of **Bruch's Violin
Concerto No. 1** (the very opening) — that broad shimmer is this exact motion, shrunk.

## The usual mistake

Gripping the string harder as the motion gets smaller, so the release disappears and you end up
with a stiff, fast tremble instead of a vibrato. The finger stays *light* — firm enough to
keep its place, loose enough to flex. If the small version feels tight, the big version wasn't
loose enough yet.
