You built *Amazing Grace* back in Stage 3 — to read a line of dynamic marks. Here it comes back
as the test piece for the whole Stage 7 toolkit: vibrato, rubato, the agogic accent, colour,
character. The notes are the same and you know them cold. The job now is to **decide** how to
play it, and then play it that way every time.

In G major, first position, 3/4. You know the tune; this lesson is about the choices on top.

## The five choices, worked

1. **Character:** a hymn — quiet gratitude, not a grand anthem. One word: *thankful*.
2. **Peak:** the high D on "once was **lost**" (phrase 3). Everything builds to it and eases
   after.
3. **Breathe:** at the end of each two-bar pair — four small silences, like the ends of sung
   lines.
4. **Vibrato:** narrow and slow, on the long dotted-quarter notes only (the Gs and Bs), and a
   little wider on the peak D. Nothing on the short notes.
5. **Rubato & colour:** a hair of extra time leaning into the peak D, given back over the
   descent. Warm, plain colour (lane 3) throughout; ease toward the fingerboard for the last
   bar so the ending sounds withdrawn.

## The one thing this tune is for

**Making a set of expressive choices and repeating them.** Not "playing it beautifully" in
some vague way — deciding, concretely, five things, and then being able to do them the same way
every time and say why you chose each. That's interpretation, and it's the fifth capability the
whole course is built toward (`PRD.md` §3).

## The usual mistake

Playing it "with feeling" but differently every time — that's improvising, not interpreting.
Or picking the choices and then not following through, so the *plan* says "narrow vibrato,
withdrawn ending" but the playing is a generic pretty run. Decide, then deliver, then repeat.
