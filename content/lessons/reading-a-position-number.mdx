Stage 3's `finger-string-or-position` already told you a roman numeral over a note can mean a
**string** or a **position**, decided by context. Now that your hand actually has a 3rd
position to go to, that fact stops being trivia.

## What `III` is asking you to do

In a real edition, `III` written over a passage — or, in a modern edition, the plain words "3rd
pos." — is an instruction: **move your hand to 3rd position before this note, and stay there
until told otherwise.** It's not naming a finger, and it's not naming a string (context tells
you which meaning is in play — a numeral sitting where fingerings usually go, next to Arabic
1-2-3-4s, is almost always a position, not a string).

The numeral doesn't repeat on every note — it appears once, at the moment the hand needs to
move, and stays in force until either a new position numeral appears or the music clearly drops
back to 1st (often marked, sometimes just implied by the notes themselves getting low again).

## Now you can verify you're actually there

This is the part that's new: up to now, `III` was a fact you noted. Now it's a fact you can
**check** — play the passage, then confirm against the octave self-check from `third-position`
(D/A/E strings against their matching open string) that your hand really did land in 3rd
position, not just somewhere approximately close.

## The usual mistake

Reading `III` as "use your 3rd finger" — the exact confusion `what-a-position-is` warned about,
now showing up in real notation instead of in the abstract. A position numeral tells the hand
where to live, not which finger to use for any particular note.
