To move the bow from one string to the next, the **whole arm** changes level — like a lift
stopping on a different floor. The hand and bow just come along for the ride.

<NotatedExample
  abc={`X:1
M:C
L:1/4
K:C clef=treble
D A D A | D A D A |]`}
  caption="Open D and open A, alternating. Cross with the arm, not the wrist — the wrist and fingers only smooth the very end of the motion."
  defaultBpm={60}
/>

Each string sits at its own arm height. G is highest (arm up), E is lowest (arm down), D and A
are in between. Once you know those four levels, a string crossing is just moving between two
of them smoothly, without a jerk and without accidentally catching the string in between.

When the crossing is fast and repeated between two neighbouring strings (like the drill above,
sped up), it's sometimes called **bariolage** — a rocking motion where the wrist and fingers do
more of the work and the arm stays fairly still. But learn the big arm-level version first; the
small fast version grows out of it.
