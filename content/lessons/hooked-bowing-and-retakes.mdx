Two small right-hand moves that solve the same problem: **getting the bow where it needs to
be**. A hooked bowing plays two notes in one direction with a stop between; a retake lifts and
resets the bow between phrases.

## Hooked bowing — two notes, one direction, a stop

A **hooked** (or "linked") bowing takes two notes and plays them **both down-bow** (or both
up-bow), with a **clean stop** between. Written as a **slur with a staccato dot** on the second
note.

Why bother? Two reasons:

- **Bow economy** — a long string of dotted notes played with alternating bows walks the bow
  right off the end. Hooking the short notes keeps it in place.
- **Landing the strong beat** — it lets you arrive at the next downbeat on a **down-bow**,
  which matters (see below).

Your dotted-rhythm fluency from Stage 2 makes this easy to *feel* — the bow just has to stop
cleanly where your ear already puts the gap.

## Retakes — lift and reset

A **retake** is a silent lift of the bow off the string, moving it back to a more useful part,
then setting it down again — a grammatical pause "filled only with resonance". You met the
symbol in Stage 3 (`down-bow-up-bow-and-the-retake`); here you make the gesture.

Use it to **get the bow home before a phrase**: you played out to the tip, and the next phrase
wants to start down-bow with lots of bow — so during the breath, you retake. The motion is a
small **circle**, not a stab: the bow loops up and back rather than jumping.

## The rule of the down-bow

By long tradition, **the strong beat wants a down-bow.** A down-bow is naturally heavier —
gravity and the arm both help — so the first beat of the bar, and accented notes, fall on
`⊓`. Hooked bowings and retakes exist largely to keep it that way when the note-count doesn't
work out on its own. There's a whole lesson on this coming (`the-rule-of-the-down-bow`); for
now, just know that's *why* you'd choose to hook or retake.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take a dotted-rhythm fragment of a tune you know (the "Oh, when the saints" pickup works, or
  any march). Play it with alternating bows and notice the bow drifting toward the tip. Now
  hook the short notes onto the long ones — down-down, up-up — and feel the bow stay put. Then
  practise one retake: a full down-bow, a loop back in the rest, another full down-bow.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Making the hooked short note a *slur* instead of a stop — so the two notes run together and
the rhythm goes soft. There must be a clean, tiny silence between them. For the retake: doing
it as a jerky lift-and-drop instead of a smooth circle, which lands with a thump. Loop it.

<Cite source="the orchestral bowing tradition — Georg Muffat's account of Lully's rules (1698); Leopold Mozart, Violinschule (1756)">
  Leopold Mozart wrote that a bar not starting with a rest should begin on a down-bow. Hooked
  bowings and retakes are the everyday tools for obeying that rule without running out of bow.
</Cite>
