A few leftover navigation and shorthand signs. You'll meet most of these in **orchestral
parts and lead sheets** rather than a solo line — but they're worth knowing on sight. This is
a reference card.

## Rehearsal marks

A **boxed or circled letter** (A, B, C…) or **number**, dropped in at structural points in the
music. They exist so a conductor can say "from **letter G**" instead of counting bars aloud.
They make **no sound** — they're a shared label.

They appear in Romantic-era scores and after — credited to Louis Spohr — once movements grew
long enough that "take it from bar 348" stopped being practical. In modern scores the numbers
are often the bar number itself.

## Repeat-bar and simile signs

| Sign | Means |
|---|---|
| **`%`** (a slash with a dot each side), centred in a bar | play the **previous bar** again |
| **`%`** with a small **2** above, spanning two bars | play the **previous two bars** again |
| a **slash** inside a bar | repeat the **previous beat** |
| the word **simile** / **simili** | carry on "in the same manner" — keep the articulation, bowing or pattern just written, without re-marking every note |

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Next time you have an ensemble part, find a multi-bar rest and actually count it — "rest,
  two, three, four; rest, two, three, four" — landing your entry on the exact beat the number
  runs out. Counting a long rest accurately is a skill, and it's the one that gets you back in
  on time.
</TryThis>
