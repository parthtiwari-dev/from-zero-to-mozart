**This lesson is optional.** Skip it unless you come from an Indian-rhythm background — nothing
later in the course depends on it. It's here once, as a map, so that if you already think in
tabla or solkattu syllables you can see how they line up with Western counting. After this,
the course speaks only in "one and two and".

## The map

| Western idea | Roughly, in the Indian system |
|---|---|
| the beat | **mātrā** — one count |
| tempo, and slow / medium / fast | **laya**; *vilambit* / *madhya* / *drut* |
| beat 1, the downbeat everything resolves to | **sam** |
| the beat-groups a time signature implies | **vibhāg**, marked by claps (*tālī*) and waves (*khālī*) |
| eighth notes (two per beat) | **dugun** — 2 : 1 |
| triplets (three per beat) | **tigun** — 3 : 1 |
| sixteenth notes (four per beat) | **chaugun** — 4 : 1 |
| three-against-two / hemiola | **āḍ** (*dedh gun*) — 3 : 2 |
| a phrase stated three times, landing on the downbeat | **tihāī** |

The layakārī ratios are the strongest fit: you already *feel* dugun, tigun, chaugun and āḍ,
so triplets, sixteenths and hemiola are mostly a matter of learning how they're **drawn**,
not how they sound.

## The one real difference

A Western time signature is **short, regular, and divisive** — you take a bar and cut it into
equal beats, grouped in twos and threes, and the pattern repeats every bar or two. A tāl is a
longer **cycle** (an āvartan) that you keep returning to. So a time signature is like a very
small, tidy tāl — 3/4 is close to a three-mātrā feel, 4/4 to four — but the Western unit is
much shorter and more repetitive, and the grouping is *drawn* on the page (barlines, beams)
rather than recited.

What's genuinely new for you: reading duration off a staff at sight, the **tie** and the
**dot** as written symbols, and connecting a written value to a length of **bow**.

## What to do with this

Count however sits in your mouth when you practise alone. When you follow the course, a
recording, or a teacher, count in "one and two and" — that keeps everyone lined up.
