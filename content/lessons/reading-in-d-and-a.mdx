G major was one sharp. **D major is two** (F♯ C♯); **A major is three** (F♯ C♯ G♯). The
reading rule doesn't change — the hand shape does.

## The usual mistake

Forgetting C♯ or G♯ from the signature — playing a natural where three sharps were declared at
the front. In A major especially, the 2nd finger (C♯) sits forward on the A string every time.

<TryThis>
  Play the do–sol–la–ti shape in G, then in D. Sing "do sol la ti" both times — the syllables
  stay put; the letters and the hand both move.
</TryThis>

<Cite source="ABRSM Grade 1–2 keys; Suzuki Book 1 in A major">
  C, G and D come first in every syllabus, then A. They're the keys that sit under the
  first-position hand — which is why the beginner repertoire lives there.
</Cite>
