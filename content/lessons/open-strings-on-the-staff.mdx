You already know these four by their sound — **G, D, A, E**, lowest to highest, the strings
you've been bowing since day one. Now meet them on the page. These four notes are your anchors:
every other note you'll ever read, you find by its distance from one of them.

## Where they sit

Each open string lives in one fixed spot on the staff, and it never moves.

- **G** is low — it hangs below the staff, under a short extra line called a *ledger line*. The
  G string reaches down into that territory, so you'll be reading below the staff from the start.
- **D** sits in the space just under the bottom line.
- **A** sits inside the staff, in the second space up.
- **E** sits high, in the top space.

Notice how far apart **G** and **E** look on the page. That gap is the whole range of your
open strings, and for a while it's the whole range of your reading too.

<TryThis>
  Play the four notes in order off the page — G, D, A, E — then backwards, E, A, D, G. Say each
  letter out loud as you bow it. When that's easy, close your eyes and just listen: can you hear
  which string is which?
</TryThis>

> This is the first sight-reading drill in almost every method book, and it's the one Suzuki
> students spend weeks on. Recognising the open strings on sight — before any fingers get
> involved — is the foundation the rest of your reading is built on.
