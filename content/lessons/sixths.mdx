A wider stretch than a third, and a hand shape most players find needs a little more time to
settle in. The interval itself — sixths, everywhere in real harmony as inverted thirds — is
worth the investment.

## More flexibility, same tuning method

The wiggle-to-lock check from `thirds` applies here too — if anything it matters more, since a
wider stretch makes it easier to land slightly off and not notice until you actually listen for
the beating.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play the minor sixth, then the major sixth, keeping the 1st finger anchored on A the whole
  time and only adjusting the 2nd finger's low/high position. Feel how little that finger has to
  move for such a clear change in colour.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Tensing the whole hand to "reach" a sixth, instead of letting it open. A stretched, gripped
hand can't make small tuning adjustments — and sixths need those adjustments more than thirds
do, simply because there's more room for the interval to drift.

<Cite source="Violinspiration, 'How to Play Double Stops on Violin'; verified against a direct semitone check across the G–D string pair">
  Sixths are consistently described as needing "more hand flexibility" than thirds — not more
  finger strength. The stretch comes from an open hand, not a forced one.
</Cite>
