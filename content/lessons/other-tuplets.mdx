Any bracketed number over a group does the same job as the triplet's **3**: fit *that many*
notes into the space a different number would normally fill. The family is called **tuplets**.

- **duplet** — a **2** in *compound* time: two notes where you'd expect three (one beat split
  in two instead of three)
- **quintuplet** — **5** in a beat
- **sextuplet** — **6** in a beat (usually felt as two groups of three, or three of two)
- **septuplet** — **7** in a beat

You will **read** these in K.216 and in Romantic repertoire. You won't drill them in Stage 2 —
this page is just so the bracketed number never surprises you.

## The usual mistake

Trying to find a neat word for a 5 or a 7. There isn't one that helps. Lock the beat with
your foot or the metronome and let the notes divide it evenly — the bracket has already told
you how many.
