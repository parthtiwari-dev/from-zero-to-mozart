The lowest string, one finger at a time. Put fingers 1, 2 and 3 down in order on the G string
and you get four notes climbing away from the open string: **G, A, B, C** — then the 4th
finger adds **D**. All natural notes, no sharps or flats yet.

| Note | Finger | Where it is |
|---|---|---|
| G | 0 — open | the string itself, not stopped by any finger |
| A | 1 | a whole step above G |
| B | 2, high | right up against the 3rd finger — *not* halfway between 1 and 3 |
| C | 3 | a half step above B |
| D | 4 | the same pitch as your open D string |

These are low notes. On the staff, open **G** hangs below a short ledger line well under the
five lines; **C** is middle C, one ledger line below the staff; **D** sits just under the
bottom line. Don't expect them up among the staff lines — the whole G string reads underneath.

## What to listen for

The **D** you play with the 4th finger should be *exactly* the same pitch as your open D
string. Play the 4th-finger D, then the open D, back and forth. If they don't match, your hand
has drifted up or down the neck — slide it until they lock together. This is your built-in
tuning check for the whole string, and it's worth doing every time you start.

## The usual mistake

The **B** is a *high* 2nd finger. Beginners put the 2nd finger in the middle, halfway between
1 and 3, because that feels even — and it plays B-flat, not B. B lives right next to where the
3rd finger goes, the two fingers almost touching. The gap is between 1 and 2, not 2 and 3.
