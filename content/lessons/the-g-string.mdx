The lowest string, one finger at a time. Put fingers 1, 2 and 3 down in order on the G string
and you get four notes climbing away from the open string: **G, A, B, C** — then the 4th
finger adds **D**. All natural notes, no sharps or flats yet. Learn these five and you can
already play real tunes without ever leaving this one string.

| Note | Finger | Where it is |
|---|---|---|
| G | 0 — open | the string itself, not stopped by any finger |
| A | 1 | a whole step above G |
| B | 2, high | right up against the 3rd finger — *not* halfway between 1 and 3 |
| C | 3 | a half step above B |
| D | 4 | the same pitch as your open D string |

## The ladder

Play straight up the string and back down. Every note is on the G string; the label under
each note tells you the string and the finger.

<Margin>
  Everything on the G string in first position lives *below* the treble staff, on and between
  ledger lines. Open **G** hangs below its own short line; **C** is middle C; **D** sits just
  under the bottom line.
</Margin>

The **D** you play with the 4th finger should be *exactly* the same pitch as your open D
string. Play the 4th-finger D, then the open D, back and forth — if they don't match, your
whole hand has drifted up or down the neck. Slide it until they lock. That's your tuning check
for the entire string.

## Skips

Tunes don't only move by step. Practise jumping straight from the open string to the 2nd
finger and the 4th — G to B to D and back — keeping each finger down as you add the next.

## The usual mistake

The **B** is a *high* 2nd finger. Beginners put the 2nd finger in the middle, halfway between
1 and 3, because that feels even — and it plays B-flat, not B. B lives right next to where the
3rd finger goes, the two fingers almost touching. The gap on this string is between **1 and
2**, not 2 and 3. If Hot Cross Buns or Ode to Joy sounds sour, this is almost always why.

  squeeze the neck — shake it out, start again.
</TryThis>
