A **trill** (**tr**) is a fast alternation between the written note and the note **above** it,
for as long as the written note lasts. It's the most common ornament — and it carries one
genuine question: which note do you start on?

## Reading the symbol

- **tr** sits above the note. A trailing **wavy line** shows how far the trill runs.
- An **accidental above** or just after the `tr` sets the pitch of the *upper* note — `tr` with
  a ♯ means trill up to the sharpened note, not the diatonic one.
- A small curl of two notes at the **end** — down to the lower neighbour and back — is the
  **termination** (or *Nachschlag*). It lands the trill cleanly onto the next note. Sometimes
  it's written out; often it's just expected.

## It depends on the era

| Era | Start the trill on… |
|---|---|
| **Baroque** | the **upper** note — always |
| **Classical** | mixed — in transition through the 1790s; the note, the harmony and the edition all weigh in |
| **Romantic and after** | the **written** note (Hummel's rule) |

A `tr` on a Mozart note is **genuinely ambiguous** — and deciding is a real interpretive
choice. This is the clearest place in the whole stage where "read it" and "play it in the
right style" pull apart.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  On a note you can hold, trill **slowly** first — just the note and its upper neighbour, back
  and forth, dead even. Then let it speed up only as far as it stays even. A slow clean trill
  beats a fast ragged one every time.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Cramming in as many notes as possible — a trill is a **shimmer**, and evenness matters more
than count. Forgetting the termination when the style asks for one. And the big one: playing a
**Baroque** trill from the main note out of modern habit, when it should almost always begin
above.

<Cite source="C.P.E. Bach, Versuch (1753); Leopold Mozart, Violinschule (1756); 'The Trill in the Classical Period 1750–1820'">
  Leopold Mozart: "All short trills are played with a quick appoggiatura [from above] and a
  turn" — that is, an upper-note start and a termination. He drew his trill examples straight
  from Tartini.
</Cite>
