The fastest way to read is to stop reading note *names* and start reading *moves*. Between one
note and the next there are only a few possibilities, and two of them cover most music.

## Step and skip

- A **step** (a 2nd) goes from a line to the space right next to it, or a space to the next
  line — *adjacent*. On the violin, usually the next finger.
- A **skip** (a 3rd) jumps from a line to the next line, or a space to the next space — hopping
  over one position. Usually two fingers, or a string.

## Bigger jumps

A **4th** or a **5th** is a leap you'll often play as one open string to another (open D to
open A is a fifth). You feel these as string crossings.

## The usual mistake

Naming every note out loud and losing the pulse. The shape pulls you through the line; the
names are a check you run *after*, not a gate you pass at every note.

<TryThis>
  Take any simple tune you can already play. Look at its notation and mark each move — S for
  step, K for skip, L for leap — without naming a note. Then play it, watching the shape go by.
</TryThis>

<Cite source="pattern reading — Percival Hodgson, and the Suzuki 'steps and skips'">
  The eye is far better at spotting *this is a step, that is a skip* than at recalling nine
  letter names in a hurry. Good sight-readers read the terrain, not the signposts.
</Cite>
