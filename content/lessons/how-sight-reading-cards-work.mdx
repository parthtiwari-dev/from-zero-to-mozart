The way you actually *build* sight-reading is by reading a large stream of short, easy,
disposable lines — one a day, always new, always a little below the music you're learning to
perform. On this site that stream is the **card ladder** (`sight-reading-cards`). Here's how
it works.

## The ladder — cumulative levels

Each level adds *one* new thing and keeps everything from the levels before it. Roughly (the
first-position span; positions come in Stage 8b):

| Level | Adds |
|---|---|
| 1 | open strings only; 2/4 & 4/4; crotchets, minims, semibreves + rests |
| 2 | one string at a time; quavers in pairs |
| 3 | **C & G major**, whole strings; the low-2 / high-2 contrast |
| 4 | **D & A major; A & E minor**; dotted crotchets; ties over the bar |
| 5 | **F major & D minor** — the first flat, the low-1 hand; single quavers; 3/8 |
| 6 | **6/8** read in two |
| 7 | **a chromatic note** (an accidental outside the key); the fermata; tenuto |
| 8 | **syncopation**; a wider dynamic range; `rit.`; ledger-line range |
| 9 | **triplets**; a slur across a string; a light open-plus-stopped double stop |
| 10 | **B♭ major, E minor harmonic/melodic**; the full first-position chromatic vocabulary |

(11+ — positions, key changes, longer lines — is Stage 8b, after you can shift.)

## The card format

Every card is the same shape:

1. **Pre-read it** — the 9-step scan (`the-pre-read`).
2. **Try it once, through, no stopping** (`the-never-stop-rule`).
3. **Check the pass condition:**
   - a **steady slow pulse**, start to finish;
   - **at least 8 of 10 pitches** correct;
   - **no panic pause longer than one beat.**
4. **Pass?** Move to the next card. **Fail?** Slow the metronome, diagnose with the
   four-layer rescue (`the-four-layer-rescue`), and take a **new card tomorrow** — do **not**
   replay this one.

## Why "don't replay" and "always new"

Replaying a card to get it right is practising a *different* skill — memorising a line — not
reading. Sight-reading only improves when the material is genuinely unseen. So the ladder is
big and the cards are throwaway: you read one, judge it, and never see it again. The habit is
**volume of fresh easy material**, not mastery of any single card.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Go to `sight-reading-cards`. Find the level where a card is *almost* easy — you can get
  through it at a slow click with maybe one wrong note. That's your level. Read one card a
  day. When a whole level goes cleanly for a week, move up one.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Choosing a level that's too hard because it feels more like "real practice." A card you
stumble through is teaching your eyes to stumble. Pick the level that's **almost boring** —
that's where fluency is built. And never replay: a new card every day.
