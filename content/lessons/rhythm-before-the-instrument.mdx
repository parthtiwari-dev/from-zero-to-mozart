You've spent Stage 1 learning *what* a note is — which pitch, which string, which finger. Now
we start on *when*: how long it lasts, and where it sits against a steady pulse. This is
rhythm, and it comes before the instrument on purpose.

Leopold Mozart, teaching his son and every other student since 1756, put it plainly: a young
player should be able to **beat the rhythm correctly, with the quarter notes in mind, before
ever taking the violin in hand**. Clap it, count it, feel it — then play it. If the rhythm is
solid before your hands get involved, your hands have one less thing to get wrong.

This should feel like home ground for you. Tabla training is built entirely on internalising
pulse and subdivision before touching an instrument — bol before dha-dhin-dhin-dha. Western
notation is just a different way of writing down the same idea: **a pulse, divided.**

<TryThis>
  Tap a slow, steady beat on your knee — four taps, evenly spaced, then start again. Don't play
  anything yet. Just get the pulse rock-steady before Stage 2 asks you to divide it up.
