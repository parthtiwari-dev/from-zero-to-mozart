A **musette** is a piece that imitates a small bagpipe — a drone humming underneath a lively
tune. This one, from the notebook Bach kept for his wife Anna Magdalena in 1725, is the
**Foundation‑gate piece**: play its main strain well and you've shown you can do everything
the first half of the course was for — read it, count it, tune it, and bow it with control.

First position, **D major**, **2/4**. Descending scale runs, string crossings, an even
détaché, and a calm held note at the end. Nothing fancy — just everything, at once.

## The middle section — for later

The full Musette has a middle section (a *B* strain) before this one returns. It wanders into
chromatic notes — D♯ and G♯ — and leaps down by octaves, which is very natural on a keyboard
(where the piece was written) and awkward on the violin. It's a **Phase‑2 piece** to play in
full, once you've started shifting. For the gate, the strain above is the test.

## The usual mistake

Playing the fast four-note runs **heavy and scrambled** — digging in with the bow so they lose
their evenness. They're light, small, quick détaché, from the forearm, not the whole arm. The
other mistake: rushing the held A and held D. They're the calm points — full, steady bows.
