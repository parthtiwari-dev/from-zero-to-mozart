Sometimes a sharp, flat or natural turns up in the *middle* of the music, attached to one note.
That's an **accidental** — a local change, not a standing one. It changes that note, and it
keeps changing every note on the same line or space **until the next bar line**. The bar line
wipes it, back to the key signature.

## A natural cancels the key, too

An accidental doesn't only add sharps and flats — a **♮ in a sharp key** takes a note the key
signature had raised and drops it back to natural, for the rest of that bar.

## Scan the bar first

Before you play a bar, look through it. A sharp early in the bar can still be in force on a
later note that has no sign of its own. Miss it and you play a wrong note without knowing why.

The **courtesy accidental** — a ♮ or ♯ in brackets, or just restated at a bar line (like the
♯ on bar 2 above) — is a reminder of where things stand, not a new instruction.

## The usual mistake

Two of them: carrying an accidental *past* the bar line (it's already gone), and forgetting it
applies to the *rest* of the bar (it's still there).

## The reading habit

**Key signature first, then local accidentals, bar by bar.** That order, every time.
