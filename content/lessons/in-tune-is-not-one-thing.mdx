Here's something that surprises most beginners: **"in tune" isn't a single fixed answer.** A
violinist's F♯ in a melody is not quite the same pitch as a piano's F♯, which is not quite the
F♯ inside a chord. And all three are "in tune." This lesson explains why, and it's mostly for
understanding — you won't drill it.

## Three tuning systems

| System | Built from | Best for | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pythagorean** | pure 5ths, stacked | **melody** — leading tones sit **high**, tight to the tonic; expressive lines | the 3rds come out wide and a bit harsh |
| **Just** | pure intervals from the harmonic series (including 3rds and 6ths) | **chords and drones** — maximum ring | you can't modulate freely; it's tied to one key |
| **Equal temperament** | the octave sliced into 12 *identical* half steps | **keyboards** — every key equally usable | every interval except the octave is very slightly impure |

## What this means at the instrument

- Playing a **melody** — especially a leading tone climbing to the tonic — you lean the note a
  little **high**, toward its target. That's Pythagorean, and it sounds expressive.
- Playing a **note inside a chord**, or against a drone — you place the 3rd a touch **lower**
  so the chord locks and rings. That's just intonation.
- The **piano and your tuner** use equal temperament, a compromise that's slightly off pure in
  every direction so that all 12 keys are equally usable.

So a great violinist never sounds *exactly* in tune with a piano — on purpose. They're
bending each note to where the music wants it.

## Why key characters faded

The last lesson mentioned that keys used to have distinct personalities. Equal temperament is
why they mostly don't any more: when D major and D♭ major are tuned with the *exact same* set
of intervals, they can only differ in pitch height, not in character. On the old unevenly-tuned
instruments, each key really did have its own flavour of in-tune-ness.

## The drone bridge

This is home turf if you come from Hindustani music. Indian classical intonation is **drone-
based and just-intonation-like** — every note tuned as a pure interval from Sa, held against
the tanpura, exactly the way a violinist tunes against an open string. (The finer microtonal
framework — *shruti* — is a debated theoretical model; don't try to map it onto Western cents.)

## The usual mistake

Chasing the tuner needle to dead centre on every note and wondering why your playing sounds
stiff against other musicians. The tuner is equal temperament; music mostly isn't. Learn the
5ths and octaves by the ring (they have one clear answer), and let your ear place the 3rds and
leading tones where the context wants them.
