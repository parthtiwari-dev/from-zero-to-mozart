The curly symbol at the start of every line of violin music is the **treble clef**, also called
the **G clef**. It isn't a note you play — it's the key to the whole coordinate system. Without
it, the five lines could mean any five pitches.

The clef curls tightest around the **second line from the bottom**, and that curl names that
line **G** — specifically the G an octave above your open G string (the G that's your third
finger on the D string).

Violin music is almost always in treble clef, so you can take it for granted most of the time.
But make it a habit: when you open a new piece, point at the clef and say "treble clef —
second line is G." It resets your eye.

Violists read a different clef (the alto clef); cellists mostly read bass clef and switch to
treble up high. You'll only ever need this one.
