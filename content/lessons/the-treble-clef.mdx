Every line of violin music opens with the same curly sign: the **treble clef**, or **G clef**.
It's not a note — it's what fixes the grid. Without it, five blank lines could mean any five
pitches.

## It's a letter G

The clef is a stylised capital **G**, and its inner curl wraps the **second line from the
bottom** — pinning that line as the note **G**. Specifically G4, the G that's your 3rd finger
on the D string, an octave above the open G string.

## You count from the anchor

"Every Good Boy Deserves Fudge" and "FACE" get you started, but they're slow — you're reciting
a list. Fluent readers keep a few **fixed landmarks** and read everything else as a step or a
skip away. The clef hands you the first one free: **second line is G.**

## The usual mistake

Losing the anchor. The moment a note lands on a ledger line, "Every Good Boy…" runs out — but
"a skip below the clef's G" still works. Reset your eye at the clef every time you start a line.

<TryThis>
  Open any piece of violin music. Point at the clef, say "treble clef — second line is G", and
  find that G on your violin (3rd finger, D string). Do it with three different pieces.
</TryThis>
