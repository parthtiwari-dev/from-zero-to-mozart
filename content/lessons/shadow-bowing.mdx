Before you play a new line, take it apart and put it back together. Four steps, in order —
each one isolates a different layer, so if something breaks you know exactly what failed.

1. **Clap and count** the rhythm. No violin, no pitches — just the pattern against a steady
   pulse, out loud.
2. **Air-bow it.** Hold the violin and bow, but keep the bow just above the string. Move it
   in the rhythm — down and up, matching the beats — with no fingers down. Now the *bow arm*
   knows the rhythm.
3. **Finger it silently.** Left hand only, no bow. Drop the fingers on the notes, in time, in
   silence. Now the *left hand* knows the notes.
4. **Play it.** Put the two together, slowly.

If step 4 falls apart, you already know whether it's the rhythm (step 1 was shaky), the bow
(step 2), or the fingers (step 3). You fix that one layer instead of replaying the whole line
ten times and hoping.

## The usual mistake

Skipping straight to step 4, playing the whole line, hearing it go wrong, and playing it
again — and again — with no idea which part is failing. Ten sloppy repetitions teach your
hands to play it sloppily. Isolate the layer first.

<TryThis>
  Take any tune you already know from Stage 1. Don't play it. Clap and count one phrase, then
  air-bow it, then finger it silently. You'll almost certainly find one of the three was
  vaguer than you thought.
</TryThis>
