Minor keys come in three related forms. All three share the same key signature — they're
variations on how the top of the scale behaves, not different key signatures.

**Natural minor** — the plain form from the last page: G A B♭ C D E♭ F G.

**Harmonic minor** — raises the 7th degree by a half step, creating a stronger pull back to
the tonic (a proper "leading tone"):

**Melodic minor** — in classical practice, raises **both** the 6th and 7th degrees going up
(G A B♭ C D **E F♯** G), then reverts to the plain natural-minor notes coming back down. Real
repertoire treats this more flexibly than the textbook rule suggests, but the classical version
is the one worth knowing first.

You don't need to memorise all three forms in every key today — REFERENCE for now. Learn the
*concept* (one key signature, three top-end variants), and drill specific minor keys only as
your repertoire actually asks for them.
