Stage 4's `phrases-breathe` taught what a phrase *is* — a musical sentence, four bars or so,
closed by a cadence, taken in one breath. This lesson is the other half: **the bow is how you
shape that sentence.** Bow speed, bow amount, and where you breathe are the tools of phrasing.

## Bow speed is expression

A phrase has a **shape**: it grows toward a high point and settles toward its close. The bow
follows that shape:

- **Toward the peak** — more bow, a little faster, a little more weight. The sound opens up.
- **Toward the cadence** — less bow, easing, tapering. The sound settles.

## A retake is a breath

At the end of a phrase there's a tiny grammatical silence — a breath — before the next one
starts. That silence is often a **retake**: you lift the bow, loop it back toward the frog,
and land ready for the new phrase (`hooked-bowing-and-retakes`). The breath and the bow-reset
happen together.

Don't play *through* a phrase-end as if the music were one long run. Let it arrive, breathe,
then begin again.

## Where the bow-plan serves the phrase

Everything from this movement lines up here:

- **Bow distribution** (`planning-the-bow`) — so you have enough bow for the peak and don't
  crush the cadence.
- **The rule of the down-bow** (`the-rule-of-the-down-bow`) — so the phrase's strong points
  land on strong strokes.
- **Dynamics with the bow** (`dynamics-with-the-bow`) — so the shape is audible.
- **Messa di voce** (`messa-di-voce`) — so a single long note inside the phrase can breathe on
  its own.

## The usual mistake

Playing every note of a phrase with the same length of bow and the same weight — technically
even, musically dead. A phrase has a direction; the bow has to show it. The opposite mistake:
over-shaping, so every note swells and lurches and the line never flows. Shape the *phrase*,
not each note.
