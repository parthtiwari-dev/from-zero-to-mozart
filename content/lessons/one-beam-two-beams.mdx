The number of beams (or flags) tells you how far a beat is being divided.

**One beam** = eighth notes. Two of them fit in one quarter-note beat — count "1 &".

<NotatedExample
  abc={`X:1
M:2/4
L:1/8
K:C clef=treble
DEDE |]`}
  caption="Four eighth notes, one beam, counted '1 & 2 &'."
  defaultBpm={68}
/>

**Two beams** = sixteenth notes. Four of them fit in one beat — a common counting syllable set
is "1 e & a".

<NotatedExample
  abc={`X:1
M:2/4
L:1/16
K:C clef=treble
DEFGDEFG |]`}
  caption="Eight sixteenth notes, two beams, counted '1 e & a 2 e & a' — the same two beats as the eighth-note line above, cut twice as fine."
  defaultBpm={68}
/>

A beam by itself doesn't tell you anything about the **bow** — it's purely a rhythm grouping.
Whether several beamed notes go in one bow or separate bows is a different mark entirely: a
curved slur line, which you'll meet two pages from now.

  Clap four steady beats per second (roughly). Then clap the eighth-note line against it —
  twice as fast. Then the sixteenths — four times as fast. Same pulse underneath the whole time.
</TryThis>
