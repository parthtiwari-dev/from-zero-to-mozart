Knowing that C is a low 2 on the A string tells you *roughly* where to put the finger. It does
not guarantee the note is in tune — the violin has no fret to stop you a millimetre short. So
reading and intonation are separate skills, and you build the second one with three checks.

## The three checks

- **Your ear.** Before you play a note, hear it in your head — its pitch, and whether it's
  higher or lower than the note before. Then land, and compare what you hear to what you
  intended. This is the one that matters most, and it improves fast if you use it.
- **Resonance.** When a stopped note is exactly in tune with an open string an octave or a
  fifth away, that open string **rings on its own** — a faint sympathetic hum. Play a
  3rd-finger D on the A string; when it's dead in tune, the open D string sings. Same for
  3rd-finger A on the D string against the open A, and so on. It's a built-in tuner.
- **An outside check.** A tuner, a drone, or a teacher's ear. Useful, but it should confirm
  your ear, not replace it.

## Two rules

Play the note **cleanly, without vibrato**, while you're still learning where it lives —
vibrato smears the pitch and hides small errors from your own ear.

And don't fish. Aim for the note, land, then judge. Sliding around hunting for it trains your
hand to be vague.
