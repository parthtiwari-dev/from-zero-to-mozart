You're going to spend years with this object. Before you play a note, learn what you're
holding and what the parts are called — so every instruction after this one lands somewhere
real instead of washing over you.

## The violin

<ViolinDiagram />

Hold yours next to the drawing and find each part as you read.

Start at the top. The carved curl is the **scroll** — decoration, and a handle. Four **pegs**
pass through the **pegbox**; you turn these to tune, and they hold by friction alone, which is
why they feel stiff at first. The strings leave the pegbox over the **nut** — a small notched
ridge that fixes where each string starts — and run down the **neck** along the black
**fingerboard**, the surface your left-hand fingers press the string against. **There are no
frets.** Nothing marks where the notes are; your ear and your hand learn that.

Lower down, the strings cross the **bridge**: a thin, curved, *unglued* piece of maple standing
up under string tension, carrying the strings' vibration down into the body. The two f-shaped
holes — the **f-holes** — let the sound out. Directly under the treble-side foot of the bridge,
hidden inside, a small wooden dowel called the **sound post** wedges the front and back plates
apart. You will never see it, and it changes the tone more than almost anything you can. At the
bottom the strings anchor to the **tailpiece**, which usually carries four **fine tuners** —
little screws for adjustments too small for the pegs.

## The bow

<BowDiagram />

The end near your hand is the **frog**; the far end is the **tip**, or point. The **screw** at
the frog end tightens the ribbon of **hair** — 150 to 200 strands of horsehair. Tighten it
before you play, to about a pencil's width of gap between hair and stick at the middle, and
**loosen it all the way every time you finish**.

<Margin>
  Loosen the bow every single time. Left tight, the stick slowly loses its spring — and a
  rehair or a new stick is not cheap.
</Margin>

The hair needs **rosin** — dried tree sap — to grip the string; fresh hair makes almost no
sound until it's rosined. Never touch the hair with your fingers: the oil from your skin kills
the grip in a stripe that takes weeks to wear off.

## Do this now

Name every part out loud, touching it: scroll, pegs, nut, fingerboard, bridge, f-holes,
tailpiece, fine tuners — then frog, tip, screw, hair. Tighten the bow to a pencil's width,
then loosen it again. That last habit is the one worth building from day one.
