Before you play a note, it helps to know what you're holding and what the words mean, so
that later instructions land somewhere.

## The violin

Start at the top. The curl is the **scroll**. Below it, four **pegs** stick out of the
**pegbox** — you turn these to tune. The strings pass over the **nut**, a small ridged strip,
and run down the **neck**, along the black **fingerboard**, which is where your left-hand
fingers press.

The strings cross the **bridge** — a thin curved piece of maple that stands on the belly under
string tension and carries their vibration into the body. The two f-shaped holes let that sound
out. Inside, hidden, a small dowel called the **sound post** props the two plates apart and
matters more to the tone than almost anything you can see.

At the bottom the strings anchor to the **tailpiece**, which usually carries four little
**fine tuners** for small adjustments.

## The bow

The end near your hand is the **frog**; the far end is the **tip** or point. The **screw** at
the frog end tightens the ribbon of **hair** (about 150 horsehairs). Tighten it before you
play — roughly a pencil's width of gap at the middle — and **loosen it every time you finish**,
or the stick warps.

The hair needs **rosin** to grip the string. Don't touch the hair with your fingers; the oil
from your skin stops it working.
