You're going to spend years with this object. Before you play a note, learn what you're
holding and what the parts are called — so that every instruction after this one lands
somewhere real instead of washing over you.

## The violin, top to bottom

Hold yours while you read this and find each part as it's named.

Start at the top. The carved curl is the **scroll** — decoration, and a handle. Below it, four
**pegs** pass through the **pegbox**; you turn these to tune, and they hold by friction alone,
which is why tuning with them feels stiff at first. The strings leave the pegbox over the
**nut**, a small notched ridge that sets where each string starts, and run down the **neck**
along the black **fingerboard** — the surface your left-hand fingers press the string against.
There are no frets. Nothing marks where the notes are. Your ear and your hand learn that.

Lower down, the strings cross the **bridge**: a thin, curved, unglued piece of maple standing
up under string tension. It carries the strings' vibration down into the body. The two
f-shaped holes — the **f-holes** — let that sound out. Directly under the treble-side foot of
the bridge, hidden inside, a small wooden dowel called the **sound post** wedges the front and
back plates apart. You will never see it, and it changes the tone more than almost anything you
can.

At the bottom, the strings anchor to the **tailpiece**, which usually carries four **fine
tuners** — little screws for the small adjustments the pegs are too coarse to make.

## The bow

The end near your hand is the **frog**; the far end is the **tip** or point. The **screw** you
turn at the frog end tightens the ribbon of **hair** — roughly 150 horsehairs. Tighten it
before you play, to about a pencil's width of gap between hair and stick at the middle, and
**loosen it all the way every time you finish**.

<Margin>
  Loosen the bow every single time. Left tight, the stick slowly loses its camber and won't
  spring — and a rehair or a new stick is not cheap.
</Margin>

The hair needs **rosin** — dried tree sap — to grip the string; fresh hair makes almost no
sound until it's rosined. Never touch the hair with your fingers: the oil from your skin kills
the grip in a stripe that takes weeks to wear off.

## Do this now

Name every part out loud, touching it: scroll, pegs, nut, fingerboard, bridge, f-holes,
tailpiece, fine tuners — then frog, tip, screw, hair. Tighten the bow to a pencil's width,
then loosen it again. That last habit is the one worth building from day one.
