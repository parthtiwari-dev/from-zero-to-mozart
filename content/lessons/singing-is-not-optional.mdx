You audiate a pitch — but how do you know your inner hearing is *right*? You can't check a
thought against itself. You need to put it into the air where your ear can judge it. The
tool for that is your **voice**.

## The voice is the test instrument

Singing a note is the most direct way to externalise what you're audiating — no fingering,
no bow, nothing between the thought and the sound. If you can sing a phrase in tune, you're
truly hearing it. If you can't sing it, you don't have it yet, and no amount of hunting on
the violin will fix that — you'd just be guessing.

This is why every serious ear-training tradition — solfège classes, Kodály, sargam
practice — is built on singing, not on an instrument. The instrument comes *after* the voice
has the tune.

## "But I can't sing"

Almost everyone who says this has an **untrained ear**, not a broken voice. The voice
wanders because the ear isn't yet giving it a clear target — and that is exactly the thing
this stage fixes. A few things that help:

- **Hum, quietly, on your own.** Nobody is grading the tone. You're checking pitch, not
  auditioning.
- **Sing in whatever octave is comfortable.** Most violin notes sit above a typical speaking
  voice; drop them an octave. The **pitch class** is what matters — a "G" sung low is still
  ^1 in G.
- **Match, don't launch.** Play a note, let it ring, then slide your voice onto it until the
  beating stops (the same merge you learned in `tuning-by-ear`). Matching a sounding pitch is
  far easier than producing one cold, and it trains the same thing.

## Sing → play → sing along

The loop for every drill in this stage:

1. **Sing** the note or phrase (from audiation, or matched to a drone).
2. **Play** it on the violin.
3. **Sing along with the string** — voice and violin together — and hear whether they lock or
   beat.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Set a G drone. Play open D (that's ^5). Sing it — match it until it locks. Stop the violin;

## The usual mistake

Skipping the singing because it feels silly or exposing, and going straight to hunting on the
violin. That turns ear training into a guessing game with 12 options per note. Sing first —
even a rough hum — and the hunt becomes a confirmation. The other mistake: straining for a
"nice" voice; you only need the pitch.
