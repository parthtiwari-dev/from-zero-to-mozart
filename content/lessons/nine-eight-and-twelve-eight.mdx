Once 6/8 makes sense, the rest of the compound family is just "the same idea, more beats."

- **9/8** — three dotted-quarter beats. A "slip jig" in Irish music; the slow movement of a
  Bach sonata. Count "**1** 2 3 **4** 5 6 **7** 8 9".
- **12/8** — four dotted-quarter beats. The engine of a blues shuffle and a doo-wop ballad;
  *We Three Kings*. Count in four big beats.

Divide the top number by 3 to get the real beat count: 9 ÷ 3 = **3**, 12 ÷ 3 = **4**.

## The odd cousin: 3/8

Not every x/8 is compound. **3/8** has just three eighth notes — one beat, felt as three
quick divisions. It's *simple* triple, the little sibling of 3/4, not a compound metre at
all. The test still works: 3 doesn't divide by 3 into a sensible beat count, so it's simple.

## The usual mistake

Assuming any time signature with 8 on the bottom is compound. 3/8 isn't. And counting 9/8 or
12/8 as nine or twelve beats — it's three and four, with everything else as division.
