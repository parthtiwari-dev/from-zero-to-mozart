The gym drills use random notes. Real melodies aren't random — they lean on ^1, ^3 and ^5,
step between them, and use the tendency tones to pull. This lesson takes the degree-naming
skill out of the drill and points it at **tunes you already know**.

## Name a tune in numbers

Pick a tune you can sing. Set its key with a drone. Go phrase by phrase, singing each note
and naming its degree. You'll find most tunes are simpler than they sound — mostly steps,
mostly stable tones, with the odd expressive leap.

## A few openings to know cold

| Tune | First notes, in degrees |
|---|---|
| **Hot Cross Buns** | ^3 ^2 ^1 |
| **Mary Had a Little Lamb** | ^3 ^2 ^1 ^2 ^3 ^3 ^3 |
| **Twinkle** | ^1 ^1 ^5 ^5 ^6 ^6 ^5 |
| **London Bridge** | ^5 ^6 ^5 ^4 ^3 ^4 ^5 |
| **Ode to Joy** | ^3 ^3 ^4 ^5 · ^5 ^4 ^3 ^2 · ^1 ^1 ^2 ^3 · ^3 ^2 ^2 |
| **Amazing Grace** | ^5 ^1 ^3 ^1 · ^3 ^2 ^1 (the pickup is ^5, below home) |

Once you can hear a known tune as numbers, the step to an *unknown* tune is small — it's the
same seven degrees, you just don't have the answer key.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take Ode to Joy (you've played it). Drone on G. Sing the first line and name every degree.
  Check against the table. Then do the second line by ear alone — no table. Then a tune
  that's *not* in the table: hum it, find its key, and number the first phrase. That last one
  is a real by-ear transcription, in miniature.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Jumping straight to unknown tunes before the known ones are automatic. Known tunes are the
bridge — they let you practise the numbering with a built-in answer key, so you find out
immediately when you're wrong. Skip them and every attempt is a shot in the dark. The other
mistake: numbering without a drone, so the key drifts and ^1 stops meaning anything.
