Everything the violin asks of your arms gets harder if the rest of you is braced. So the first
real technique is just standing well — balanced, tall, and unlocked.

## Standing

- **Feet** about shoulder-width apart, weight even between them — or a touch more on the left,
  since the violin sits out to your left. Turn slightly so the left foot is a little forward.
- **Knees** soft. Never locked.
- **Spine** tall — imagine the top of your head floating up — but **shoulders down and level**,
  not hunched and not hiked toward your ears.
- **Head** balanced over your spine, not craned forward toward the chin rest.
- **Breathing** easy and continuous. Holding your breath is the first sign you've tensed up.

## Sitting

Sit toward the **front** of the chair, both feet flat, back not touching the chair. You want
exactly the same tall, loose posture as standing — a chair is a place to rest your legs, not
your spine.

## Rest position

Between attempts, bring the violin **down to your side.** Don't hold it up under your chin for
a whole session — that's how the neck and shoulder learn to clamp.

## The usual mistake

Tension, and it always shows in the same places. Check them often:

- **knees** — locked straight? Soften them.
- **left shoulder** — crept up to meet the violin? Drop it, and let the setup (next lessons)
  bring the violin to you instead.
- **jaw** — clamping the chin rest? Let it just rest.
- **breath** — held? Let it go.

A hunched, braced player has a pinched, effortful sound. An easy body is the pre-condition for
an easy sound.

<TryThis>
  Stand in playing position with the violin up but the bow down. Breathe slowly, five times.
  On each breath out, let one more thing go — jaw, then shoulders, then the small of your
  back. Then bring the violin down to rest position and shake your arms out.
</TryThis>
