Everything the violin asks of your arms gets harder if the rest of you is braced. So the first
technique is just standing well.

## Standing

Feet about shoulder-width apart, weight even between them, knees unlocked. Stand tall — imagine
the top of your head lifting — but let your shoulders hang. Your chest is open, your breathing
is easy. Turn slightly so your left foot points a little more forward than your right; the
violin will sit out to your left.

## Sitting

Sit toward the front of the chair, both feet flat on the floor, back not touching the
chair. You want the same tall, loose posture as standing — a chair is a place to rest your
legs, not your spine.

## The tension check

Leopold Mozart spends the opening of his 1756 method on posture and ease, and every teacher
since has said the same thing: **tension is the enemy**. Three places to check, often:

- your **jaw** — is it clamping the chin rest? Let it just rest.
- your **shoulders** — have they crept up toward your ears? Drop them.
- your **bow hand** — are the knuckles white? Loosen the grip.

<TryThis>
  Stand in playing position with the violin up but the bow down. Breathe slowly five times. Each
  breath out, let one more thing go — jaw, then shoulders, then the small of your back.
</TryThis>
