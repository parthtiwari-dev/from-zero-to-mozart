You met the slur in Stage 2 — a curved line over moving notes, "join them." On the violin
that instruction is **literal and specific**: play the whole group **in one bow**, one
unbroken stroke, no change of direction until the slur ends.

> **Read now, perform later.** Reading the slur — knowing it's one bow, and where the bow
> turns — is this lesson. Making a smooth, even slurred stroke is **Stage 5** (*Legato and
> slurred bowing*).

## The slurring is also the bowing

A slur over two notes = two notes on one bow. Over four = four on one bow. **Where the slur
ends, the bow changes.** So on a string part the curves are a map of the bow — you can read
the down / up pattern straight off them, starting (by the old rule) with a down-bow on the
first beat of the bar.

<Margin>
  **The Classical barline rule.** In Haydn and Mozart, slurs almost always **stop at the
  barline** — a curve that looks like it crosses is usually two slurs, and the downbeat takes
  a fresh bow. And under any slur, the **first note leans** slightly — a touch longer and
  stronger — while the rest taper away "without the slightest accent."
</Margin>

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play D–E–F♯–G on the D string, **two notes per bow**: a down-bow for D–E, an up-bow for
  F♯–G. Now play all four in **one** down-bow — divide the stroke into four equal quarters so
  the last note has as much bow as the first. That planning is what a long slur asks of you.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Re-bowing inside a slur — a little push for each note — which breaks the legato the slur was
asking for. Running out of bow halfway through a long slur because you spent it all on the
first two notes. And reading a **phrase mark** as one enormous slur.

<Cite source="Clive Brown, Classical and Romantic Performing Practice 1750–1900 (OUP, 1999); Neue Mozart-Ausgabe editorial notes">
  Classical slurring is metrical — bar by bar, the phrase built from short bow-groups. The
  long, singing phrase-slur is a later, Romantic way of marking music; applied to Mozart it
  changes the articulation into something he didn't write.
</Cite>
