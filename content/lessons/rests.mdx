Silence is written down too, and it has an exact length. Rests use the same value system as
notes — the same halving tree — just with no pitch to play.

| Rest | Length in 4/4 | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| whole rest | 4 beats | a small bar hanging *below* a staff line |
| half rest | 2 beats | a small bar sitting *on top of* a staff line |
| quarter rest | 1 beat | a squiggle |
| eighth rest | ½ beat | a single flag |
| sixteenth rest | ¼ beat | a double flag |

(The whole and half rests are the two people mix up: the whole rest **hangs down**, the half
rest **sits up**. "Whole is heavier, so it hangs.")

## A rest is not a break

Your job during a rest is *active*: keep the count running, and get ready for whatever comes
next — lift or reset the bow, move a finger to its next note, take a breath for the phrase.
A tabla player never loses the cycle in a gap; a rest is the same — the pulse carries straight
through it.

## The usual mistake

Treating a rest as "nothing to read" — rushing off it, or letting the count go fuzzy, so the
next note lands early or late. Count a rest as deliberately as you count a note.

<Cite source="the original PDF course, §28">
  "A rest is not 'nothing to read.' You must keep the pulse and prepare the next bow or
  finger during the silence." The rest is where good players do their setting-up.
</Cite>
