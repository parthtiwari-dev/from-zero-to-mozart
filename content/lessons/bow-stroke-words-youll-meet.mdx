A part might print any of these words above a passage. Each names a **way of moving the bow** —
a bow stroke. This is a reference card: recognise the word, know roughly the sound, and know
that **learning to do it is Stage 5's job** (*The bow*). Reading it is enough for now.

## The card

| Word | Literally | The sound | Learn it in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **détaché** | "detached" | smooth, separate strokes, one per note, even and connected-feeling | Stage 5 |
| **legato** | "bound" | notes joined under one bow, no re-articulation | Stage 5 |
| **martelé** | "hammered" | a sharp bitten attack, a stop, then a firm release — crisp and strong | Stage 5 |
| **staccato** | "detached" | short notes with air between (on one bow: a row of clicks) | Stage 5 |
| **portato** / **louré** | "carried" | gently pulsed notes in one bow, a small swell on each | Stage 5 |
| **spiccato** | "separated" | a controlled off-string bounce — short and springy | Stage 5 |
| **sautillé** | "skipping" | a fast, light natural bounce in the middle of the bow | Stage 5 |
| **ricochet** / **jeté** | "thrown" | the bow dropped so it bounces several notes on one stroke | Stage 5 (reference) |
| **collé** | "glued" | a pinched note caught from the string and released | Stage 5 |
| **bariolage** | "multi-coloured" | rocking quickly across strings, often with one open string ringing through | Stage 5 / Stage 6 |
| **col legno** | "with the wood" | struck (*battuto*) or drawn (*tratto*) with the **stick**, not the hair — thin and brittle | reference only |
