Every time signature answers one question first: **does the beat split in two, or in three?**

- Split in **two** → **simple** time. 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 — each beat is a quarter note, and a
  quarter divides into two eighths. Count "1 **and** 2 **and**".
- Split in **three** → **compound** time. 6/8, 9/8, 12/8 — each beat is a *dotted* quarter
  note, and a dotted quarter divides into three eighths. Count "1‑2‑3, 1‑2‑3" or "1‑&‑a".

That single split — two versus three — changes how a tune *feels* more than almost anything
else.

## Reading the bottom number differently

In **simple** time the bottom number *is* the beat: 3/4 means three quarter-note beats. In
**compound** time the bottom number is the *division*, not the beat: 6/8 doesn't mean six
beats — it means six eighth-note *divisions*, which group into **two** dotted-quarter beats.
Divide the top number by 3 to get the real beat count: 6 ÷ 3 = 2, 9 ÷ 3 = 3, 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

## The usual mistake

Counting 6/8 as six beats. It's **two**, each worth three eighths. Six-beat counting turns a
lilting jig into a plod. When you see any x/8, check whether the top number divides by 3 —
if it does, it's compound, and the real beat is the dotted note.
