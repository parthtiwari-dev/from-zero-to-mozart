Subdivision is feeling the **smaller** notes even when you're not playing them. On a held
half note, "2 &" is still ticking inside your head. Just before a run of sixteenths, you're
already feeling "e & a". The beat stops being a single point and becomes a little grid you
drop the notes onto.

This is the single most useful anti-rushing habit there is.

## The usual mistake

Rushing the instant the notes get shorter — a fast run, a string of sixteenths — because the
pulse was being felt in big whole beats with nothing inside them. And the mirror image:
**clipping** a long note short because nothing was counted through it. Subdivide, and both
problems go away.

<Cite source="Essential Elements for Strings; Simon Fischer, Basics">
  Essential Elements drills subdivision "from page one"; Fischer's fix for a rushed passage is
  almost always to subdivide the beat before it. "Rushing when the notes get short" is the
  most-cited beginner rhythm fault.
</Cite>
