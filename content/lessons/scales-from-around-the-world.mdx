A reference card. Beyond the major, minor, modal, pentatonic and symmetrical scales, there is a
long tail of named scales — from other traditions, from jazz, from Romantic and 20th-century
composers reaching for a particular colour. You don't drill these. **Recognise the name, know
roughly the sound, move on.**

## The main ones you'll see named

| Scale | Roughly | Sound | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phrygian dominant** (Freygish) | Phrygian with a **major 3rd** (♭2 ♮3) | Spanish, Middle-Eastern, klezmer | flamenco; klezmer; "Hava Nagila" |
| **Harmonic major** | major with a **♭6** | major, but with a shadow | Romantic colour |
| **Double harmonic** (Byzantine / Arabic) | ♭2, ♮3, ♭6, ♮7 — *two* augmented 2nds | very "exotic", ornate | Arabic maqām Hijaz-Kar; Turkish music |
| **Hungarian / Gypsy minor** | harmonic minor with a **♯4** as well | dark, dramatic, ornamental | Liszt; Roma music |
| **Acoustic** (Lydian dominant) | major with **♯4 and ♭7** | bright but unresolved | jazz; Bartók; film |
| **Bebop scales** | a major/dominant scale with **one chromatic passing note** added (8 notes) | keeps the arpeggio landing on the beat | jazz improvisation |
| **Neapolitan minor / major** | minor with a **♭2** | operatic, tragic | 18th-c. Naples; Beethoven |
| **Enigmatic** | Verdi's invented scale (♭2, ♯4, ♯5, ♯6, ♮7) | deliberately strange | Verdi's *Ave Maria* |

## From other traditions

- **Japanese pentatonics** — *Hirajoshi*, *In*, *Yo*, *Iwato*: five-note scales, several with a
  half step right next to the tonic, giving that spare, still, koto-and-shakuhachi sound. The
  tune *Sakura* (in the practice bank) is one of these.
- **Indonesian gamelan** — *sléndro* (roughly five near-equal steps) and *pélog* (seven
  uneven steps): tuned by ear per ensemble, not to any Western standard.
- **The pitch material of Indian ragas** — a raga isn't a scale (see *A raga is not a scale*),
  but the *notes* it draws on can be laid out: raga Bhairav's material has a ♭2 and ♭6; Yaman's
  is Lydian-flavoured (♯4); Todi's is very chromatic. Compare the **pitch set**, never equate
  the raga to the scale.

## The framing

**There is no finite list of "all scales."** A scale is just an agreed-on set of pitches with a
home — cultures and composers make new ones whenever they want a colour the existing ones
don't have. This course teaches the Western tonal spine in depth and gives you enough of a
map to recognise the rest.
