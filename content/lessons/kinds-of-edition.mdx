The same piece of music comes in different **editions**, and they are not interchangeable.
This is a reference card — know which kind you're holding, and what that means for how much to
trust the page.

## The four kinds

| Kind | What it is | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **Facsimile** | a photograph of the composer's manuscript or the first print | scholars |
| **Urtext** | the composer's text, rebuilt from all the reliable sources, with any editorial additions clearly flagged | players who want to decide for themselves |
| **Performing / "artist" edition** | the text **plus** a named soloist's fingerings, bowings, dynamics and phrasing | learners; following a particular school (Flesch, Auer, Joachim, Oistrakh) |
| **Critical edition** | Urtext **plus** a full report of every source and every editorial decision | the reference standard — e.g. the *Neue Mozart-Ausgabe* |

## What "Urtext" really means — and doesn't

- German for "original text." The aim: the music **as the composer wrote it**, free of an
  editor's fingerings and interpretation.
- **Urtext is not the manuscript.** The autograph is usually the best source but not always
  the last word — it can be lost, unfinished, or replaced by a corrected first edition. The
  editor **weighs all the sources** to decide the most reliable reading.
- **Urtext is not infallible.** Where the sources disagree, the editor makes a call — and two
  Urtext publishers can reach different answers for the same bar.

## K.216 specifically

The reference text is the **Neue Mozart-Ausgabe**. The concerto's **cadenzas and lead-ins are
not by Mozart** — the common ones are by Joachim, Auer, Franko and others, printed in a
supplement. Which cadenza you play is a choice, and the page should say whose it is.
