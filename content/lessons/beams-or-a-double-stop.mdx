Here's the exact confusion that started this whole reading project: two notes drawn close
together, connected by a line. Are they two quick notes in a row, or two notes played at once?

The rule is about **direction**, not distance.

<NotatedExample
  abc={`X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C clef=treble
D/2E/2 z2 z | [DA]2 z2 |]`}
  caption="Left: two eighth notes joined by a horizontal beam — play them one after another. Right: two noteheads stacked on top of each other — play them together. That's a double stop."
  defaultBpm={60}
/>

- **Side by side, joined by a beam** → sequential. Play one, then the other, fast.
- **Stacked vertically at the same point in time** → simultaneous. Play both together — on the
  violin, that means two strings at once, a **double stop**.

One more thing this is *not*: **vibrato**. Vibrato is a wobble in pitch on a single held note —
it never adds extra noteheads to the page. If you see extra notes, it's rhythm or a double
stop; if you hear a wobble with no new notehead, it's vibrato (something to add much later,
in Stage 7).
