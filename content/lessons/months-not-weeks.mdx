Here is a sequence you can follow to build a vibrato — and here is the honest truth about how
long it takes. The two belong together, because the sequence only works if you're not
expecting it to finish on schedule.

## The sequence

Adapted from the source course's six-week plan. Treat each row as "when this feels loose and
even, move on" — not "spend exactly one week here."

| Stage | What you're doing |
|---|---|
| 1 | Slow arm (or wrist) motion, off the string then on it — big, exaggerated, control not speed |
| 2 | The same, on the other motion type, and on other fingers |
| 3 | The pulse ladder: 2, then 3, then 4 rocks per beat at 60 — all strong fingers, no speed race (this is ~2–4 Hz, still a practice speed) |
| 4 | Sustained notes: start and stop the vibrato deliberately |
| 5 | Add it to the long notes of an easy melody, keeping the pitch centre |
| 6 | Vary the width and speed a little for character |

## The truth about the timeline

The source book says it plainly, and it's worth quoting: *"Vibrato development often takes
months, not six weeks; this is a sequence, not a guarantee. If tension or pitch-center loss
increases, go backward."*

Going **backward** is not failure — it's the method. If your vibrato starts sounding tight, or
your intonation starts slipping, drop down a stage or two, rebuild the looseness, and come back
up. Most players cycle up and down this ladder for the better part of a year before the vibrato
is genuinely theirs.

## How to practise it

**Short sessions.** Five to ten minutes at a time. Vibrato practice fatigues the hand fast, and
a tired hand practises the *wrong* motion — it tightens to compensate. Better four two-minute
goes across a day than one twenty-minute grind.

<TryThis>
  Pick your current stage from the table. Do five minutes on it — no more. If the hand tightens
  before the five minutes are up, stop early. Tomorrow, start with one minute of the pulse
  (Movement B) to warm up, then five minutes on your stage again. That's the whole practice
  plan; the only variable is patience.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Treating week six as a finish line and getting discouraged when the vibrato isn't there. It
won't be — and that's normal, not a sign you're doing it wrong. Hilary Hahn's "100 days of
practice" idea is the right frame: it's a long, light, repeated process, not a sprint. The
other mistake is the opposite — hour-long vibrato sessions that just carve tension deeper.
