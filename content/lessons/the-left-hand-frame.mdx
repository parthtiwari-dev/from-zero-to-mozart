The left hand has one job at this stage: hold a loose, consistent shape and drop fingers onto
the string from above. That shape is the **frame**.

## Where the hand goes

The neck rests in the soft web between your thumb and first finger — lightly. The **thumb**
sits on the side of the neck, roughly opposite your first or second finger, and stays gently
curved or straight, never pressing hard. Your **palm does not touch the neck**; there's a gap.

The fingers arch **over** the string and come down onto it from the knuckles, like small
hammers, landing on their tips. Keep them curved and hovering close even when they're not
playing — a finger that flies away has to travel back.

## The squeeze test

A relaxed hand can lift and drop fingers quickly and land in tune. A gripping hand can't do
either. Two checks:

- With a finger pressed down, can you **wiggle your thumb**? You should be able to.
- Can you tap the other fingers lightly on the string while one is playing? If they're rigid,
  you're clamping.

Klara Berkovich, Hilary Hahn's first serious teacher, drilled a light, organised left hand
from the very start — it's the thing that lets speed and vibrato happen later. Build it slow now.
