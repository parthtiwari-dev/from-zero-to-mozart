A **half step** (semitone) is the smallest ordinary step in this system — on the violin, your
neighbouring low/high finger positions are a half step apart. Two half steps make a **whole
step**. That's the entire vocabulary you need for what's next.

Every major scale is built from the exact same recipe of whole (W) and half (H) steps, starting
from the tonic:

**W – W – H – W – W – W – H**

| Degree | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Step to next | W | W | H | W | W | W | H | — |

Apply that recipe starting on **any** note and you get *that* note's major scale — which is
exactly how the next page derives G major from scratch instead of asking you to memorise it.
The two half-steps in the pattern (between degrees 3–4 and 7–8) are the "glue" points — they're
why a major scale sounds like it's pulling toward its tonic rather than just wandering.
