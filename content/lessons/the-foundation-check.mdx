This is the end of the foundation half of the course — and the first of the two hard gates.
You've covered setup, first-position reading, rhythm, the score's whole vocabulary,
musicianship, and the bow. Before the road turns toward shifting, vibrato and real repertoire,
it's worth checking the ground is solid.

This is a **self-audit, not a test.** Nothing here unlocks or locks. Read it honestly, and if
a few things feel shaky, circle back — the next half of the course is built directly on this
ground.

## The six things the Foundation gate asks

**1 · Read and play any first-position tune in G, D, A or C at sight.** Every note named, its
string and finger found, the rhythm counted, the key signature understood — slowly, without
panic.

**2 · Keep steady time.** Clap and count a line of quarters, eighths, dots and rests against a
pulse before you play it, and hold that pulse through the piece. (This was your strength coming
in; the gate just confirms it survived adding the bow.)

**3 · Produce an even, controlled tone with the core strokes.** A sustained *son filé* frog to
tip. An even *détaché*. A connected *legato* slur. A *martelé* with a clean prepared bite. A
*string crossing* with no bump. On open strings and simple scales — not polished, but
reliable.

**4 · Hear when a note is out of tune, and fix it.** Against an open string, against the
drone, against a ringing tone — you notice the note is high or low, and you move it.

**5 · Hear a simple four-bar tune in a known key and play it back — unaided.** No notation.
You find home, work out the pitches by ear (`finding-a-tune-by-ear`), correct your guesses,
and get there. Slowly is fine. *This is the goal the whole course points at.*

**6 · Read a printed bowing.** Direction signs, where-in-the-bow marks (`Sp.`, `au talon`,
`WB`), slur groups, an `f`–`p` echo that needs a bow change, the difference between the
composer's marks and the editor's.

## Where this leaves you

If most of that felt solid, you've reached the **Foundation gate**. You can read a
first-position piece, play it honestly with a real bow, hear when you're out and fix it, and —
the thing you came here for — hear a tune and find it yourself. That's a genuine musician's
starting kit.

The intermediate half (Stage 6 onward) begins here: shifting into the higher positions,
vibrato, sight-reading as a trained skill, and then the giants — Bach, Vivaldi, and Mozart's
Third Concerto. None of it is a leap from here; it's all built on exactly this ground.

If a few areas felt shaky, there's no rush. Spend a week or two circling back through those
stages. The foundation is worth getting right.
