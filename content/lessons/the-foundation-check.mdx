This is the first of the two gates. You've covered the whole foundation half of the course:
setup, first-position reading, rhythm, score symbols, the basics of theory, and the core bow
strokes. Before the road turns toward shifting, vibrato and real repertoire, it's worth
checking the ground is solid.

## Where you should be

- **Setup** — you can hold the violin with your head, keep a loose left hand and a relaxed bow
  hold, and tune the A string to a reference.
- **Reading** — you can name any natural note in first position on sight, work out its string
  and finger, and read a simple line slowly without panicking. Sharps, flats, key signatures
  and accidentals make sense.
- **Rhythm** — you can clap and count a line of quarters, eighths, dots and rests against a
  steady pulse before you play it.
- **Symbols** — a page of music no longer looks like random dots and Italian words; you can
  categorise most marks.
- **Theory** — you understand why G major has one sharp, what major vs. minor means, and which
  scales are actually worth practising now.
- **Bow** — you have an even long tone, a relaxed détaché, and a slur, on open strings and
  simple scales.

None of this needs to be polished. It needs to be *reliable and slow*.

If most of that felt solid, you've reached the **Foundation gate** — the intermediate half
(Stage 6 onward) can begin. If a few areas felt shaky, spend a week or two circling back
through those stages. There's no rush, and the ground you build the next half on is exactly
this ground.
