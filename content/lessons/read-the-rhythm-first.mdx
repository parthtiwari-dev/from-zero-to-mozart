When a sight-read falls apart, it's almost always the **rhythm** that goes first — not the
pitches. A player with the right notes and a wobbly beat sounds worse, and gets more lost,
than one who fluffs a note but holds the pulse dead steady. So the method is: **read the
rhythm before you read the pitches.**

## Clap – tap – play

Before you play an unseen line:

1. **Clap the rhythm** — just the durations, no pitch. Say the counts as you go.
2. **Tap the beat** on your leg with one hand while you clap the rhythm with the other (or
   say it). Now the rhythm is *against* a steady pulse.
3. **Play the notes**, keeping that same beat going.

## Subdivide

Count in the **smallest note value that appears**. If there are sixteenths anywhere, count
"1 e & a" all the way through — then every longer note is measured against a grid you're
already feeling. This is Stage 2's `subdivision`, and it's what stops a dotted rhythm or a
syncopation from throwing you.

## The rhythm skeleton — a live rescue

If the pitches are collapsing mid-line, **drop to just the rhythm on one note** (or the open
strings), keep the pulse going, and add the pitches back in when you can. Better a bar of the
right rhythm on the wrong notes than a stop.

## The half you can already do

Here's the thing worth saying plainly: **rhythm is the side of sight-reading you came in good
at.** A tabla player feels a whole cycle as one shape — sixteen beats of Teentaal as a single
sentence, not sixteen separate counts. That's *exactly* the skill fluent readers use: catching
a whole bar's rhythm as one figure instead of adding up durations. Complex subdivisions,
tuplets, syncopation — things you can already *play* — the notation is just a diagram of them.

So when you read: **don't spell out the rhythm note by note.** Catch the *cell* — "long,
short-short," "the dotted pair," "the triplet," "the push onto the &" — the way you'd catch a
bol pattern.

## The usual mistake

Reading pitch and rhythm at the same time, cold — trying to figure out *what* note and *how
long* in one go, and doing neither well. Separate them: rhythm first, on a clap; pitches
second. The other mistake: adding up a rhythm note-by-note ("eighth plus eighth plus
sixteenth plus sixteenth…") instead of recognising the whole figure at a glance.
