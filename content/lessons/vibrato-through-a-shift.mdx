Players talk about "continuous vibrato" — a shimmer that never stops. It's a slight fiction.
The motion physically **pauses** every time a finger lifts, a string is crossed, or the hand
shifts. What great players actually do is make that pause so short you don't hear it. This
lesson is about the gaps.

## Where the vibrato stops

- **A finger change on the same string** — the old finger's vibrato has to hand over to the
  new finger's.
- **A string crossing** — the hand angle changes and the motion resets.
- **A shift** — the hand *releases* to travel the neck (Stage 6), and a released hand can't
  vibrate.

The skill is keeping the hand **alive** into and out of each of these, so the shimmer resumes
the instant the new note settles rather than a beat later.

## The exercises

Simon Fischer's approach, simplified:

1. **Vibrate while changing fingers** on one repeated pitch — 1, then 2, then 3, then 4, all on
   the same note, the vibrato never stopping.
2. **Vibrate across a slow string crossing** — a long note on the A string, then the same
   motion continuing onto a note on the D string.
3. **Vibrate across a slow shift** — as above, keeping the dead moment minimal.

<TryThis>
  On the A string: vibrate a 1st-finger B, then without stopping the shimmer, put down the 2nd
  finger (C) and let *it* take over the vibrato, then the 3rd (D). One continuous wobble, three
  fingers. Then try B up to D with a shift, keeping the gap tiny.
</TryThis>

## Hear it

In a Romantic concerto slow movement, listen to a phrase that shifts position mid-line — the
vibrato barely flickers across the shift. In a beginner recording, the line breaks into
separate vibrated beads with dead shifts between. That continuity is the last thing to come.

## The usual mistake

Two opposite errors. One: the vibrato dies at every note so the line sounds like a string of
separate beads. The other: forcing the vibrato *through* a shift so hard that the shift itself
gets sloppy and out of tune. The shift's accuracy comes first; the continuity is layered on
once the shift is reliable on its own.
