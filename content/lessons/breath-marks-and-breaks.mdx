Three marks that all say **make a gap here** — but very different sizes of gap, from a flick of
the bow to a whole bar of silence.

## The three

| Mark | Looks like | Size of gap |
|---|---|---|
| **breath mark** | a comma **'** above the staff | tiny — a quick lift, like a singer taking a breath |
| **caesura** | **//** two slashes through the top of the staff — "railroad tracks" | a real stop: the sound and the time both break |
| **G.P.** | the letters over a bar (often an empty one) | *Grand Pause* — the whole texture silent for that bar |

## The breath mark

Borrowed from vocal and wind music, where the player literally has to breathe. On the violin
it's a **small bow lift and retake**. The time for it is usually taken by **clipping the note
before it short** — not by stretching the bar.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play G–A–B–C, then C–B–A on the A string. Clip that last A short, lift the bow up and back
  toward the frog, and land the next phrase (D–C–B–A–G) on a fresh down-bow. The gap should
  sound like a comma — brief, deliberate, and *inside* the pulse.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Adding the breath as **extra** time — stretching the bar to fit it in — when it should come
out of the note before. Playing straight through a **caesura** because it doesn't look like a
rest. And treating a **G.P.** as an ordinary counted rest when it's usually a suspended,
held silence — the music stops, and the stopping is the effect.

<Cite source="standard notation; the breath mark is borrowed from vocal and wind practice">
  A singer breathes; a flautist breathes; a violinist doesn't have to — so a string player
  reads a breath mark as a phrasing instruction: lift, separate, begin again.
</Cite>
