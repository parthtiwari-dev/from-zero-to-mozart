## The pre-read routine, in miniature

Before the bow moves, ten seconds:

- **Clef** — treble, second line is G.
- **Key** — how many sharps? That tells you which fingers sit forward.
- **Range** — how far up and down the staff? That tells you which strings.
- **Anything odd** — an accidental, a big leap, a note you don't know.

Stage 8 turns this into a full sight-reading method. For now, just the habit: look before you
play.

## Where you should be

- You can name any first-position natural note on the staff by **anchor + interval**, not by
  reciting nine names.
- You know its **string and finger**, and whether the 2nd finger is low or high.
- You can **land it roughly in tune** and check it against an open string, a ringing tone or a
  drone.
- You can read a **one-, two- or three-sharp key signature** and apply it without a sign on
  every note.
- Reading a simple first-position line, slowly, feels like *reading* — not decoding.

If those are mostly true, you're ready for the tunes. If one is shaky, its lesson is a click
away — nothing here is behind you for good.
