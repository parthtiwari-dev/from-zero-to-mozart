Two NEXT-level strokes that sit between the ones you have. **Portato** is the expressive one
you'll actually use soon. **Collé** is a training stroke that quietly makes everything else
better.

## Portato — pulsed notes in one bow

**Portato** (also called *louré*) is several notes under **one bow**, gently **pulsed** — each
note swelled a little, with a faint separation, but the bow **never stops or changes
direction.** It's neither fully connected (legato) nor stopped (staccato) — a soft "ta… ta…
ta…" carried on one stroke. Written as **tenuto dashes under a slur** (sometimes dots under a
slur).

Portato is **`dynamics-are-a-bow-thing` in miniature** — you're shaping every note with the
bow, on one stroke. It's the sound of a tender phrase in a slow movement, and it connects
straight back to Stage 3, where you read the dash-under-a-slur.

## Collé — "pizzicato with the bow"

**Collé** (French, "glued" — the name is about the *release*, oddly) is a tiny, pinched stroke
driven by the **bow-hand fingers**: from the string (or a hair above it), a sharp short pinch,
then an immediate stop or lift. Galamian called it "a pizzicato with the bow."

**Its real value is as a training stroke.** It builds **bow-hand finger flexibility** and a
clean, instant string "catch" — which feed détaché, martelé, and every off-string stroke
later. Your tabla background helps here: the finger independence you have in the right hand
transfers straight to this. Four collé notes on open D, from the fingers, is a better daily
minute than "learning collé" as a piece of repertoire.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Portato: play a one-octave D major scale, four notes per slur, each note gently pulsed, the
  bow never stopping. Then collé: on open D, twenty short finger-pinches, arm still, listening
  for a clean pluck-like start every time. The portato is music; the collé is gym.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

For portato — **stopping** the bow between the pulses, which turns it into slurred staccato.
The bow must keep travelling; the pulse is a swell, not a halt. For collé — using the **arm**
to make the pinch instead of the fingers, which defeats the point (it's a finger exercise).

<Cite source="Ivan Galamian, Principles (1962); the term louré — French, 'tied/slurred with pulses'">
  Galamian grouped portato with legato (it's a connected stroke) and collé with the "pinched"
  strokes. Collé, he taught, is where a student first trains the bow-hand fingers to act on
  their own — the foundation of every controlled bounce.
</Cite>
