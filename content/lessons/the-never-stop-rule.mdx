There is one rule in sight-reading that matters more than all the others, and it's the one
beginners break every time: **once you start, you don't stop.**

## Why

You hit a wrong note. Every instinct says: go back, fix it, get it right. In sight-reading,
that instinct is wrong, and here's why:

- **In every real setting the music keeps moving.** An accompanist, an orchestra, a quartet,
  an audition panel — none of them wait while you correct bar 3. If your practice trains you
  to stop, you'll stop when it counts.
- **A wrong note costs nothing if you play through it.** You played it, it's gone, nobody
  remembers. A *stop* costs the whole line — the pulse breaks, you lose your place, and now
  you're not reading, you're rescuing.
- **The pulse is the thread.** Lose a note, you've lost a note. Lose the pulse, you've lost
  the piece. Hold the beat from the first note to the last, mistakes and all, and you're still
  reading.

## The practice discipline that comes with it

If you never stop, then you also can't **replay a card to get it right** — that's just
practising the wrong thing. The routine (`how-sight-reading-cards-work`): play the card
**once, through, no going back**; then *diagnose* what broke (`the-four-layer-rescue`); then
tomorrow, a **new** card. You improve by reading a large volume of fresh easy material
fluently, not by grinding one line clean.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Pick any easy line. Turn the metronome to a slow, comfortable click. Play the line
  beginning to end, and make it a game: *no matter what happens, the bow does not stop and the
  beat does not break.* Do this with five different lines. Notice how much less lost you get
  when you stop trying to fix things.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

The instinct to correct. It's a *good* instinct when you're learning a piece to perform — but
sight-reading is a different skill, and here the instinct is poison. Train the split: when
you're *working up* a piece, fix everything; when you're *sight-reading*, fix nothing, keep
the pulse, move on.

<Cite source="ABRSM's 'golden rule' of sight-reading; universal across instruments and exam boards">
  Every graded-exam board says the same thing in almost the same words: *the golden rule is
  'once you start, you can't stop.'* The examiner wants a steady pulse from beginning to end,
  wrong notes included.
</Cite>
