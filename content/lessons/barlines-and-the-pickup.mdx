**Barlines** are the vertical lines that divide music into **measures** (bars). Each bar holds
exactly the number of beats the time signature promises — no more, no less. Before you play a
line, run your eye along it and find where **beat 1** sits in every bar. That's your anchor
for the whole thing.

## When a tune doesn't start on beat 1

Some tunes open with a **pickup** — also called an **anacrusis**: one or more notes that come
*before* the first full bar, leading into the first strong downbeat. The opening bar is
deliberately short, and the missing beats are usually made up in the **last** bar, so the two
add up to one complete measure.

A pickup changes how you **count the phrase** — not the pitches. You just have to notice the
short first bar before you start, so you know where "beat 1" really lands.

## A real pickup: *When the Saints*

The tune opens "**Oh, when the** SAINTS…" — three quick pickup notes, and "saints" lands on
the downbeat.

## The usual mistake

Playing the pickup as if it were beat 1 — which shoves the whole phrase one beat out of
place, and every barline after it feels wrong. Or not noticing the short first bar at all and
losing the count immediately. Always check: does the first bar have a full set of beats? If
not, it's a pickup.
