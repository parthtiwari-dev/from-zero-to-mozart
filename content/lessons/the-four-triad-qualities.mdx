A triad is two thirds stacked. But a third can be **major** (4 half steps) or **minor** (3),
and the two choices give you **four** kinds of triad — four different colours.

| Quality | Lower 3rd | Upper 3rd | Root → 5th | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Major** | major | minor | perfect 5th | bright, settled |
| **Minor** | minor | major | perfect 5th | dark, settled |
| **Diminished** | minor | minor | **diminished** 5th | tense, unstable (it contains a tritone) |
| **Augmented** | major | major | **augmented** 5th | floating, no gravity |

## Major and minor rolled

The two "settled" triads are playable as a rolled chord on the violin — here's G major and G
minor, open G and open D with the 3rd on the A string:

## The usual mistake

Hearing a diminished or augmented triad and assuming someone played a wrong note. They're
*meant* to sound unsettled — that's their job. A diminished triad usually appears on the way
to the tonic (it's built on the leading tone); an augmented one is a colour a composer reaches
for deliberately.
