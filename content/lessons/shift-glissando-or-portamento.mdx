Stage 3's `slides-and-octave-signs` taught you to *read* a glissando line and to recognise
portamento. Now that you can actually shift, here's the full picture: a clean shift, a
portamento, and a glissando are **the same physical motion**, at three different levels of
how audible you let it be.

## Three levels of the same slide

- **A clean (silent) shift** disguises the slide. The departing finger lifts just enough that
  it stops sounding as the hand travels, and the new finger lands cleanly on the new note. This
  is the default, invisible-technique goal for almost all shifting — including everything you
  practised in the last two lessons.
- **Portamento** ("to carry") is a short, subtle, *expressive* slide — usually starting late,
  just before the arrival note, rather than evenly paced the whole way.
- **Glissando** is the full, evenly-paced slide the whole distance — meant to be *heard* as a
  journey from note to note, not disguised. This is what Stage 3's line-between-two-noteheads
  notation asks for.

The full evenly-paced glissando version — the whole descent audible, start to finish — is the
one Stage 3 already showed you notated with a line.

## Which era wants which

Portamento and glissando as *expressive choices* are a **Romantic (19th-century)**
development — they weren't part of Classical or Baroque violin technique. Sliding your way
through a Mozart phrase the way a late-Romantic recording would is a style violation, the same
way playing every note with continuous vibrato would be (Stage 7's territory). Play Brahms or
Elgar with *zero* portamento, on the other hand, and you've missed part of *their* idiom. This
is the same "the symbol tells you WHAT, the era tells you HOW" framing Stage 3 built —
now it applies to a technique the page doesn't even notate.

## The usual mistake

Adding portamento to Classical repertoire because it feels expressive, or — just as often —
letting an ordinary shift turn *accidentally* audible through sloppiness, which reads as a
mistake rather than a choice. Which of the three you use should always be a decision, not an
accident of how careful you were.
