There are two small dots in music that look identical and mean completely different things.
The only way to tell them apart is **where they sit**.

- A **duration dot** sits *directly after* the notehead, level with it, on the same line or
  space. It **lengthens** the note by half again (the lesson you just read).
- A **staccato dot** sits *above or below* the notehead, clear of the staff line the note is
  on. It means **short and detached** — a way of *playing* the note, nothing to do with how
  long it's written.

## Read now, play later

You only need to **read** the difference here — spot which dot it is, and know a staccato
dot doesn't change the count. **Playing** a clean staccato is a bow skill, and that's Stage 5.
For now: if the dot is beside the head, add half the value; if it's above or below, carry on
counting normally and note that the composer wants it short.

## The usual mistake

Seeing any dot near a note and stretching it — so a staccato passage comes out slow and
heavy. Or seeing a dotted quarter and clipping it short because it looked like staccato.
Check the dot's **position** before you do anything with it.
