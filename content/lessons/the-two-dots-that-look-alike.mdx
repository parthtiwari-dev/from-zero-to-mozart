There are two small dots in music that look identical and do opposite jobs. The only way to
tell them apart is **where the dot sits**.

- A **duration dot** sits *directly after* the notehead, level with it, on the same line or
  space. It **lengthens** the note by half again (the lesson you just read).
- A **staccato dot** sits *above or below* the notehead, clear of it. It means **short and
  detached** — a way of *playing* the note, nothing to do with how long it's written.

## Read now, play later

You only need to **read** the difference here — spot which dot it is, and know a staccato dot
doesn't change the count. **Playing** a clean staccato is a bow skill, and that's Stage 5.
For now: dot beside the head → add half the value; dot above or below → count normally, and
note that the composer wants it short.

## The usual mistake

Seeing any dot near a note and stretching it — so a staccato passage drags. Or seeing a
dotted quarter and clipping it short because it looked like staccato. Check the dot's
**position** before you do anything with it.
