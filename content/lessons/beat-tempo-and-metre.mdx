Four words get thrown around loosely and mean four different things. Sorting them out now
saves a lot of muddle later, because almost every rhythm problem is really a confusion
between two of them.

- **Beat** — the pulse you'd tap your foot to. Steady, even, usually felt without counting.
- **Tempo** — how *fast* that pulse goes. Measured in beats per minute: 60 is one beat a
  second, 120 is twice that.
- **Metre** — how the beats *group*. Music leans harder on some beats: a march goes
  "**one**-two, **one**-two"; a waltz goes "**one**-two-three". That pattern of leaning is
  the metre.
- **Rhythm** — the actual pattern of longs and shorts you play *on top of* the beat. The
  tune's own shape in time.

The **beat** is the grid. **Tempo** is how fast the grid scrolls past. **Metre** is how the
grid is grouped. **Rhythm** is what you draw on it.

## The rhythm is not the beat

The beat keeps marching underneath while the rhythm does whatever the tune wants on top —
sometimes matching the beat, sometimes not.

## The usual mistake

Saying "faster" when you mean "more notes." Playing eighth notes instead of quarters is **not**
a faster tempo — it's the same beat, cut into smaller pieces. If a passage feels like it's
getting away from you, check first: did the pulse actually speed up, or did you just hit a
busier rhythm and start to panic? Nine times out of ten it's the second one, and the fix is
to keep your foot steady.

<TryThis>
  Set a metronome to 80 and tap along in plain beats. Now, without touching the metronome,
  fit two claps into each click, then three, then four. The tempo never moved. You were
  subdividing — a rhythm question, not a speed question.
</TryThis>
