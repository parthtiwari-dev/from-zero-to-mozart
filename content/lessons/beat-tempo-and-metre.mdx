Four words get used loosely and mean four different things. Getting them straight now saves a
lot of confusion later.

- **Beat** — the pulse you tap your foot to. Steady, even, usually felt without thinking.
- **Tempo** — how *fast* that pulse goes. Measured in beats per minute (bpm): 60 is one beat
  a second, 120 is twice that. The Italian words for tempo — *Adagio*, *Allegro* — come in
  Stage 3.
- **Metre** — how the beats *group*. Music leans on some beats more than others: a march
  feels "**strong**-weak, **strong**-weak" (groups of two); a waltz feels "**strong**-weak-weak"
  (groups of three). That grouping is the metre.
- **Rhythm** — the actual pattern of longs and shorts you play *over* the beat. The tune's
  own shape in time.

So: the **beat** is the grid, the **tempo** is how fast the grid moves, the **metre** is how
the grid is grouped, and the **rhythm** is what you draw on it.

## The usual mistake

Saying "faster" when you mean "more notes." Playing eighth notes instead of quarters is not a
faster tempo — it's the *same* beat, cut into smaller pieces. If a passage feels like it's
running away from you, check first whether the pulse actually sped up or whether you just hit
a busier rhythm and panicked.

<TryThis>
  Set a metronome to 80. Tap along in plain beats. Now, without changing the metronome, clap
  two claps per click, then three, then four. The tempo never moved — you just subdivided.
</TryThis>
