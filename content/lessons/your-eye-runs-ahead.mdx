While your bow is playing one note, your eyes should already be reading the next few. That gap
— between where you're looking and what you're playing — is the **eye-hand span**, and
growing it (a little) is what lets reading flow instead of stutter.

## How far ahead — not as far as you think

A skilled violinist reads roughly **three to six notes ahead**. That's it. Even experts
"rarely look a full bar ahead." Beginners read one note ahead, or *zero* — eyes locked on the
note being played, so there's no time to prepare the next one and every note is a fresh
surprise.

The lead is also **flexible**: it *stretches* when the music is easy (you can afford to look
further) and *shrinks* when a bar looks hard (you need your attention on the notes in front of
you). Skilled readers do this automatically. "Look as far ahead as possible" is genuinely
**bad advice** — too big a lead overloads you.

## How to grow it

- **Let the eyes drift forward on a long note or a rest.** A held half-note is two beats where
  your fingers have nothing to do — spend them reading the next bar. A rest is the same. Fluent
  readers *use* the long notes to look ahead.
- **Don't stare at the note you're playing.** Once your hand is committed to a note, your eye
  is done with it — move on. Trust your hand to finish it.
- **Pre-scan the hard bar.** When the pre-read (`the-pre-read`) flags a tricky bar, let your
  eye *revisit it early* — glance at it while you're playing the easy bars before it, so it's
  not a cold read when you arrive. (Your eye actually wants to do this — a difficulty pulls
  your gaze toward it before you get there.)

## The card-over-the-note drill

A classic: play a line, and have someone (or a card, or your other hand) **cover each note the
instant you start playing it** — so you're forced to have already read it. Uncomfortable at
first; it builds the span fast.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take an easy first-position line with some long notes in it. Play it slowly with the
  metronome, and make a rule: **your eyes are never allowed to be on the note you're playing.**
  On every long note, read the next bar. It'll feel like reading blind for a day or two, then
  it clicks.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Two opposite ones. **Reading too close** — eyes on the current note, no lead, constant
stutter. And **reading too far** — trying to hold a whole line in your head, which overloads
your memory and you lose the place. The sweet spot is small (a beat or two) and it moves. Let
the long notes carry your eyes forward; pull them back in when a bar looks dense.

  The measurements are consistent: the span is modest, it adapts inversely to difficulty, and
  skilled readers hold about a second of music "in the buffer" regardless of tempo. The

</Cite>
