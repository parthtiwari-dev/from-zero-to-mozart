Handel wrote **"See, the Conqu'ring Hero Comes"** for his oratorio *Joshua* in 1747, then moved
it into the more popular *Judas Maccabaeus* — and it never left. You'll know it as the Easter
hymn *"Thine Be the Glory"*. It's a **march**, it's grand, and it's the perfect piece for
**martelé**: every note wants a firm, prepared attack.

First position, **G major**, common time. The whole main strain uses only five notes — D, E,
G, A, B — no sharps to place, so your ear is free to work on the *stroke*.

## The usual mistake

Playing it *legato* because it's a hymn tune — smoothing the notes together so the march loses
its stride. It's a **conquering hero's** entrance: every note is a prepared, marked stroke, with
a real stop between. The other mistake: making strain 2's chromatic notes flat (plain D and C)
out of habit — D♯ and C♯ are leaning notes, place them high.
