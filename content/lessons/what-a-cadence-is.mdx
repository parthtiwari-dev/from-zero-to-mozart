A **cadence** is the little chord formula that ends a phrase. It's **punctuation** — the
musical equivalent of a full stop, a comma, or a "…". Every phrase of every tune you've played
ends with one, and learning to hear them is learning to hear where the sentences are.

## The full stop: the perfect cadence

The strongest ending is **V → I** (or V7 → I) — the dominant resolving home, with the melody
landing on **^1**. It's called a **perfect authentic cadence**, and it's how most pieces
actually finish.

You already met the mechanism in *The dominant seventh* — the tritone inside V7 squeezing onto
the tonic triad. A cadence is that resolution, placed at the end of a phrase to close it.

## How to spot one

Look for the end of a **musically complete unit** — usually marked by:

- a **longer note** or a **rest**;
- a **slur or phrase mark** closing;
- a **barline** before a new idea starts.

Then check what chord the harmony lands on. **Lands on I** → an authentic or plagal cadence
(an ending). **Lands on V** → a half cadence (a comma — more to come).

## A common decoration: the cadential 6/4

You'll often see the tonic chord in **second inversion** (the "6/4" from *Inversions and
spelling*) landing right before the V of a cadence — I(6/4) → V → I. It's a suspension in
chord form: two notes of the tonic hang over the dominant bass before resolving. Recognise the
shape; you don't need to write it yet.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play the V7 → I cadence at the top of this lesson a few times, then play just a plain V → I
  (drop the C). Both are perfect cadences; the V7 version is more emphatic because of the extra
  pull. Now play I → V (reverse it) and stop — hear how that *doesn't* sound like an ending. A
  cadence's direction is the whole point.

## The usual mistake

Playing through a cadence as if it were just more notes. When you reach one — the harmony
arriving home, the melody landing on the tonic, a longer note — *let it land*. Give it a hair
more time, ease the bow. That's how you make a phrase sound finished instead of merely stopped.
