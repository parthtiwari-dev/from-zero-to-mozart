Take-it-down reps for `taking-a-rhythm-down` and `taking-a-tune-down`. For each: play it
**once**, work it out (clap the rhythm, sing the tune in numbers), write it, **then** reveal
to check. Don't replay before you've committed to an answer — that's a different, easier
exercise.

Rhythmic and melodic drills, roughly Level 1–3 here; extend by transposing and lengthening.

## More drills

A seed set. Extend by transposing the melodic drills to A / C major and B / E minor, adding
tendency tones, and lengthening to two phrases. When Level 3 of both kinds is reliable, you
have the ear for `learning-a-tune-by-ear`.

<Cite source="Clendinning & Marvin, 'The Musician's Guide to Aural Skills'; Karpinski, 'Aural Skills Acquisition'; Stage 2 rhythmOnly drills">
  Graded dictation — a little each day, rhythm and melody kept separate at first, always
  checked against the source — is the backbone of every conservatoire aural course. The
  point is volume of short, corrected reps, not difficulty.
</Cite>
