6/8 is the most common compound time, and worth getting completely straight. Six eighth
notes are written in each bar — but they group **3 + 3**, into **two dotted-quarter beats**.

There are two ways to count it, and you'll use both:

- **The full count** — "**1** 2 3 **4** 5 6", every eighth, with 1 and 4 stressed. Use this
  when you're learning a bar slowly.
- **The feel** — "**1** – – **2** – –", just the two big beats. Use this at speed. It's the
  swing of a jig, a barcarolle, a lullaby.

## The classic 6/8 figure

The most common rhythm in 6/8 is a dotted-quarter (the whole beat) followed by a
quarter–eighth ("long–short") on the next.

## Why 6/8 is not 3/4

Both hold six eighth notes. The difference is entirely the **accent**: 3/4 has three beats
and stresses beat 1; 6/8 has two beats and stresses 1 and 4.

## A real 6/8 tune

The rhythm of *Row, Row, Row Your Boat*: "**Row**, row, row your boat, **gent**-ly down the
stream" — it rocks in two, like the boat.

## The usual mistake

Counting 6/8 as six beats, so the tune plods; or accenting every eighth evenly, which kills
the lilt. Feel the **two** dotted beats, lean into them, let everything between float.
