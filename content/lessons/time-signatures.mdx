The two numbers stacked right after the key signature are the **time signature**. They tell
you how the bar is built.

- The **top** number: how many beats in a bar.
- The **bottom** number: which note value counts as one beat. **4** means the quarter note;
  **8** means the eighth note; **2** means the half note.

So **3/4** is "three quarter-note beats to a bar", **2/4** is "two", **4/4** is "four".

## The metre is an accent pattern

A time signature isn't just arithmetic — it tells you where the music **leans**.

- **2/4** — a march. **Strong**–weak. "**One**-two, **one**-two."
- **3/4** — a waltz. **Strong**–weak–weak. "**One**-two-three."
- **4/4** — **strong**–weak–*medium*–weak. Beat 1 is heaviest, beat 3 has a smaller lift.

## Name the metre of a tune

*Twinkle, Twinkle* goes "**Twin**-kle **twin**-kle **lit**-tle **star**" — four steady beats
to a line, so it's in **4/4**. A waltz like *Amazing Grace* swings in **threes** — 3/4.
*Yankee Doodle* marches in **twos** — 2/4.

## The usual mistake

Reading the bottom number as "how many" of something. It's not a count — it's a *kind of
note*. "4" on the bottom always means the quarter note is the beat, whatever the top number
says. And: ignoring the metre's accent and playing every beat the same weight, so a waltz
comes out flat.
