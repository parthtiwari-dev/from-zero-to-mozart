The two halves of Stage 8 — reading fluently and hearing well — are meant to fuse. This
lesson is the weld: **reading a line while audiating it**, so your inner ear hears the music
a beat or two before your bow plays it.

## Sound before sign

Since Stage 1 you've mostly gone **sign → sound**: see the notehead, work out the pitch, play
it, hear what came out. That's how reading starts. But fluent musicians run it the other way —
**sign → inner sound → play** — and by-ear players never had the sign in the loop at all.

Stage 8 closes the loop. The eye still reads the symbol, but it feeds the **inner ear**
first: you hear the phrase off the page (`hearing-it-first`, `your-eye-runs-ahead`), and the
bow just confirms what you already heard. When that clicks, reading stops feeling like
decoding and starts feeling like *listening to a score*.

## The drill: sight-sing, then sight-play

For any new line:

1. **Pre-read it** (`the-pre-read`) — key, metre, range, shape.
2. **Sight-sing it** — in scale-degree numbers, slowly, against a drone on the tonic. No
   violin. If a spot won't sing, that's a spot you don't yet hear — solve it in your head.
3. **Sight-play it** — now the fingers follow an inner ear that already knows the tune.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take a fresh sight-reading card (`sight-reading-cards`), a level below your usual. Before
  playing it: set a drone, and sing it through in degree numbers. Then play it. Compare how it
  felt to a card you *didn't* sing first — the sung one should feel like you already knew it.
  Make the sing-first step a habit on every card.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Keeping reading and ear-work in separate boxes — doing the sight-reading drills with the eyes
only and the ear drills with no page — so they never combine. The point of Stage 8 is that
they're one skill. Sing your reading; read your singing. The other mistake: only audiating
the downbeat of each bar and letting the rest be reflex — audiate the *whole line*, running
ahead of the bow.
