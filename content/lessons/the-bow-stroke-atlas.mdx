Every bow stroke from this stage, on one page, as a real card each — name, when it matters,
where in the bow, how it's written, what it sounds like, how it's made, and what *not* to
confuse it with. Keep this open as a reference. You are **not** meant to have all of these in
your hands this year; the first five are the year's work, and the rest is a map of the road.

## The core — perform now

### Son filé (the long tone) · *perform now*

- **Where:** whole bow, frog to tip. · **Written:** a long held note.
- **Sound:** one even, sustained, unchanging tone.
- **Made by:** a constant bow speed and a constant sounding point, adding arm weight toward the
  tip to level out the bow's own weight.
- **Not:** anything with an accent or a shape — this is *flat and even* on purpose.
- Lesson: `one-long-tone`.

### Détaché · *perform now*

- **Where:** follows the note speed — middle for fast, more bow for broad. · **Written:** plain
  noteheads, no slur/dot/dash.
- **Sound:** separate bows, but a continuous, unbroken sound — only the direction changes.
- **Made by:** matching every up-bow to every down-bow, keeping the speed even through the
  turn.
- **Not:** staccato (which stops) or accented détaché (which leans).

### Legato / slur · *perform now*

- **Where:** anywhere; divide the bow evenly among the slurred notes. · **Written:** a curved
  line over the notes.
- **Sound:** several notes joined in one bow, no gap, no bump.
- **Made by:** the bow moving at a steady speed while the left hand changes notes underneath.
- **Not:** a tie (same pitch, joined) or a phrase mark (shape, not one bow).

### Martelé · *perform now*

- **Where:** anywhere, upper half to whole bows. · **Written:** a wedge, an accent, or `sfz` +
  context.
- **Sound:** a hard "clicked" start, the note rings, then a clean stop.
- **Made by:** gripping the string in the silence before the note, then a fast bow, releasing
  the weight the instant it moves.
- **Not:** accented détaché (no stop) or staccato (gentler, shorter).

### Clean string crossing · *perform now*

- **Where:** the four arm levels — G high, E low. · **Written:** just notes on different lines.
- **Sound:** the string changes with no accent, no gap, no scrape on the string in between.
- **Made by:** the upper arm setting the level *early*, the forearm rotating to finish.
- **Not:** bariolage (a fast continuous rock — same motion, more of it).

Lesson: `string-crossings` (and `slurred-crossings-and-bariolage`).

## The next layer — attempt now

| Stroke | Where / written | Sound | Not to be confused with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Détaché porté** | middle, moderate bow · no mark, read from a lyrical context | each note gently swelled by bow speed, singing | accented détaché (a *lean*, not a swell) |
| **Staccato** (solid) | middle, small strokes · a dot | short, crisp, stopped; the bow stays on the string | martelé (harder) · spiccato (off the string) |
| **Portato / louré** | one bow, pulses · dashes under a slur | notes gently throbbed in one continuous stroke | slurred staccato (which *stops*) |
| **Collé** | at the frog, from the fingers · usually unmarked | a sharp finger-pinch, like a bow-pluck; a training stroke | martelé (from the arm, sustains) |
| **Hooked bowing** | same direction, a stop · slur + dot on the 2nd note | two notes one direction, a clean gap between | a slur (which connects) |
| **Pizzicato** | right hand, no bow · `pizz.` … `arco` | a plucked, ringing note | *col legno* (wood on string — a bow effect) |
| **Rolling a chord** | drop low, sweep up · a stacked chord | a fast bottom-to-top brush landing on the top note | a harp-like slow spread |
| **Tremolo** (measured) | middle, small even strokes · 2–3 slashes on the stem | a counted subdivision on one repeated note | rhythmic beaming (different notes, once each) |

Lessons: `the-detache-family`, `staccato`, `portato-and-colle`, `hooked-bowing-and-retakes`,
`pizzicato`, `rolling-a-chord-with-the-bow`, `tremolo`.

## Off the string — recognise (later)

| Stroke | What it is | Not to be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| **Brush stroke** | broad, gentle, barely off the string — the way in to bouncing | spiccato (a real bounce) |
| **Spiccato** | a *controlled* bounce, an impulse per note, below the middle | staccato (on the string) · sautillé (faster, automatic) |
| **Sautillé** | a *fast, automatic* bounce — the stick springs, you don't lift | spiccato (slower, you lift each note) — same gesture, different speed |

Lessons: `spiccato`, `sautille-and-the-continuum`.

## Colour — reference

| Marking | What you do | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| **sul ponticello** | bow at the bridge | glassy, metallic, eerie |
| **sul tasto / flautando** | bow over the fingerboard | veiled, hollow, flute-like |
| **col legno** | wood of the bow on the string (tap or draw) | dry percussive click / ghostly pitch |
| **con sordino** | fit the mute | soft, dark, overtones stripped out |
| **au talon / à la pointe** | at the frog / at the tip | heavier, more bite / lighter |
| **tremolo** (unmeasured) | repeat as fast as a loose wrist goes | shimmer, tension |

Lessons: `colour-strokes`, `tremolo`.

## The far shore — reference

| Stroke | What it is | Where you hear it |
|---|---|---|
| **Ricochet / jeté** | a thrown bow, several bounces one direction | *William Tell*; Paganini |
| **Flying staccato** | many lifted short notes, one direction | Wieniawski |
| **Fouetté** | a whipped accent, flung at the string | Mendelssohn concerto |
| **Viotti stroke** | short weak → long strong, one bow: *energy and release* | Kreutzer; Classical style |
| **Chopping** | percussive bow strike near the frog | modern fiddle, Turtle Island Quartet |

Lesson: `the-far-shore`.

## How to use this page

Your work for the next long while is the **five core strokes** and the **eight in the next
layer**. Everything below "off the string" is a map of where the road goes after the
Foundation gate. When a piece asks for a stroke you don't recognise, come here first, then go
to its full lesson.
