The most useful ear skill you can build now isn't hearing that a note is wrong — it's hearing
*which way*. Too high or too low? Because the fix is different, and it's tiny.

## What each one sounds like

- **Too high** — bright, sharp, pinched, tight. It sits on top of the drone and won't settle.
- **Too low** — dull, flat, sagging, heavy. It droops under the note it should be.
- **In tune** — it *rings*. Against a drone or an open string, it locks in and seems to get
  louder (that's the resonance from the last lesson).

The correction is a **millimetre**, not a re-place. Lift-and-drop-again trains you to keep
missing. Nudge the finger — in the direction your ear just named — and listen again.

## The usual mistake

Hearing "wrong" but not *which way*, so the correction is a guess — and half the time it goes
further out. Name the direction first, out loud if it helps, *then* move. And don't overshoot:
small, small, small.

<TryThis>
  Take a tune you know cold. Play it, and on purpose land one note flat. Stop. Say "flat".
  Nudge it up until it rings. Do this with a different note each time — you're training the ear
  to report, not just to wince.
</TryThis>
