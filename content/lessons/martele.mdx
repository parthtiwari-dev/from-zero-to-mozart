**Martelé** (French for "hammered") is an accented, on-string stroke: a sharp bite at the very
start of the note, then the bow releases and coasts, and stops clean before the next one.
Between notes there's a definite silence — you *prepare* the next bite during it.

**Notation:** an accent mark, or sometimes a small wedge; often it's just implied by the tempo
and character rather than a unique symbol — martelé and hard staccato overlap in how they're
written, and you tell them apart from context.

**How to practise:** start on an open string. Set the bow on the string with a little pressure,
release that pressure sharply *as* the note starts (that release is the "bite"), let it ring,
then re-set for the next one. Four repetitions, then a scale. Stop if the right hand grips.

**Where it fits:** NEXT — add it once your détaché is even and relaxed. It's the first stroke
that deliberately builds silence into the sound.
