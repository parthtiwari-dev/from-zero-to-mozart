**Martelé** is French for "hammered". It's a powerful accented stroke with a sharp bite at the
front of each note, then the note rings, then the bow **stops** — a real silence before the
next one, and you *prepare* the next bite during that silence. It is the third stroke worth
learning to actually perform, after détaché and legato.

## It grows out of détaché

You already have most of martelé from `the-detache-family`:

| | bow speed | weight | start of note | between notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| plain détaché | even | even | nothing | seamless |
| accented détaché | fast → slow | heavy → light | a lean | connected |
| **martelé** | fast → slow | heavy → light | a **sharp bite** | a **definite stop** |

So martelé adds two things to accented détaché: a real *consonant* at the front, and a *rest*
at the back.

## The mechanism — prepare, bite, release, stop

The whole trick is that **the accent is prepared during the silence *before* the note.**

1. **Bow on the string, stopped.** Sink weight in and grip the string with the hair — you can
   wiggle the string silently side to side without sounding it. That's the string *caught*.
2. **Move — a fast bow — and release the weight the instant the bow moves.** The note starts
   with a **click, not a scratch.**
3. **The rest of the note is a fast, light bow** — the string rings freely.
4. **Stop the bow on the string.** During the stop, **let the hand go completely loose**, then
   re-prepare the next bite.

## Where in the bow

Anywhere — from a few centimetres to almost whole bows, for any note length except very fast
passages. Start in the **upper half or the middle**, moderate length. Then try it from the
frog (harder — bigger muscles, the bow's own weight) and as broad whole-bow martelé.

## On the page

There's no unique symbol. You read martelé from **context**: a small **wedge** or **arrowhead**
over the note, or an **accent** (`>`), or **`sfz`**, together with the character of the passage
— marked, detached, firm. Stage 3 taught you to read the wedge and the accent; this is where
those become "prepare the bite".

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Open A. Set the bow, sink weight, silently move the string side to side a few times — feel it
  gripped. Then play one martelé: fast bow, release the weight as it starts, stop. Rest a full
  second, hand loose. Do four. Then a G major scale, one martelé per note. Stop if the hand
  clenches and won't let go between notes.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Keeping the weight **on** for the whole stroke — which chokes the string and gives a
strangled, crushed sound instead of a ringing one. The weight is a spike at the very front and
gone by the middle. The second mistake: no real stop between notes, so it turns into accented
détaché. The third: a slow bow — martelé needs a *fast* bow, and fast is not the same as
heavy.
