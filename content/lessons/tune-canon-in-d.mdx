Pachelbel's *Canon in D* is really a **chord progression** — the same eight chords, over and
over, with new melodies layered on top each time. It's the perfect tune for *hearing* harmony,
because the harmony is the whole piece.

## The eight chords

The bass repeats this, endlessly:

| Chord | D | A | Bm | F♯m | G | D | G | A |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| In D major | **I** | **V** | **vi** | **iii** | **IV** | **I** | **IV** | **V** |
| Function | Tonic | Dominant | Tonic | — | Predom. | Tonic | Predom. | Dominant |

It ends on **V** so it can loop straight back to **I** — that's why it feels like it could go
forever.

## How to hear it

<TryThis>
  Play the slow eight-note line while a D drone holds underneath. Some notes will agree with
  the drone (the ones over D chords), some will grind (over A, over F♯m). That grinding *is*
  the harmony moving away from home and coming back. Listen to the eight-bar journey: I – V –
  vi – iii – IV – I – IV – V, home and away and home.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Playing the slow line as eight separate notes. It's one long descending gesture — shape it as a
single falling line that turns around at the bottom, not a list. And keep the bow moving:
Canon is all about a smooth, unbroken tone.
