3rd position is the easiest position to add after 1st — and not by accident. Its hand-frame is
an exact copy of the one you already have.

## Wherever your 3rd finger used to land, that's home now

In 1st position, your 3rd finger already visits every one of these notes. 3rd position just
picks the whole hand up and sets it down there, so the 3rd finger's old spot becomes the 1st
finger's new spot — and every low/high-finger habit you've already built (low-2, high-2, and so
on) transfers across unchanged.

| String | 1st finger in 3rd position |
|---|---|
| G | C |
| D | G |
| A | D |
| E | A |

## The self-check: an octave, not a unison

On three of the four strings, the 3rd-position note shares its **letter name** with an open
string — sounding a clean **octave** above it, not the same pitch. Play the 3rd-position note
and the open string together and listen for a ring with no beating:

The **G string** is the one exception — its 3rd-position note (C) has no open string to check
against, since there's no open C string. You'll rely on ear and muscle memory there, the same
way you already do for the low-2/high-2 choice in 1st position.

## Reading `III`

You'll start seeing a small roman numeral `III` over notes in real editions from here on. It
means exactly what Stage 3 told you it could mean and what `what-a-position-is` just made
physical: the hand sits in 3rd position for this passage. `reading-a-position-number`, next,
walks through it properly.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Shift from 1st position up to 3rd on each string in turn (open, then 1st finger up to the
  3rd-position note). Check the D, A and E strings against their matching open string for a
  clean octave. Then play the four 3rd-position notes across all four strings, back to back —
  G string included, by ear alone.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Guessing at 3rd position by feel alone, without ever checking it against something you can
hear precisely. The octave self-check exists because "close enough" isn't checkable by
eye — your ear is the only thing that can tell you the hand actually arrived.
