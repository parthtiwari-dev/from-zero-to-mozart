Here are the arpeggios to know now — the broken chords of the keys you're playing. Each is
just ^1 – ^3 – ^5 – ^8 of the scale, and each uses a clean first-position shape.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play the G major arpeggio up and down, four times, slowly, listening for the ring on the two
  open strings and the two G's. Then do the same for D major (even more resonant). Then C major
  — no open strings, so use your ear hard. Arpeggios are where you find out whether your 3rd
  finger really knows where G lives.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Rushing the skips because they feel harder than steps, and landing the 3rd finger short (flat)
after a jump. The notes of an arpeggio are further apart than a scale's, so the hand travels
more — go *slower* here, not faster, and check each landing against the open strings. The other
mistake: a bumpy bow at the string crossing in the middle. Keep the bow arm moving smoothly
across; the crossing should be seamless, not a lurch.

<Cite source="scale and arpeggio requirements — ABRSM / RCM graded syllabi, Initial to Grade 2">
  Every graded system pairs a scale with its arpeggio from the first grade. The arpeggio
  isolates string crossing and the hand's shape for the key; the scale isolates stepwise
  intonation. You need both.
</Cite>
