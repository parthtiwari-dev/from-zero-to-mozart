**Spiccato** is the first *off-string* stroke — the bow leaves the string between notes,
bouncing. It's a **LATER** stroke: a real, even spiccato needs a relaxed, organised bow hand
and a sure feel for the bow's balance, which come from months of the on-string strokes first.
The job here is to know what it is, hear it, and take the one honest first step toward it.

## What makes it spiccato

You give **an individual impulse for every single note** — it's a *controlled* stroke, not
one that runs on its own. The bow is dropped or placed onto the string and lifts off again,
note by note, at or just **below the middle** of the bow. (Détaché lives just *above* the
middle — a handy contrast.)

The catch: even though it bounces, **each note still has to ring.** A bounce that only clicks,
with no pitch, is a failed spiccato. You want the crisp bounce *and* a clear note.

## The honest on-ramp — the brush stroke

Don't start by trying to bounce. Start with a **brush stroke**: a broad, gentle,
*barely*-off-the-string stroke — more horizontal than vertical, with some length to each note,
singing rather than crisp. A loose arm, a flexible hand, a small "give" at each note. It's how
most methods introduce the *feeling* of an off-string stroke without the difficulty of a true
bounce.

The next step, later: play a light, fast détaché in the middle of the bow and gradually **stop
controlling it so hard** — let the bow start to bounce by itself. Spiccato is basically
"détaché that you stopped holding down."

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Just the brush stroke for now. Open A, sixteen light broad strokes in the lower-middle of the
  bow, arm loose, each note a little separated. Then the same on F♯. If the strokes are even
  and your hand stays soft, that's a real Stage-5 achievement — the bounce will come later.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Trying to force a bounce with a stiff hand — you get an uneven scrabble, some notes loud, some
missing, and a tired arm. Spiccato comes *out of* looseness, not effort. And reading every
dotted note as "off the string": in a slow or moderate passage, dots mean on-string staccato
(`staccato`), not spiccato.

  Galamian: in spiccato "the bow is thrown down on the string for every single note and lifted

</Cite>
