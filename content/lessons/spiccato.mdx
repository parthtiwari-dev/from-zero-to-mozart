**Spiccato** is the first *off-string* stroke: a controlled bounce, where the bow leaves the
string between each note. It's played around the middle-lower part of the bow, letting the
stick's natural springiness do most of the bouncing while the arm guides it.

**Notation:** there's rarely a unique symbol. Staccato dots at a brisk tempo, in a light
passage, generally mean spiccato; the same dots slow and heavy mean on-string staccato. You
read it from context.

**Why it's LATER:** a real, even spiccato needs a relaxed, well-organised bow hand and a
reliable feel for the bow's balance point — things that come from months of the on-string
strokes first. Trying to force it early just produces an uneven scrabble. Know what it is, hear
it in recordings, and come back to it.

The related family — sautillé, ricochet, flying spiccato — are all faster or more automatic
cousins of this, covered on the next page as reference only.
