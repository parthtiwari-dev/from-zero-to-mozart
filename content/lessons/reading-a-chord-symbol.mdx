Open a folk songbook, a jazz lead sheet, or a guitar-chord chart, and above the melody you'll
see short labels: **C**, **Am**, **G7**, **Dsus4**, **F/A**. That's **chord-symbol
shorthand** — a compact way of naming a chord. You'll meet it constantly the moment you play
with other people, so it's worth being fluent.

## The core code

| Symbol | Means | Notes (on C) |
|---|---|---|
| **C** | major triad | C E G |
| **Cm** (or C–, Cmin) | minor triad | C E♭ G |
| **C°** (or Cdim) | diminished triad | C E♭ G♭ |
| **C+** (or Caug) | augmented triad | C E G♯ |
| **C7** | dominant 7th | C E G B♭ |
| **Cmaj7** (or CΔ) | major 7th | C E G B |
| **Cm7** | minor 7th | C E♭ G B♭ |
| **Cm7♭5** (or Cø) | half-diminished 7th | C E♭ G♭ B♭ |
| **C°7** | diminished 7th | C E♭ G♭ B-double-flat (sounds as A) |
| **Csus4** / **Csus2** | 3rd swapped for 4th / 2nd | C F G / C D G |
| **C6** | major triad + 6th | C E G A |
| **Cadd9** | major triad + 9th | C E G D |
| **C5** | just root + 5th (a "power chord") | C G |
| **C/E** | C chord with **E in the bass** (a slash chord = an inversion) | E … C G |

## How to read it

The **letter** is the root. Everything after is a modification:

- nothing → **major**
- **m** → make the 3rd minor
- a **number** (7, maj7, 9…) → add that note on top
- **sus** → replace the 3rd
- **add** → add a note without a 7th
- a **slash** → put the named note in the bass

So **G7** = G major triad + F (the ♭7). **Dm7** = D minor triad + C. **F/A** = an F major
chord with A on the bottom (first inversion).

## Why a violinist cares

You won't strum chords. But chord symbols tell you **what key you're in, what the harmony is
doing, and which notes will clash or agree** — exactly the "understand what you're playing"
information. If the chart says **Am** and your melody note is a B, you know it's a passing
tone. If it says **E7** in the key of A minor, you know a strong cadence home is coming.

  shape before you play a note.

## The usual mistake

Ignoring the chord symbols because "I only play the melody." They're the fastest read you have
on the harmony. A glance at the chord letters over a phrase tells you where it's going and
where it rests — information that shapes how you phrase and where you breathe.
