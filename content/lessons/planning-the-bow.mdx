You have one bow-length of hair. A phrase might be four notes or fifteen. Deciding **in
advance** how much bow each note gets — so you arrive at the end of the phrase and the end of
the bow at the same time — is a real skill with a real name: **bow distribution**. It's the
answer to "why did I run out of bow halfway through that line?"

## The idea: divide the bow to match the notes

If four equal notes are slurred into one bow, each note gets **a quarter of the bow**. Simple.
It stops being simple the moment the notes aren't equal — a long note needs more bow, the peak
of the phrase wants more, the quiet tail wants less — and the moment there are enough notes
that "just play it" runs you off the end.

## How to work it out — stop first, then smooth

The method every teacher uses:

1. **Look at the hair** and divide it by eye into as many equal parts as the phrase has beats.
   (A strip of tape on the stick helps at first.)
2. **Play the phrase as separate stops** — actually halt the bow at each division point, so
   you hear and feel the equal portions. It's basically a rhythm exercise, and rhythm is your
   strength.
3. **Smooth it out** — take away the stops, keep the portions identical. Now it flows.
4. **Then bend it on purpose** — give the important note more bow, the throwaway note less.

## When you'll run out anyway — the retake

Sometimes a phrase ends at the tip and the next one needs to start down-bow with lots of bow.
You fix that with a **retake**: during the breath between phrases, lift the bow and quietly
reset it back toward the frog. A silent moment, "filled only with resonance" — you met the
symbol for it in Stage 3 (`down-bow-up-bow-and-the-retake`); here you actually make the move.

## The usual mistake

Playing the first few notes with generous, satisfying bow and then discovering the phrase
isn't over — so the last notes get crushed into the last inch at the tip, weak and scratchy.
Plan **backwards from the end**: "I need to still have a third of the bow left when I reach
that note." Then the whole phrase fits.

  method on even bow division for the same reason.
</Cite>
