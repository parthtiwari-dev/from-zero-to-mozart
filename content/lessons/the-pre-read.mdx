The first time an unseen line makes a sound should be the **second** time you've "played" it —
the first time was silent, in your head, during a quick scan. That scan is the **pre-read**,
and doing it in a fixed order, every time, is most of what separates a reader from a decoder.

## The scan — a fixed order

Each answer sets up the next, so the order matters:

1. **Clef.** Treble, for us — but name it, so the habit survives meeting a viola part one day.
2. **Key signature** → *name the key* (and its relative minor). Which finger pattern? **Do I
   know that scale under my hand?**
3. **Time signature + any tempo word** → *feel one bar of the pulse* before anything else.
4. **The range.** Glance at the lowest and highest note. First position all the way? Ledger
   lines? This tells you whether you can play it at all yet.
5. **Accidentals.** Scan for any sharp/flat/natural *not* in the key signature. Mark the hard
   ones with your eye.
6. **Rhythm traps.** A dotted figure, syncopation, a tuplet, a fast run, an awkward rest —
   **clap or count the hardest bar now.**
7. **Slurs & bow marks.** Which notes share one bow? Any `⊓`/`V`, `Sp.`, staccato, an `f`–`p`
   echo?
8. **Repeats & road map.** `:||`, 1st/2nd endings, `D.C.`/`D.S.`/Coda, a fermata. Know the
   route before you leave.
9. **Audiate beat 1.** Hear the first note — and its pull (is it *do*? *sol*?) — and count
   yourself in, silently, one full bar, before the bow touches the string.

## The compact version — S-T-A-R-S

For the middle of the scan, there's a classroom mnemonic worth keeping as a fallback:

| | |
|---|---|
| **S** | **S**harps & flats — the key signature |
| **T** | **T**ime signature (and tempo) |
| **A** | **A**ccidentals — the ones outside the key |
| **R** | **R**hythm — count the tricky bars, watch the rests |
| **S** | **S**igns — dynamics, articulation, repeats, endings |

The full 9-step version just adds *clef*, *range*, *bows*, *road map*, and *audiate* around it.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take any tune from Stage 1–3 you haven't played in a while. Run the full 9-step scan out
  loud — clef, key, time, range, accidentals, rhythm, bows, road map, audiate beat 1. Only
  then play it, once, no stopping. The scan should take under a minute; with practice, twenty
  seconds.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Starting to play and doing the "scan" as you go — discovering the key change in bar 5, the
repeat at the end, the accidental you already played wrong. The pre-read is a *separate step*,
before the first note. The second mistake: skipping the **audiate** step — a reader who hasn't
heard beat 1 in their head is guessing.
