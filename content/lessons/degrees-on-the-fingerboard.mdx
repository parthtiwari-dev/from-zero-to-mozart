Everything so far has been **hear a degree → say its number**. Playing by ear needs the other
direction: **hear a degree → put the finger on it**, without naming anything. Same skill,
turned around.

## The degree map of a key

In a fixed key and position, each degree lives at a specific spot. Learn the map for a key
and "hear ^6, play ^6" becomes one move:

The same degree can live in more than one place — ^1 in A major is *both* the open A **and**
3rd finger on the D string, and (up an octave) 3rd finger on the E string. Knowing all its
homes is what lets you catch a tune wherever it sits.

## This is the reverse of the fingerboard map

Stage 5's `finding-a-tune-by-ear` taught you to hunt a tune out by trial and correction —
play a guess, hear if it's too high or low, adjust. That's this same degree-to-finger link,
built from the *playing* side. The scale-degree gym builds it from the *hearing* side. When
they meet in the middle, you can play back what you hear.

## The drill

1. **Map a key.** Play the one-octave scale over its drone, saying degree numbers, three
   keys: A, D, G.
2. **Hear → place.** Random degree sounds. Put the finger there and play it. *Then* check by
   playing up the scale.
3. **Two-note fragments.** "^5 to ^3." Find both, play them, no naming out loud.
4. **Switch keys.** Same drill in D. The *shapes move*; the *functions don't*. ^6 feels like
   ^6 in every key even though it's a different finger.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Drone on D. Sing ^6 (that's B). Now, without playing a scale to find it, put what you think
  is ^6 straight down — 1st finger, A string — and play it against the drone. Right? If it
  sounds a hair off, that's the `finding-a-tune-by-ear` correction loop: nudge and re-check.
  Do ^3, ^7, ^2 the same way.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Always running up the scale from ^1 to find the degree you want. That works in a slow
practice room and fails the moment a tune moves at speed. Train the **direct** jump: degree
in the ear, finger on the note. The scale-run is only your *check*, afterward.
