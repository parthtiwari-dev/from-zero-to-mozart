There's a third kind of vibrato, and this lesson is here so you know what it is and then set it
aside. **Finger vibrato** is a **REFERENCE** technique for this course — recognise the term,
don't drill it.

## What it is

In finger vibrato the motion comes from the finger's **own base knuckle** bending in and out —
no forearm motion, and barely any hand motion. It's the **smallest** and subtlest of the
three, a tiny flutter used for very fast, very narrow shimmer, or on a note too short for a
full arm or wrist vibrato to bloom.

## Why it's not your next step

Two reasons:

- It's genuinely advanced. It needs very loose, independent finger joints and a completely
  secure hand — the kind of control that comes after years, not months.
- Many teachers argue it isn't really a separate technique at all. Fritz Gearhart calls it
  "a result of the other two, not an independent motion" — i.e. once your arm and wrist vibrato
  are free, a bit of finger-joint give happens on its own, and *that's* finger vibrato. Chasing
  it directly, before the bigger motions are loose, just teaches the hand to tense.

The source course tags it the same way: *"Finger vibrato... is not the next step simply
because your wrist vibrato feels difficult."*

## What to do instead

Build a loose arm vibrato and a loose wrist vibrato (Movements B–C). The finger contribution
will arrive by itself, and you'll be able to narrow and speed your vibrato for a fast passage
without ever having practised "finger vibrato" as a thing.
