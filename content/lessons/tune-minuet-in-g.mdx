Bach's *Menuet in G* — actually by **Christian Petzold**, and long mis-attributed to Bach
because it sits in the little notebook he kept for his wife Anna Magdalena. This is the easy
first-position version, moved to **D major** so it stays on the friendly strings.

A minuet is a dance in **three**, and its strain is written once with a **repeat**: play it,
go back, play it again with a different last bar. That structure — `‖: … :‖` with a first and
second ending — is the whole point of this tune for Stage 3.

## The one thing this tune is for

**The repeat structure.** `‖: … :‖` with a first and second ending, traced correctly: play
through, take the `1.` ending, obey the repeat, play again, skip to the `2.` ending. And the
**terraced dynamic** — `f`, then a quiet `p` echo on the repeat, the Baroque way.

## The usual mistake

Playing both endings in a row the first time, or forgetting the repeat. And playing the echo
at the same volume as the first time — the whole character of a Baroque repeat is that it
answers itself, quieter.
