A small **3** bracketed over a group of notes means: fit **three** of them into the time that
**two** would normally take. The eighth-note triplet — three notes in one beat — is by far
the most common.

Count it "**1‑&‑a**", or "**1‑trip‑let**", or "**1‑la‑li**" — three even syllables per beat.
Whichever you use, the three notes are **equal**.

This should feel natural to you: it's the same three-in-the-space feel as compound time
(6/8), and the same as *tigun* in tabla — the beat carrying three even strokes.

## The quarter-note triplet

The bracket works on any value. A **quarter-note triplet** fits three quarter notes evenly
into the space of two beats.

## The usual mistake

Playing the triplet as "long–short–short" (a dotted feel) or as "two notes plus a grace" — a
triplet is **three even notes**. And rushing straight out of it back into the duple notes.
Slow the whole thing right down until the three are genuinely equal, then bring the tempo up.
