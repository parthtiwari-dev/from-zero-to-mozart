Stage 4 covered a lot of theory. Here's what actually belongs in your daily practice right now,
versus what to file away for later.

| Stage | Scales |
|---|---|
| NOW | G major, D major, A major, C major — one octave each |
| NEXT | E natural minor; G major, two octaves; F major; B♭ major, as your fingering vocabulary expands |
| LATER | the full major/minor set, 2–3 octaves, faster bowings, broken thirds, chromatic |
| ADVANCED | 3-octave scales and arpeggios, double-stop scales, advanced bowing patterns |

A well-known external benchmark (ABRSM, the UK grading system) treats scales/arpeggios and
sight-reading as genuinely **separate** skills, tested separately — worth remembering here too:
playing a scale fluently and sight-reading fluently are related but different abilities, and
neither one substitutes for the other. This book isn't following ABRSM's syllabus, but that
separation is a useful reminder of what a scale actually trains.
