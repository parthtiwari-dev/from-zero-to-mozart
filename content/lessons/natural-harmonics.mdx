Stage 3's `harmonics-on-the-page` taught you to *recognise* a harmonic — the circle, the
diamond notehead — and promised the producing would come here. This is that lesson.

## Touch, don't press

Rest a finger **lightly** on the string at one of its nodes — flatter than a normal stopping
finger, with no downward weight at all — while the bow, ideally already moving before contact,
stays close and parallel to the bridge. Press even slightly and the harmonic dies into a dull,
muted thud. The whole technique is "less is more."

Every string has this same set of three — the fractions (½, ⅓, ¼ of the string) are the same
regardless of which open string you're touching.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Find the octave harmonic on the D string first — it's the easiest to locate, since the
  midpoint is a large, forgiving target. Once that speaks cleanly, hunt for the octave-plus-fifth
  a third of the way up, then the two-octave harmonic a quarter of the way up. If a harmonic
  won't speak, bow a little faster and a little closer to the bridge before assuming your finger
  placement is wrong.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Pressing instead of touching — the single most common failure, and the reason a harmonic
attempt turns into an ordinary stopped note or a dead thud. The lighter your finger, the purer
the ring; there's no such thing as "a little bit pressed."
