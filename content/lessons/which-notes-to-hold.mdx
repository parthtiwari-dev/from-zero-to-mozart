Sometimes a chord genuinely can't be held whole — the hand runs out of fingers, or the tempo
doesn't leave time to set every finger before the bow needs to move. This is the professional's
triage, not a cop-out: full chords in fast passages are simplified this way constantly, even by
players who could technically hold every note given enough time.

**Keep the outer two notes: the bass and the melody top.** They carry the chord's identity —
the low G anchors the harmony, the high G is usually where the melodic line actually continues
into the next note. **The inner voices (D, B here) are the first to go** when there isn't time
to hold everything — treat them as quick passing notes the bow brushes through on its way up,
rather than notes you stop to tune precisely.

## The usual mistake

Treating a rushed, half-formed attempt at holding *every* note as more honest than a
deliberately simplified version. It isn't — a clean bass-and-top with a blurred middle sounds
far more musical than four notes all fighting for a hand that doesn't have time to set them.
Triage on purpose, don't just fail by accident.
