**Melodic dictation** — hear a tune, write it down — is every skill in this stage working
together. It has a fixed order that every method teaches, and following the order is most of
the battle.

## The three passes, always in this order

1. **Rhythm first.** Before a single pitch, take down the rhythm (`taking-a-rhythm-down`) —
   *where* the notes are. You'll fill in *what* they are afterward. Trying to get pitch and
   rhythm at once is how people freeze.
2. **Contour.** Mark the shape — up, down, same — and circle the leaps. Now you have a
   skeleton: the right number of notes, in the right rhythm, going the right directions.
3. **Scale degrees.** Find the tonic (it's usually the last note, or the most stable one).
   Then fill in each note as a degree, using the pulls to check (`the-tendency-tones`). Sing
   the phrase back **in numbers** before you commit — if you can sing it, you have it.

## Then play it

The last step turns dictation into playing by ear: once you've sung the phrase in numbers,
put it on the violin (`degrees-on-the-fingerboard` — each degree already has an address).
Writing it and playing it are the same act now.

## The level ladder

| Level | Melody |
|---|---|
| 1 | 3–4 notes, stepwise, in the tonic triad, one key (G) |
| 2 | 5–6 notes, steps + one small leap; still major |
| 3 | a full phrase; a tendency tone or two; D and A major |
| 4 | two phrases; a wider range; C major and E minor |
| 5 | a short whole tune; one chromatic note |

Full drills at `the-dictation-drills`.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Drone on G. Have a 4-note phrase played (or shuffle your own). Take it down in the three
  passes — rhythm, then contour, then degrees. Sing it back in numbers. Then play it. Check
  against the original. If you missed a note, which pass failed — did you mishear the rhythm,
  the direction, or the degree?
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Going for pitches first, or going for pitch and rhythm simultaneously. The order is rhythm →
contour → degrees, every time; each pass makes the next one easier. The other mistake: not
singing the phrase back before writing or playing it. If it won't come out of your mouth, you
haven't heard it yet — listen again.

  Every aural-skills text teaches melodic dictation as staged passes — prosody and metre,
  then contour, then scale-degree content — because working memory can't hold a whole melody
  at full detail on one hearing. The passes let you build it up in layers.
</Cite>
