Take a perfect or a major interval and stretch it a half step wider — it becomes
**augmented**. Squeeze a perfect or a minor interval a half step narrower — **diminished**. Most
of the time you can ignore these. One of them you can't: the **tritone**.

## The tritone

**F up to B** is a 4th (F–G–A–B, four letters). But it's *wide* — three whole steps, a half
step bigger than a perfect 4th. That makes it an **augmented 4th**, and it has a nickname from
the middle ages: **diabolus in musica**, "the devil in music."

It's the most **restless** interval in tonal music. The two notes are itching to move — and
they resolve by the two of them squeezing *inward* to a 3rd, or splaying *outward* to a 6th:

M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C clef=treble
[FB]4 | [Ec]4 |]`}

  defaultBpm={54}
/>

You'll see why it matters so much in *The dominant seventh* — the tritone is the live wire
inside that chord.

## Restful and restless — consonance and dissonance

Intervals sort roughly into "can sit still" and "wants to move":

| | Intervals | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Perfect consonance** | unison, octave, perfect 5th | totally at rest, a little hollow |
| **Imperfect consonance** | major & minor 3rds and 6ths | at rest, but warm and full — melodies and harmony are built on these |
| **Dissonance** | 2nds, 7ths, the tritone | tense — needs to resolve to a consonance |

The **perfect 4th** is the odd one out: consonant when it sits above a bass note in a chord,
treated as a dissonance in strict two-part writing. Don't lose sleep over it.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  On the D and A strings, play F (low 2, D string) and B (1st finger, A string) together,
  rolled. Hold it — hear the tension. Now move the B up to C (low 2, A string) and the F down
  to E (1st finger, D string): E and C, a 6th, resolved. Do it a few times. That release is
  the sound of a cadence, in miniature.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Hearing "dissonance" as "mistake." Dissonance isn't wrong — it's the **engine**. Without the
tritone straining toward resolution, tonal music would have no pull, no arrivals, no drama.
The dissonances are what make the consonances feel like home.
