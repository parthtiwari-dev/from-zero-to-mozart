REFERENCE — the top of the map. Past 5th position, the hand keeps climbing onto the body of the
violin itself, and this course stops treating positions as a performed skill.

## Why it's REFERENCE here

Past 5th position, notes cluster closer together (an octave gets physically shorter the higher
you go), and the **4th finger in particular loses its comfortable curve** and has to flatten to
reach. Suzuki doesn't introduce 7th position until Book 7 — well past this course's
Intermediate-gate scope. Full treatment belongs to a repertoire module, when an actual piece
calls for it — not to a general technique stage.

## The one landmark worth knowing now

**7th position's 1st finger is always exactly the octave** — the same pitch, and the same
physical spot, as the octave natural harmonic you already learned in `natural-harmonics`. If
you ever need to find 7th position in a hurry, that harmonic node is where your 1st finger
belongs.
