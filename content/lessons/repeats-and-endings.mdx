Music repeats itself constantly, and rather than print the same bars twice, notation uses a
few shorthand signs. You need two of them to play a strophic tune or a round.

## The repeat barline

A **thick barline with two dots** — `:||` — means "go back and play from the start again"
(or from the matching `||:` if there is one). Play the enclosed section **twice**, then carry
on.

## First and second endings

Often a repeated section should end *differently* the second time round. That's what the
bracketed **"1."** and **"2."** are for:

- Play through to the **first ending** (under the "1." bracket), then take the repeat.
- On the way back through, **skip the first ending** — jump straight to the **second ending**
  (the "2." bracket) — and carry on from there.

<NotatedExample
  abc={`X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4

## Just these two, for now

Scores have a whole set of navigation signs — **D.C.** (go back to the beginning), **D.S.**
(go back to the sign), **Coda**, **Fine**, **segno**, rehearsal letters. Those are the rest of
the road-map, and they get their own lesson in **Stage 3**. Here you only need the repeat and
the two endings.

## The usual mistake

Playing both endings in a row the first time through, or forgetting to take the repeat at
all. Trace the route with your finger *before* you play — where does the repeat send you, and
which ending do you take each time?
