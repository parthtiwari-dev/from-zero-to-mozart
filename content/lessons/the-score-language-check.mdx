## Where you should be

You can now open an unfamiliar first-position piece and **name every mark on it** — what it is,
what it asks for, and, where it matters, which era's reading applies. That's the **reading**
half of the objective, at this level of music. It's a real milestone, and worth feeling.

## What's still ahead

"Read it" and "play it" are different, and you've read a lot that you can't yet do:

| You can read… | You'll do it in… |
|---|---|
| `spiccato`, `martelé`, `détaché` | Stage 5 — the bow |
| a `p` as bow speed + contact point | Stage 5 — an even messa di voce is a real skill |
| `tr`, a turn, an appoggiatura | Stage 6 — the left hand |
| "Baroque trills start above" | Stage 7 and the repertoire modules — *choosing* how to shape one |

  The goal is to open a score and make something honest of it on your own. Reading every mark
  is the first of the five parts of that — and the one you've now, for first-position music,
  largely got.
