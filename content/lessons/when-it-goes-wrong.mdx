Vibrato goes wrong in a handful of specific, recognisable ways. Learn the list now — you'll do
most of them at some point, and knowing which one you're doing tells you exactly what to fix.

## The catalogue

| What it sounds like | What it is | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| A fast, tight, narrow bleat — a "goat" or "nanny" vibrato | the hand is tense and the motion has shrunk and sped up to escape it | go right back to slow, wide "sirens"; rebuild from a loose big motion |
| A slow, wide lurch that never settles into a shimmer | the wrist isn't flexible enough yet; the motion is coming from too big a lever | the knock exercise; build wrist give before speeding up |
| The vibrato won't start, or jerks | the thumb is clenched against the neck | practise with the thumb lifted clean off the neck until the motion is free |
| The line goes dead in the gaps between notes | the vibrato stops at every finger change | slow, coordinated finger-change practice (the next lesson) |
| The note sounds sharp | the wobble is going *above* the pitch, not just below it | consciously roll only backward from the note; the pitch is the ceiling |
| The tone pulses or scratches in time with the vibrato | the wobble is transmitting into the bow arm | bow-only and vibrato-only practice, then combine slowly |
| The note drifts flat or sharp as you vibrate | the *centre* of the wobble is moving | anchor the pitch dead first; check the centre against a drone |

## The usual mistake

Treating every vibrato problem as "not enough practice" and just grinding. Almost every fault
on that list is **tension** or **a missing sub-skill**, and grinding makes tension worse. Name
the fault, apply its specific fix, keep the sessions short.
