Two different relationships between major and minor keys, and it's worth keeping them straight
because they answer different questions.

## Parallel keys — same tonic, different signature

G major and G minor (from two pages back) are **parallel**: same starting note, genuinely
different key signature, different scale.

## Relative keys — same signature, different tonic

Every major key has a **relative minor** that shares its *exact* key signature — same sharps
or flats, different note treated as "home."

**G major's relative minor is E minor.** They share one sharp (F♯); the pitch collection
overlaps almost completely — what differs is which note *feels* like home, and that changes the
harmony and the mood built on top.

Quick way to find any major key's relative minor: count down **three half steps** from the
major tonic (or look at the inner ring of the circle of fifths, directly inside its matching
major key).
