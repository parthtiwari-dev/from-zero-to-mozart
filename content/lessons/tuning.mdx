The four strings are **G, D, A, E**, low to high. Each is a **fifth** above the one below it,
and they're tuned to fixed pitches — the violin has no way to enforce them, so you set them by
ear or by a tuner every time you play.

## The reference

The **A** string is tuned to **A = 440 Hz** (the pitch an oboe gives an orchestra). Get that
from a tuner, a piano, or the drone in the practice bar below, and tune the A first. Then tune
the others: either match each to the tuner, or listen for a clean, ringing fifth between
neighbours (G–D, D–A, A–E) with no wobble.

## Pegs and fine tuners

The **fine tuners** — the little screws at the tailpiece — handle small adjustments; use these
day to day. The **pegs** handle big ones. Pegs work by friction: turn slowly and **push the peg
gently inward** as you turn, or it'll slip back the moment you let go. Small movements. A string
that's way off is usually a peg job; a string that's a hair flat is a fine-tuner job.

## It drifts

New strings stretch for a week or two. Temperature and humidity move everything. Tune **every
time**, before you play and again after a few minutes.
