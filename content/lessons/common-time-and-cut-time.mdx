Two symbols turn up where a time signature should be, and both catch beginners out.

- A large **C** = **4/4**. Four quarter-note beats a bar, exactly the same as writing "4"
  over "4". You'll see it constantly — Mozart's editions of K.216 use it.
- A **C with a vertical slash** (**¢**) = **cut time**, also called *alla breve* or **2/2**.
  Two *half-note* beats a bar — the same total length as 4/4, but felt "in two", with a
  quicker lilt.

## The C is not a letter

It looks like a C, and people say "common time", but it was never the letter C or short for
the word "common". It's a **broken circle**, left over from medieval notation: a full circle
meant triple time, a broken circle — which happens to look like a C — meant duple. The slash
through it just halves the beat value again.

## The usual mistake

Believing the C stands for "common" (a nice mnemonic, but wrong, and it trips you up on the
slashed one). Or pausing mid-read to wonder whether a C is a note — its slot, right after the
clef and key, tells you it's the metre.
