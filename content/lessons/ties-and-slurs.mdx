A curved line joins two or more notes. It's either a **tie** or a **slur**, and you tell them
apart by *what it joins*.

## Tie — same pitch, held as one

A tie connects two noteheads at the **exact same pitch**. You don't play the second one — you
hold the first straight through it, as a single longer sound.

M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C clef=treble
D2 D2- | D2 z2 |]`}

  defaultBpm={66}

A tie is how you write a length that no single notehead can show — a five-beat note is a
whole note tied to a quarter — or a note that has to carry over a barline.

  defaultBpm={66}
/>

## Slur — different pitches, one bow

A slur curves over notes at **different** pitches. It's a phrasing mark, and on the violin it
almost always means: **play the whole group in one bow**, without re-starting the sound for
each note. The left-hand fingers still change; the bow just keeps travelling.

<NotatedExample
  abc={`X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C clef=treble
(D E F G) |]`}
  labels={[

  ]}
  caption="One curved line over four moving notes — one bow carries all four. The notes still sound separately; the bow simply doesn't stop or change direction."
  defaultBpm={66}
/>

## The quick test

    { name: "F", string: "D", finger: "low 2" },
    { name: "F", string: "D", finger: "low 2" },
    { name: "G", string: "D", finger: "3" },
    { name: "A", string: "D", finger: "4" },
  ]}
  caption="Three curves here. Which are ties, which are slurs? Check what each one joins — same pitch or moving — then reveal. (D–E: slur. F–F across the bar: tie. G–A: slur.)"
  defaultBpm={60}
/>

Does the curve join two noteheads at the **same height** on the staff? Tie — hold it. Does it
arc over notes that **move up or down**? Slur — one bow. A curve across a barline joining
identical pitches is almost always a tie.

## The usual mistake

Re-bowing or re-plucking across a tie — you get two attacks where the music wants one long
note. Or reading a slur as a tie and sitting on one pitch while the tune was meant to move.

<Cite source="standard notation; the slur-as-one-bow convention is specific to strings">
  A singer slurs by not re-articulating; a violinist slurs by not changing bow. Same curve —
  the instrument decides what it asks of you. Slurs come back as a bow stroke in Stage 5.
</Cite>
