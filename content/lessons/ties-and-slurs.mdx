Same curved line, two different jobs — told apart by what it connects.

## Tie

A tie joins two notes of the **same pitch**. Don't replay the second note — just hold the
first one through it. It's how you write a duration that doesn't fit neatly into one notehead,
or a note that carries across a bar line.

<NotatedExample
  abc={`X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C clef=treble
D2-D2 |]`}
  caption="Tied half notes, same pitch — one continuous 4-beat sound, not two separate attacks."
  defaultBpm={66}
/>

## Slur

A slur joins two or more notes of **different** pitches — it's a phrase mark. On the violin it
almost always means: play the whole group **in one bow**, without re-articulating each note.

<NotatedExample
  abc={`X:1
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C clef=treble
(D E F G) |]`}
  caption="A slur over four different pitches — one bow stroke carries all four notes."
  defaultBpm={66}
/>

Context tells you which one you're looking at almost instantly: if the curve joins **two
noteheads at the exact same staff position**, it's a tie. If it arcs over **notes that move**,
it's a slur.
