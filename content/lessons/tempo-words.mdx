Above the very first note of a piece, in Italian (mostly), you'll find a word telling you how
fast to go. These are the ones you'll meet constantly.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Largo | very broad, very slow |
| Adagio | slow |
| Andante | at a walking pace |
| Moderato | moderate |
| Allegro | fast, lively |
| Presto | very fast |

A few modifiers soften or sharpen these: **molto** (very), **poco** (a little), **meno**
(less), **più** (more), **ma non troppo** (but not too much) — so "Allegro ma non troppo" is
fast, but don't overdo it. You'll also see **rit.** or **rall.** (gradually slower) and **a
tempo** (return to the previous speed) written mid-piece, plus a metronome mark like
"quarter note = 126" giving an exact number.
