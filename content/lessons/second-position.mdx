2nd position has no landmark of its own — no thumb touch, no hand-frame copy. You find it by
ear, or by shifting partway back down from 3rd. That's exactly why this course teaches it
*after* 3rd and 5th, not before: it's easiest to learn once you already have two solid
positions to triangulate from.

## What it's actually for

2nd position rarely earns its own long passage the way 3rd or 5th do. Its real job is
**smoothing** — reaching a note just outside 1st position's comfortable top without the bigger
jump to 3rd, or avoiding a string crossing that would otherwise interrupt a slur. You'll meet it
most often as one note in an otherwise 1st- or 3rd-position phrase, not as a place you settle
into for long.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Shift up from 1st position on the D string toward 3rd, but stop halfway — check your 1st
  finger against F (2nd position) using the example above or a drone. Then do the same on the A
  string, stopping at C. Un-shifting partway down from 3rd is often the more reliable way to
  find 2nd, since you already have a known position to measure the distance from.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Practising 2nd position as if it needed its own repertoire, the way 3rd and 5th do. It doesn't —
its value is entirely in smoothing single moments inside music that's otherwise anchored in 1st,
3rd or 5th. Spend your practice time on the *transition* into and out of it, not on dwelling
there.
