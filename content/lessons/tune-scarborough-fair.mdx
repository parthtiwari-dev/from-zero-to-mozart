*Scarborough Fair* is a very old English ballad, and it's the classic example of a tune in
**Dorian mode** — a minor key with **one note lifted**. This is a traditional setting, in
first position, in **3/4** (a slow waltz feel — one strong beat, two lighter).

## The key: E Dorian

E Dorian is E natural minor with the **6th degree raised** — **C♯** instead of C. That one
change makes the tune sound less mournful than plain minor, more open and wistful. Same key
signature as G major and E minor (one sharp); the C♯ is written in as an accidental.

## The usual mistake

Playing the C♯ flat, out of the habit that "it's a minor tune so the 6th should be low." In
Dorian it's high — that's the point of the mode. Place it as a proper high 2nd finger, up
against the 3rd. The other mistake: choppy separate notes. Scarborough Fair should sound like
one long, unhurried breath per phrase.
