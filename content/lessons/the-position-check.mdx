Unlike the Foundation check, it doesn't sit at a hard gate either: the Intermediate gate is
still ahead, at the end of Stage 8b, after Stage 7's vibrato. This is just an honest look at
whether Stage 6's ground is solid before you keep building on it.

## What Stage 6 asked of your hand

**1 · Find 1st, 3rd and 5th position cold, no notes to check against first.** The thumb-touch
landmark for 5th, the hand-frame copy for 3rd — both should be muscle memory by now, not a
search.

**2 · Play a stopped 3rd, a stopped 6th, and an octave, each in tune.** Not perfect — in
tune enough that the wiggle-to-lock check settles into a still, beat-free ring within a couple
of tries.

**3 · Hold a simple 3-note stopped chord**, spread across three strings, without the shape
collapsing before the bow finishes rolling through it.

**4 · Trill evenly** on at least one finger pair, starting slow and controlled rather than
rushed and blurry.

**5 · Touch the two easy natural harmonics** — the octave and the two-octave — cleanly enough
that they ring rather than thud.

**6 · Check a shift by degree, not just by feel.** This ties straight back to Stage 8's ear
gym: when you land a shift, you should be able to tell — by ear, against a drone or an open
string — whether you actually arrived on the scale degree you meant to, not just "somewhere
that felt about right."

## Where this leaves you

If most of that felt solid, the fingerboard is genuinely open to you now — not just in theory,
but in the hand. Stage 7 (vibrato) builds directly on this: you can't add a controlled
oscillation to a hand that isn't yet secure in the position it's sitting in. If a few things
still feel shaky, that's normal for the most purely physical stage this course has asked of
you yet — circle back to whichever movement felt least solid before pushing on.
