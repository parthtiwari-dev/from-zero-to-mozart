An **ornament** is shorthand. A tiny symbol — or a little cluster of small-print notes —
standing in for a quick flurry of extra notes that decorate the written one.

The symbol tells you **which** ornament it is. Exactly how to play it — how fast, which note
to start on, how long — depends on **when the music was written**. That's the thread running
through this whole movement:

> **The symbol tells you *what*. The era tells you *how*.**

## Read now, perform later

Reading an ornament — knowing its name and its rough shape — is a Stage 3 skill, and this
movement builds it. **Playing** trills and turns cleanly and up to speed is a left-hand skill
and it belongs to **Stage 6**. As everywhere in this course, "read it" and "play it" are
separate milestones.

## The inventory

*(P = the written note · U = the step above it · L = the step below)*

| Symbol | Ornament | Rough shape |
|---|---|---|
| **tr** (often with a wavy line) | trill | P and U, alternating fast, for the note's length |
| a short **zigzag**, no line | upper mordent | **P–U–P**, one quick flip |
| a short **zigzag with a vertical line** | lower mordent | **P–L–P**, one quick flip |
| a lazy sideways **S** | turn | **U–P–L–P** |
| a small note, **no slash**, slurred to P | appoggiatura | a leaning note, on the beat, taking time from P |
| a small note **with a slash** through the stem | acciaccatura | crushed in fast, before the beat |
| two small rising notes into P | slide | **L–L–P**, filling in from below |
| a **vertical wavy line** before a chord | arpeggio / rolled chord | the notes played fast, bottom to top |

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play G–A–B–C plain. Now, on the B, add a quick flick up to C and back — "B–C–B" as fast as
  you can, then straight on to the C. You've just played an upper mordent. An ornament is
  never mysterious; it's a couple of small notes played quickly.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Trying to lock in the one "correct" way to play an ornament from the symbol alone. For a trill
especially, where you start and how you shape it change with the period and the harmony — the
next lessons handle that. For now: name the family, know the rough shape, and don't over-think
the execution.
