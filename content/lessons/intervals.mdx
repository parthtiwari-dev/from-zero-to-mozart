The distance between two pitches is an **interval**, named by a number (how many letter-names
it spans, counting both ends) and a quality (major, minor, perfect, augmented, diminished).

| Interval | Example from G | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| unison | G → G | same note |
| major 2nd | G → A | a whole step |
| major 3rd | G → B | the gap in a major chord |
| perfect 4th | G → C | |
| perfect 5th | G → D | the open-string distance — G to D |
| major 6th | G → E | |
| major 7th | G → F♯ | one step short of the octave |
| octave | G → g | same letter, double the frequency |

Intervals are the vocabulary your ear actually uses — reading pitch on the page tells you the
letter names, but recognising "that's a 5th" or "that's a step" by ear is what makes sight-reading
and tuning fast instead of laborious. You'll build that skill properly in Stage 8's ear-training
work; for now, just connect the number-names to what you hear.
