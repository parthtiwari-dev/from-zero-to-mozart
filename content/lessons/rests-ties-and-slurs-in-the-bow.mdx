Three of the marks you've been reading — the rest, the tie, the slur — each ask something
specific of the **bow**. Knowing what, before you play, saves a lot of stumbling.

## A rest — the bow stops, and resets

At a rest, the bow **stops moving** (a short rest) or **lifts off and comes back** (a longer
one, or the end of a phrase) — a *retake*. Either way the silence is active: you use it to
get the bow set for the next note.

## A tie — don't re-bow

Across a tie, the bow **keeps travelling in the same direction**, no new stroke, no
re-articulation. The sound just continues.

## A slur — one bow, several notes

Under a slur the bow travels once while the **left-hand fingers** do the note changes. The
rhythm is exactly as written; only the bow-changes move.

## The rule of the down-bow

An old orchestral convention: the **downbeat tends to take a down-bow**. It's not a law, but
when a bowing isn't marked, starting the strong beat with a down-bow usually feels right — and
when you're reading, a down-bow mark near the start of a bar is a hint about where beat 1 is.

## The usual mistake

Re-bowing across a tie (two attacks where the music wants one). Filling a rest with sound
because the bow didn't actually stop. Changing bow in the *middle* of a slur. All three come
from reading the notes but not the curve or the gap around them.
