The violin is not held up by the left hand. It's held between your collarbone and your jaw,
with the shoulder rest bridging the gap underneath. The left hand is free to move.

## How it sits

The back of the violin rests on your **collarbone**, tucked in against your neck. Your **jaw**
settles onto the chin rest — not clamped, just resting, with the weight of your head. The
scroll points out to your left and slightly down or level, roughly nose height. Angle the
violin so the strings are close to flat, tilted just enough that the bow can reach the G string
without hitting your leg.

## The test

If the setup is right, you can **take your left hand off the neck entirely** for a few seconds
and the violin stays put, held by your head. Try it. If it droops, your chin rest or shoulder
rest needs adjusting, or your head isn't resting its weight.

## The trap

The temptation is to *squeeze* — jam the violin between chin and shoulder so it can't fall.
Don't. A clamped hold travels straight down your arm and into your bow, and everything stiffens.
Aim for the lightest contact that still holds it. You'll spend months refining this, and so
does everyone.
