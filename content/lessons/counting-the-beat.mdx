You need a word for every beat and every division of a beat, said out loud, so your voice
and your foot can hold the pulse while your hands are busy with the notes. The standard
system is just numbers and a few small words:

- **Quarter notes** — one per beat: "**1, 2, 3, 4**".
- **Eighth notes** — two per beat: "**1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and**" (say "and", or "&").
- **Sixteenth notes** — four per beat: "**1 e and a, 2 e and a**".
- **Held notes** — keep the number running: a half note is "**1 – 2 –**", a whole note is
  "**1 – 2 – 3 – 4 –**". The count never stops, even when the note does.

## Out loud, with your foot

Counting silently in your head is not the same — the moment a passage gets hard, a silent
count drifts. Say it aloud and tap your foot on the numbers. Your tabla practice already
does exactly this: the hand keeps the cycle while the other hand plays. Keep that habit;
just use the Western numbers here, so what you count matches what's on the page and what a
teacher would say.

## The metronome

Set it slow — 60 to 70 — and play only as fast as you can play **accurately**. Speed comes
from repetition at a tempo you can already manage, never from pushing. If the metronome and
your playing keep drifting apart, the metronome is right.

## The usual mistake

Counting only the beats you play a note on, and going quiet on the "&"s and through long
notes and rests. The count has to run **continuously** — that unbroken count is the thing
keeping you in time.

  is the same instinct: a spoken, tapped pulse that runs under everything you play.
</Cite>
