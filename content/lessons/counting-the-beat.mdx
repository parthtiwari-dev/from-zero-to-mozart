You need a spoken word for every beat and every division of a beat, so your voice and your
foot can carry the pulse while your hands are busy with the notes. The standard system is
just numbers and a few small words.

| You're playing | You count | Per beat |
|---|---|---|
| quarter notes | "**1, 2, 3, 4**" | one |
| eighth notes | "**1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and**" | two |
| sixteenth notes | "**1 e and a, 2 e and a**" | four |
| a held note | keep the numbers running: half = "**1 – 2 –**", whole = "**1 – 2 – 3 – 4 –**" | — |

Say "and", or "&". Say "e" as *ee* and "a" as *uh*. The words don't matter; the steadiness
does.

## Out loud, and with your foot

Counting silently in your head is not the same thing. The moment a passage gets hard, a
silent count drifts and you don't notice. Say it aloud, tap your foot on the numbers. Your
tabla practice already does exactly this — one hand keeps the cycle while the other plays.
Keep that habit; just use the Western numbers here so what you count matches the page and
what a teacher would say.

## The metronome

Set it slow — 60 to 70 — and play only as fast as you can play **accurately**. Speed comes
from repeating something you can already do cleanly, never from pushing. If your playing and
the click keep sliding apart, the click is right.

## The usual mistake

Counting only the beats you actually play a note on, and going silent on the "and"s and
through the long notes and rests. The count has to run **continuously** — that unbroken
count *is* the thing keeping you in time. A held note is not a rest from counting.
