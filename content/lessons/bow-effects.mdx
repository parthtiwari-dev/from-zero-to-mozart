Not strokes so much as **sound colours** — ways of using the bow to change the timbre, usually
marked with a word. REFERENCE: recognise them on the page, don't drill them.

| Marking | What you do | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| tremolo | repeat one note as fast as possible, unmeasured (shown by slashes through the stem) | shimmer, tension, film-score menace |
| sul ponticello | bow right up against the bridge | thin, glassy, eerie |
| sul tasto (flautando) | bow over the fingerboard | soft, hollow, flute-like |
| col legno | tap or draw the **wood** of the bow on the string | dry, percussive clicks |
| au talon | play at the frog | heavy, powerful |

One trap worth flagging: **tremolo slashes look like beams**. A single-note tremolo puts short
diagonal strokes *through one note's stem* (repeat that note fast); a two-note tremolo puts
beam-like strokes *between two notes* (alternate rapidly between them). Neither is the same as
ordinary rhythmic beaming, which connects a group of *different* short notes to be played once
each. Check whether the strokes touch a stem (tremolo) or sit between noteheads as a rhythm
group (beams).
