Your daily reps for the scale-degree gym. **Set the drone to the key's tonic** (the practice
bar now has G, D, A, C, B and E), let each drill set home, then name the target notes — out
loud, or sing them back in numbers first. **Reveal only after you've committed to an answer.**

Work one wave until it's reliable across every key here, then move up. A few minutes most
days beats a long session once a week.

## More drills

This is a seed set — one or two per wave and key. Extend it by transposing each drill to the
other tonics the practice bar supports (G / D / A / C major, E / B minor), and by making the
target strings longer as you improve. When you can take a random seven-note fragment down in
numbers reliably, you're ready for real melodic dictation (`taking-a-tune-down`).

<Cite source="functional ear-training method (waves of stable then active tones); Karpinski, 'Aural Skills Acquisition'; the drone / sargam tradition">
  The wave structure — tonic triad first, then tendency tones by their resolution, then all
  seven in random order — is the standard functional-ear progression. The drone under every
  drill is the non-negotiable part: it's what makes the degrees mean something.
</Cite>
