When a big chunk of a piece comes back unchanged, notation doesn't reprint it. Instead, a few
Italian words and two symbols send you **jumping around the page** — back to the start, back
to a marked spot, or forward to a tail. Reading music like this is following arrows.

## The marks

| Mark | Italian | Sends you |
|---|---|---|
| **D.C.** | *da capo* — "from the head" | back to the **very beginning** |
| **D.S.** | *dal segno* — "from the sign" | back to the **𝄋** (an S with a slash and two dots) |
| **Fine** | "the end" | this is where you **stop** (even though there's more printed after) |
| **𝄌** + "**To Coda**" / "**al Coda**" | *coda* — "tail" | when you hit *To Coda*, **jump forward** to the matching **𝄌** and play the tail to the end |

## The four routes

- **D.C. al Fine** — back to the top, play until *Fine*, stop.
- **D.S. al Fine** — back to the sign, play until *Fine*, stop.
- **D.C. al Coda** — back to the top, play until *To Coda*, jump to **𝄌**, play the tail.
- **D.S. al Coda** — back to the sign, play until *To Coda*, jump to **𝄌**, play the tail.

## A worked route — D.S. al Coda

Imagine a piece with a **𝄋** at bar 3, a **To Coda 𝄌** at bar 10, a **D.S. al Coda** at bar
18, and a **𝄌 Coda** section at bars 19–22.

| You play, in order | Which printed bars |
|---|---|
| the opening | bars 1–18 |
| jump back to the sign | → bar 3 |
| from the sign to *To Coda* | bars 3–10 |
| jump to the Coda | → bar 19 |
| the Coda to the end | bars 19–22 |

Twenty-two printed bars; thirty-something played.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take any song with *D.C. al Fine* or *D.S. al Coda* printed on it. Before playing a note,
  number the bars **1, 2, 3…** in the order you'll actually play them — writing "1, 2, 3, …,
  1, 2" for a da capo. Then play from your numbers, not the layout.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Taking the *D.C.* jump but sailing straight past **Fine** and playing to the end of the page.
Missing a *To Coda* and never jumping. And — the subtle one — not knowing whether to take the
inner repeats on the way back through: that's the next lesson.

<Cite source="Grove, on the da capo aria and dal segno">
  The habit comes from 18th-century opera. In a **da capo aria** (A–B–A) the third section was
  never written out — the composer just wrote *da capo*, and the singer returned to the top,
  adding ornaments. The instrumental **Minuet–Trio–Minuet** is the same idea for players.
</Cite>
