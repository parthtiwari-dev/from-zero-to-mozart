Vibrato is the first thing in this course that has a real **readiness gate** — not a gate that
locks anything (nothing here does — `RULES.md` #4), but an honest checklist. Vibrato built on a
hand that isn't ready doesn't add expression; it just wobbles whatever was wrong underneath.
This lesson is four things to check before you start Movement B.

## 1 · You play in tune in first position, without tension

Vibrato moves the finger *around* a pitch. If the pitch itself is a guess, the wobble has no
centre to return to — you get a note that's vaguely sour *and* shaky. Test it:

## 2 · Your left hand is relaxed

Specifically: the **thumb is not clenched** against the neck, and the **wrist is not
collapsed** inward (it should be roughly straight, or gently arched away from the neck — never
folded in so the wrist bone touches the violin). A clenched thumb or a collapsed wrist
physically cannot vibrate — the motion needs a free, springy hand. If yours grips, that's the
thing to fix first (back to Stage 0's `the-left-hand-frame`).

## 3 · You can hold the violin without your left hand

The plain test: play a note, then **take your left hand right off the neck.** The violin should
stay level, held between your collarbone and jaw. If it drops, your hold is still leaning on
the left hand — and a hand that's propping up the instrument can't also be rocking freely.
Fix the hold before the vibrato.

## 4 · You've started shifting

You don't need 3rd position to be polished, but the hand should already know how to **travel
the neck loosely** — which is exactly what Stage 6 built. A hand that can shift is a hand
that's given up gripping. This is why vibrato comes now and not in Stage 2.

## The usual mistake

Rushing past this because vibrato is the exciting one. A year of playing with a shaky,
tense-handed vibrato is much harder to undo than a few more weeks of plain, in-tune,
relaxed first position. If two or three of these aren't solid yet, that's your practice list —
and it's a short one.
