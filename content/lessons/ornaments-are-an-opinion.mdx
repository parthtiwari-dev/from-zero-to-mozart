An ornament symbol is an instruction **with room in it**. Two skilled players, or two editors,
can realise the same `tr` differently and both be right. When you read an ornament, you're
reading a question as much as an answer.

## What you'll actually see

| In an… | The trill looks like |
|---|---|
| **Urtext** | just **tr** — the composer's mark, nothing added. You decide. |
| **teaching / performing edition** | **tr**, *and* the trill written out in small notes above the staff — the editor's recommended realisation |

That editorial small-print is why the same concerto in a **Flesch** edition and an
**Oistrakh** edition can carry different ornament realisations. Neither is "the text" — they're
readings of it.

## Read now, decide later

Reading the symbol — and spotting where an editor has stepped in — is Stage 3. **Playing** the
ornament is Stage 6. Choosing *which* realisation you want is the interpretive work of Stage 7
and the repertoire modules — and it's genuinely **your** call. Ornaments are one of the first
places a player gets to choose.

## The usual mistake

Treating the editor's small-print realisation as the text — it's a strong suggestion, not
gospel. And the reverse: ignoring a helpful realisation just because it's editorial. Read
both — the composer's symbol, and the editor's reading of it — and know which is which.
