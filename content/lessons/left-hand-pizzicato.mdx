A pluck with a **left-hand finger** instead of the bow-hand's — a genuinely different gesture
from the `pizz.` you already know (Stage 5), and one that lets you pluck a note while the bow
is still in motion somewhere else, or busy holding a different note.

## The `+` sign

A small **`+`** printed above or below a note means: pluck this one with a spare left-hand
finger — usually the 4th (pinky), since it's the one most often free. Nothing about the bow
changes for that note; it simply doesn't touch the string.

## Why it's a genuinely different skill

Ordinary `pizz.` (Stage 5) is a bow-hand decision — you set the bow aside and pluck with the
same hand that normally bows. Left-hand pizzicato lets the **bow keep doing something else
entirely** while a single note gets plucked on the side — useful in orchestral writing where a
plucked note has to slot into a still-moving bowed line, and, at the opposite extreme, in
virtuoso writing (Paganini-style passages use the same `+` sign for rapid-fire effect).

## The usual mistake

Reaching for the bow out of habit when a `+` appears. The whole point of the sign is that the
bow does *not* touch that note — if you catch yourself bowing it, you've missed the mark
entirely, not just played it a little rough.
