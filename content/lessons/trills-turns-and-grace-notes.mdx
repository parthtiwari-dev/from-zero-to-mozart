A closer look at the three ornaments from the last page — what they actually ask for, once you
get past the symbol.

## Trill

A trill alternates the written note with the note **one step above** it, starting either on
the main note or the upper note depending on style and edition — Baroque and early-Classical
performance practice (the exact tradition Leopold Mozart's *Violinschule* devotes three
chapters to) often starts **on the upper note**; later Classical and Romantic trills more often
start on the main note. If the upper note needs a sharp or flat that isn't in the key
signature, a small accidental is printed right above the trill sign.

## Grace notes

A tiny note before the main one comes in two flavours, told apart by how it's drawn:

- **Acciaccatura** (a grace note with a slash through its stem) — as fast as possible, barely
  taking time from the main note.
- **Appoggiatura** (no slash) — takes a real, noticeable chunk of the main note's time, more
  like a genuine extra note that resolves into the one after it.

## Turn

A turn packs a short four-note shape around the main note — typically upper neighbour, main
note, lower neighbour, main note again — played quickly. An inverted turn (the symbol upside
down) reverses the direction: lower neighbour first.

None of this needs to live in your fingers yet. **Recognise which ornament you're looking at,
and that its exact realisation is genuinely style-dependent** — that alone puts you ahead of
someone guessing from the shape of the squiggle.
