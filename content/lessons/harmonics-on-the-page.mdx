A **harmonic** is a note you get by touching the string **lightly** instead of pressing it
down. The string then vibrates in halves, or thirds, or quarters, and you hear a pure, high,
whistling, flute-like tone. The page marks them in two ways.

Producing harmonics cleanly is **Stage 6** — this lesson is just about recognising them.

## Natural harmonics

Played on an **open** string, touching a node — the halfway point (an octave up), the third
point (a twelfth up), and so on.

| On the page | Means |
|---|---|
| a small **circle (○)** above the note | play this as a natural harmonic |
| the notehead drawn as an **open diamond** | same — the diamond shows the touch point |
| a **0** plus a finger number above a high note | 0 = it's a natural harmonic; the number = which finger touches |

## Artificial harmonics

Made on a **stopped** string, so you can get a harmonic on any note. Written as **two
noteheads on one stem**:

- a normal **oval** — where the **1st finger presses firmly**;
- a **diamond a fourth above it** — where the **4th finger touches lightly**.

The note that actually sounds is **two octaves above the pressed (oval) note**. A small note in
parentheses sometimes spells out that resulting pitch.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Rest your 4th finger very lightly on the D string, up where the string passes over the end
  of the fingerboard — no weight at all, just contact. Bow fairly fast, fairly near the
  bridge. You should get a clear high ring an octave above open D. If you get a dull thud,
  you're pressing; if you get nothing, bow faster and closer to the bridge.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

**Pressing** the string — you get the ordinary stopped note, not the harmonic. The touch has
to be feather-light. Bowing too slowly or too far from the bridge, so the harmonic won't
speak. And reading the **diamond** as the pitch you'll hear — it's only the *touch point*;
the sound is two octaves higher.

<Cite source="Stage 4 (the harmonic series) explains why harmonics exist; standard string writing">
  Every harmonic is a note already present in the string's overtone series. Composers reach
  for them when they want a colour with no weight in it — glassy, distant, weightless. Ravel
  and Debussy are full of them.
</Cite>
