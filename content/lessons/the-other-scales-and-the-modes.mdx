A reference card. Major and minor are the spine of everything in this course — but they aren't
the only scales. These are the ones to **recognise**, not drill.

## Other scale families

| Family | What it is | Character | An anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chromatic** | all 12 half steps | no tonic, pure colour and connection | the "creeping" line in a suspense film score |
| **Whole-tone** | six notes, every step a whole step | dreamy, weightless — no leading tone, no home | Debussy |
| **Major pentatonic** | ^1 ^2 ^3 ^5 ^6 (no half steps) | open, folk, "can't sound wrong" | *Amazing Grace* (mostly); *Auld Lang Syne*; the piano's black keys |
| **Minor pentatonic** | ^1 ♭^3 ^4 ^5 ♭^7 | blues and rock riffs | *Sweet Child O' Mine* riff |
| **Blues** | minor pentatonic + a ♭^5 "blue note" | vocal, bent, expressive | any 12-bar blues |
| **Octatonic / diminished** | 8 notes, alternating whole–half | eerie, ambiguous, symmetrical | Stravinsky; horror scores |

## The seven modes

Take the major scale and **start it on a different degree** — same seven notes, a new note as
home. The half steps now fall in different places relative to the tonic, so each has its own
character.

| Mode | Start on ^ | = major or minor, with… | Character | Heard in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ionian** | 1 | the major scale itself | bright, settled | everything |
| **Dorian** | 2 | minor with a **raised 6th** | minor but hopeful | *Scarborough Fair*; folk; "So What" |
| **Phrygian** | 3 | minor with a **♭2** | dark, Spanish | flamenco |
| **Lydian** | 4 | major with a **♯4** | floating, wondrous | film themes (*The Simpsons* opening leap) |
| **Mixolydian** | 5 | major with a **♭7** | bright but earthy | folk, bagpipe tunes, rock |
| **Aeolian** | 6 | the natural minor itself | melancholy | folk; minor-key pop |
| **Locrian** | 7 | minor with **♭2 and ♭5** | unstable, rare | almost nothing — a curiosity |

## The big idea

There is **no finite list of "all scales"** — other traditions have their own, and composers
invent more. This card covers the standard Western families. The one point to carry: **same
notes, different home, different music** — it's the mirror of relative keys, except here you
*stay* on the unusual home note.

<Cite source="the modes — Heinrich Glarean, Dodecachordon (1547), extending the medieval church modes; the other families are standard 20th-century harmony (Persichetti)">
  Glarean's system of modes predates major/minor. Ionian and Aeolian eventually became "major"
  and "minor"; the other five stayed as modes and are alive in folk, jazz and film music.
</Cite>
