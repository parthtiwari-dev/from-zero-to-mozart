You don't need much, and most of what you own will outlive you if you build two or three
habits early. Here's the kit and the care.

## What you actually need

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Violin + bow | A full-size (**4/4**) for most adults. If your arm is short, try a **7/8**. |
| Rosin | A cake of dried tree sap — the bow makes almost no sound without it. |
| Case | Hard case, kept closed. Not a car boot, not next to a radiator. |
| Soft cloth | To wipe rosin dust off after every session. |
| Tuner | An app is fine. See the next lesson. |
| Shoulder rest | Optional but usual — fit it, don't just clip on the first one. |
| Music stand | So you're not hunching over a table. |
| Spare set of strings | They break, usually at the worst moment. |

## Rosin

Draw the cake along the hair a few times — a little more near the frog. It's a **light
dusting, not a coating.** A brand-new bow might need a dozen strokes to wake up; after that,
just a few every second or third session, when the sound starts to feel slippery.

<Margin>
  A rosin-starved bow slides off the string and barely speaks. An over-rosined one grabs too
  hard — a gritty, crunchy sound, and a cloud of white dust every stroke.
</Margin>

## Looking after it

Three habits, from day one:

- **Loosen the bow** every time you finish (you met this in the last lesson).
- **Wipe it down** — a dry cloth over the strings and the belly of the violin after each
  session. Rosin dust left to sit hardens and dulls the varnish.
- **Keep it stable** — out of direct sun, off radiators, out of hot cars and very dry rooms.
  Wood cracks when it dries out fast; in a dry winter, a small case humidifier helps.

Strings last roughly **six months to a year**, or until they sound dull, feel rough under the
finger, or start to fray. If you hear a new buzz, or the bridge starts leaning forward, take
it to a luthier rather than poking at it yourself.

<TryThis>
  Rosin your bow now — five to ten light strokes, evenly along the hair. Play an open string.
  If it still slips, a few more. When you finish, loosen the bow and wipe the strings.
</TryThis>
