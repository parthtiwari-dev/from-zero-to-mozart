## Size

Most adults play a full-size (**4/4**) violin. If your arm is short, a 7/8 exists and is worth
trying — you should be able to hold the violin up with your jaw and still curl your left hand
comfortably around the scroll with the arm not locked straight.

## The rest

A **shoulder rest** clips across the back and fills the space between the violin and your
collarbone; a **chin rest** sits over or beside the tailpiece. Fit matters far more than brand
— the goal is to hold the instrument up with almost no clamping. If your neck aches or your
shoulder creeps up, the setup is wrong, not you. It's worth adjusting this with a teacher
early.

## Rosin

Draw the cake along the hair a few times, more at the frog. It's a light dusting, not a
coating — a brand-new bow might need a dozen strokes, then only every few sessions after. Too
much rosin makes a gritty, crunchy sound and a cloud of dust.

## Looking after it

Loosen the bow. Wipe the rosin dust off the strings and the belly with a dry cloth — left on,
it hardens and dulls the varnish. Keep the violin out of car boots, direct sun and radiators;
wood cracks when it dries out fast. If you hear a new buzz or the bridge starts leaning, take
it to a luthier rather than poking at it.
