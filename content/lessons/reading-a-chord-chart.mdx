Outside the classical world, most music is written as a **melody line with chord names above
it** — G, C, D, Am, E7. Stage 4's `reading-a-chord-symbol` taught you what each name spells.
This lesson is what a **violinist** does with a chart like that, because you'll meet them the
moment you play with other people.

## Three things the chart lets you do

1. **Play the melody, and know the harmony.** The tune is the notes on the staff. The chords
   tell you *why* those notes sound the way they do — bar 1's B and G are chord tones of G,
   bar 2's C and A sit over a C chord. You're reading two layers at once: `hearing-the-chord-
   under-the-tune` (Movement F) is the ear version of this.

2. **Play a chord tone or the bass, if asked.** In a group, someone may want you on the
   *root* (play G under "G", C under "C") or holding a **chord tone** as a pad. You can do
   that straight off the chart — the name tells you the three notes.

3. **Sketch an accompaniment.** Under "G" you can play a slow **G–B–D arpeggio**; under "C",
   C–E–G; under "D", D–F♯–A. A gentle broken-chord pattern that follows the chart is a real,
   useful thing to contribute, and it's how you learn to *hear* a progression from the inside.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take the first chart (G – C – D – G). Play the written melody once. Then ignore the melody
  and play just the **root** of each chord — G, C, D, G, one long note per bar. Then play each
  chord as a slow up-and-down arpeggio. Same four bars, three different jobs, all read off the
  same four chord names.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Ignoring the chord names because "I only play the tune." The names are how you stay oriented
when the melody rests, how you join a jam, and how you start hearing harmony instead of just
melody. The other mistake: playing a full 3-note chord on every beat under each symbol —
heavy and clumsy; a violin sketches harmony with *broken* chords and held chord tones, not
block chords.

  A lead sheet assumes the player will *realise* the harmony, not just read it. For a
  melody instrument that means: play the tune, or outline the chord, or hold a chord tone —
  your choice, changing by the moment, all from the same row of names.
</Cite>
