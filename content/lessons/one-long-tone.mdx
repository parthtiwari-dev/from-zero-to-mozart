The first thing to actually build: **one sustained, even sound from frog to tip and back** —
called *son filé*, a "spun" tone. It sounds simple and it's the hardest thing on this page,
because the bow is not naturally even.

Why it's hard: near the **frog** the bow is heavy and wants to dig in; near the **tip** it's
light and wants to fade. To keep the sound even you have to *add* a little arm weight as you
travel toward the tip and *release* it as you come back to the frog — a constant, subtle
adjustment. And the change of direction at each end has to be seamless, with no bump or gap.

This is the drill every serious player keeps doing forever. Hilary Hahn, Galamian's students,
conservatoire first-years — all of them spend time every day on long slow bows on open strings.
Do four of these at the very start of each practice session.
