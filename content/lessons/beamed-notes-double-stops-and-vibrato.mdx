This is the confusion that started this whole course. Somewhere in a Vivaldi line there's a
little knot of tightly-drawn notes, and it's not obvious whether it means *two fast notes*,
*two notes at once*, or a *wobble*. Three different things, and you tell them apart by looking
at one thing: **which way the noteheads sit.**

| What you see | What it means | Not to be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| noteheads **side by side**, joined by a **horizontal beam** | play them **one after another**, fast — the beam just groups them by beat | a double stop |
| noteheads **stacked vertically**, one on top of another at the same spot | play them **together** — two strings at once — a **double stop** | a beam |
| a wavy line, a `tr`, or nothing at all, on **one** held note | **vibrato** — the pitch shimmers; there are **never extra noteheads** for it | either of the above |

separate notes, played quickly, in order.

## Three layers, always

When a line looks impossible, split it into three questions and answer them separately:
**pitch** — which notes? **rhythm** — how long is each? **bowing** — one bow or separate?
Sort those one at a time and the knot comes apart.

## The usual mistake

Freezing at a cluster of small notes and reading it as a chord, or deciding it must be
"vibrato" and skipping it. Look at the beam: side-by-side means one-after-another. Then slow
down and read the notes.
