A **cadence** is a short chord pattern that punctuates a phrase — musical punctuation, the
equivalent of a full stop, a comma, or a question mark. In G major, using the primary chords
from the last page:

  caption="Plagal cadence: IV then I — the 'Amen' cadence from hymn endings. Softer than a perfect cadence."
  defaultBpm={50}
/>

<NotatedExample
  abc={`X:1
L:1/2
K:G clef=treble
[GBd] [DFA] |]`}
  labels={[{ name: "I" }, { name: "V" }]}
  caption="Half cadence: ends on V, unresolved — a comma, not a full stop. It wants to keep going."
  defaultBpm={50}
/>

<NotatedExample
  abc={`X:1
L:1/2
K:G clef=treble
[DFA] [EGB] |]`}
  labels={[{ name: "V" }, { name: "vi" }]}
  caption="Deceptive cadence: V resolves to vi instead of I — sets up a full stop, then sidesteps it. A small surprise."
  defaultBpm={50}
/>

You don't need to identify these by ear instantly yet — just recognise that phrase endings
aren't random, and that "sounds finished" (perfect), "sounds like a comma" (half), and "sounds
like a twist" (deceptive) are three genuinely different, namable events.
