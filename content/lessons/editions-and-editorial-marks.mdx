Two copies of the "same" piece can look different, and it's worth knowing why before it
confuses you.

An **edition** is a specific published version of a score. Beyond the composer's actual notes,
an edition typically adds: fingerings, bowings, dynamics the composer may not have written,
suggested tempo, and sometimes a cadenza (a whole passage written by someone other than the
original composer). Different editions of Mozart's K.216, for instance, can suggest different
fingerings for the same fast passage, or a different cadenza entirely.

Publishers sometimes mark **editorial additions** with brackets `[ ]` or parentheses `( )`
around a dynamic or a note, signalling "this wasn't in the composer's original — an editor
added it for guidance." An **urtext** edition tries to strip all of that back to exactly what
the composer wrote, with editorial suggestions kept clearly separate (often in smaller print or
above the staff) rather than blended in.

None of this is REFERENCE trivia for its own sake: when you eventually sit down with a real
K.216 score, **the first thing worth doing is identifying which edition you're holding**, so
you know which markings are Mozart's and which are an editor's opinion.
