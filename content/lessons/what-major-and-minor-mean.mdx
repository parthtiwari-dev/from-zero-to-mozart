"Major" and "minor" are not moods. They name an **interval structure** — a particular pattern
of steps — and the note that decides which one you're in is the **third degree**.

## It's the 3rd

From the tonic up to the 3rd:

- a **major 3rd** = 4 half steps (two whole steps) → the scale is **major**;
- a **minor 3rd** = 3 half steps (a whole step and a half) → the scale is **minor**.

G major's 3rd is B. G minor's 3rd is B♭. Move that one note down a half step and the whole
scale changes character.

Minor also lowers the **6th and 7th** degrees compared to major — so the full natural-minor
recipe is **1 2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7**. But the 3rd is the one your ear registers first as "it went
minor."

## The natural minor scale

Its step recipe is **W – H – W – W – H – W – W**. (It's the major-scale pattern started from
its 6th note — more on that in *Relative and parallel*.)

The one you'll actually play this stage is **E natural minor**: E F♯ G A B C D E. It has one
sharp (F♯) — and on the violin it uses the **exact same first-position hand shape as the G
major scale.** You already know the fingering.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  On the A string, play A (open), then B (1st finger), then C♯ (high 2) — that's ^1 ^2 ^3 of A
  major, a major 3rd from A up to C♯. Now slide the 2nd finger back to C natural (low 2) — ^1
  ^2 ♭3 of A minor, a minor 3rd. Back and forth, listening. The half-step slide of one finger
  is the whole difference between major and minor.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

"Minor means sad." It doesn't. Plenty of minor-key music is fierce, driving, or triumphant
(think of a fast folk fiddle tune), and plenty of major-key music is heartbreaking. The key
sets up an interval structure; the *mood* comes from tempo, rhythm, dynamics, melody and
touch. Don't play every minor piece mournfully by reflex.

<Cite source="Gioseffo Zarlino, Le istitutioni harmoniche (1558); the user's own Violin Reading Book, Part V">
  Zarlino identified the major and minor third as the two "harmonic" thirds that give chords
  and scales their bright or dark colour. And as the source book puts it: "Major does not
  literally mean happy. It describes an interval/key structure."
</Cite>
