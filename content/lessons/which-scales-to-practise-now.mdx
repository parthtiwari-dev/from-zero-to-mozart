Not all at once. Here's the order, and where you are in it.

## The ladder

| Stage | Scales | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **NOW** | **G, D, A, C major** (1 octave); **E natural minor** (1 octave) — plus the matching **arpeggios** | all first position; this is your target for this stage |
| **NEXT** | **G major, 2 octaves** (still first position — the shape twice); **F** and **B♭ major** (the low-1 pattern); **E harmonic and melodic minor** | as your fingering vocabulary grows |
| **LATER** | the full major/minor set; two octaves everywhere; **broken 3rds**; the **chromatic** scale; faster bowings | |
| **ADVANCED** | three-octave scales and arpeggios; **double-stop scales** (3rds, 6ths, octaves) | Stage 6 and beyond, once shifting is in place |

The other four (G, D, A major, E minor) all appear as full annotated lines on the practice
page and in *Scales and arpeggios to play*. Rotate through two or three of them per practice
session, plus their arpeggios.

## A benchmark, not the curriculum

The graded exam systems (ABRSM, RCM) ask for roughly this same set at their first grades:
D and A major one octave, G major two octaves, E minor one octave, with separate and slurred
bowings. That's a useful **external check** on where you are — but it's a benchmark, not your
syllabus. Your syllabus is this course.

And a reminder from the last lesson: those systems test **scales and sight-reading
separately**, because they're different skills. A session needs both.
