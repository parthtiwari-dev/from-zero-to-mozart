The advanced off-string strokes, as a map. All LATER — you're building toward these, not
practising them yet.

| Stroke | What it is |
|---|---|
| Sautillé | a fast springing stroke — the bow stays near the string and the *stick* does the bouncing at speed, rather than the arm lifting it. Grows out of a fast détaché. |
| Ricochet (jeté) | you throw the bow and let it bounce several times **naturally** in one bow direction — used for rapid repeated-note figures |
| Flying staccato | many staccato notes in one bow direction, each with a tiny bounce |
| Flying spiccato | spiccato notes carried in one bow direction rather than alternating |
| Fouetté | a whipped stroke — the bow is lifted and flicked down onto the string with an accent |
| Viotti stroke | an up-bow accent on the second of a slurred pair, a signature Classical-era bowing (you'll meet it in Mozart) |

The thing to notice: almost all of them are refinements of strokes on the last three pages,
sped up or made automatic by the bow's own physics. That's why the on-string foundation
(détaché, martelé, staccato, the long tone) is worth so much time — every one of these is built
on it.

Watch a video encyclopedia of bow strokes if you're curious what they sound like. You are
**not** meant to master 24 bow strokes in your first year — nobody does.
