Flags and beams do the same job: they mark notes shorter than a quarter. A lone short note
gets a **flag**; a group of them gets a **beam** — a thick bar across the stems — instead.

- **One** flag or beam = an **eighth note** — two per beat. Count "1 **and**".
- **Two** = a **sixteenth note** — four per beat. Count "1‑e‑&‑a".
- **Three** = a thirty-second note. Rare; recognise it and move on.

## Beams show you the beat

Here's the useful part. Notes are beamed **in beat-groups** — a bar of eighths in 4/4 is
beamed in pairs, one pair per beat; sixteenths are beamed in fours. So the beaming itself
tells you where beats 1, 2, 3, 4 fall. When a run of small notes looks like a wall, look at
where the beams break — each break is a new beat.

## The usual mistake

Reading a beam as "play all these in one bow." A beam is **rhythm only** — it groups notes by
time, nothing else. Whether beamed notes share a bow is a separate mark: a curved **slur**
line (next lesson). A beam and a slur can even sit on the same notes and mean different
things.

<TryThis>
  Clap a slow, steady four. Now fit two claps into each beat (eighths), then four (sixteenths),
  without letting the underlying four speed up. That steady four is what the beams are drawn
  against.
</TryThis>
