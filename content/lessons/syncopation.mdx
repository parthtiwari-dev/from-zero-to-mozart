Syncopation is putting the emphasis somewhere **other** than the strong beat you'd expect —
without breaking the pulse underneath. It's the rhythmic surprise that makes a line feel
alive instead of mechanical, and it's everywhere: fiddle tunes, ragtime, pop, and plenty of
Haydn and Beethoven.

Your ear for this is already good — tabla is built on the play between the theka's stress and
a phrase's stress. What's new is only how it's *written*.

## The ways it's written

- a **rest on the downbeat** — the note you expected on "1" arrives on the "&" instead
- a note **struck on an off-beat** and held across the next strong beat
- an **accent mark** on a weak part of the beat
- the **back-beat** — the stress moved onto beats 2 and 4

## The usual mistake

"Straightening" the syncopation — quietly pulling the off-beat note back onto the beat
because it feels wrong. Don't. Keep the pulse rigid **underneath** — foot, metronome, count —
and let the note sit exactly where it's written. And: don't drop the count during the tied or
rested beat; that's where syncopation falls apart.
