The bow hold looks fussy and is worth getting roughly right, because a locked bow hand kills
the sound and a floppy one loses control. Most players use the **Franco-Belgian** hold; here it
is.

## The hold, finger by finger

- **Thumb**: bent, tucked into the little gap where the stick meets the frog. This is the
  anchor. It stays curved — a straight, stiff thumb is the most common beginner fault.
- **Middle two fingers**: draped over the stick, wrapping down its far side. The ring finger's
  tip sits roughly over the frog's pearl dot.
- **First finger**: a little separated from the middle two, contacting the stick around its
  middle joint. This is where you'll add and release weight.
- **Pinky**: curved, balanced on *top* of the stick near the frog. It counterbalances the tip
  and helps you lift the bow.

Hold it like a small bird: secure enough that it won't escape, gentle enough that you won't
hurt it.

## Other holds exist

The **Russian** and **German** holds differ in the first finger and thumb. Your teacher may
prefer one; none is wrong. Start with Franco-Belgian and adjust later if needed.
