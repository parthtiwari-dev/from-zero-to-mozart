When a melody jumps instead of stepping, that jump is an **interval**. You've been naming
intervals on paper since Stage 4 (`what-an-interval-is`). Hearing one is easier than it
sounds, because you already have the tool: an interval is just **two scale degrees**.

## "That's ^5 up to ^1"

Don't hear a leap as an abstract distance. Hear both ends as degrees:

- The famous *Twinkle* opening jump is **^1 up to ^5** — a perfect 5th, but you don't need
  the name; "one to five" *is* the sound.
- *When the Saints* starts **^1 up to ^3** — a major 3rd.
- *Amazing Grace*'s pickup is **^5 up to ^1** — a perfect 4th.

If home is set and you know your degrees (Movement E), naming a leap is just naming its two
endpoints. The interval name is a label you can add later.

## Three forms

- **Ascending** — the low note first. Easiest.
- **Descending** — the high note first. Harder; practise these deliberately, they lag.
- **Harmonic** — both notes together. You hear it as a *colour* (a rich 6th, a hollow 5th, a
  grinding 2nd) rather than a motion.

## The anchor songs are training wheels

"Perfect 4th = *Here Comes the Bride*" works to get started, but it's slow (you hum a whole
song to check one interval) and it fails for descending or harmonic intervals. Use the anchors
to bootstrap, then drop them for the **degree** method as soon as you can. On the violin
there's a third layer: every interval is also a **hand shape** — a 5th is "the next string,
same finger," an octave is "across two strings" — so your fingers start recognising them too.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Drone on G. Play ^1 (G), then leap to a random other degree and hold it. Name the degree
  you landed on — that names the interval. Do ten ascending, then ten *descending* (start on
  the high note). The descending ones will feel much shakier; that's normal, and it's exactly
  why you drill them separately.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Learning intervals as free-floating pairs with no key — then they don't transfer to real
melodies, which always have a key. Anchor them to degrees. The other mistake: assuming
descending intervals will come along for free with the ascending ones. They don't; a
descending 6th is genuinely a different recognition task from an ascending 6th.
