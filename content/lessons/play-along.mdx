The final step of playing by ear, and one of the best practice tools there is: playing
**along with a recording** — matching its pitch and its time, in real time.

## Why it's the top of the ladder

Playing along does two hard things at once:

- **It's the ultimate "keep going."** You physically cannot stop — the track doesn't wait.
  It's the same forcing function as duet sight-reading (`the-sight-reading-habit`), but
  relentless. Lose your place and you *must* recover on the next downbeat and rejoin.
- **It's the ultimate ear check.** If your note is out of tune or off the beat, you hear the
  clash immediately, against a fixed reference that never drifts. There's nowhere to hide.

Do it once you can find the tune by ear (`learning-a-tune-by-ear`) — first learn the tune
slowly and alone, then put the track on and lock to it.

## How to do it

1. **Learn the tune by ear first**, slowly, drone-checked. Don't try to sight-play along cold.
2. **Match the key.** Find the recording's tonic; if it's in an awkward key, use a
   pitch-shift app to move it to G, D, A or C.
3. **Start slow.** Many apps (or a video player) let you slow a track without changing its
   pitch. Begin at 60–70% speed.
4. **Lock the pulse first, pitches second.** If you have to choose in the moment, stay in
   *time* and let a wrong note go — rejoin on the next beat.
5. **Bring the speed up** a little at a time as it locks in.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take a tune you already play — Ode to Joy, a folk tune. Find a simple recording of it,
  slowed to about two-thirds speed and in your key. Play along, all the way through, no
  stopping. Notice where you drift — usually a specific phrase — and where the intonation
  fights the track. Those spots are your next practice targets.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Trying to play along with a full-speed recording of a tune you don't really know yet — you
end up chasing it, always a beat behind, learning nothing. Learn it alone first, then slow
the track, then match. The other mistake: stopping every time you slip. The entire value is
in *not* stopping — recover and rejoin.
