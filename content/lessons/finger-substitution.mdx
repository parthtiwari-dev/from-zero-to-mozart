Changing which finger holds a note **while it's still sounding**, with no new attack and no
audible bump. It's a small, almost invisible move — used to free up a finger for what comes
next, or to set up a smoother shift a beat later.

## Why it's harder under the bow

In pizzicato, a finger substitution is nearly free — the string isn't ringing continuously the
way a bowed note does, so any small noise from the swap gets masked by the pluck's own decay.
Under the bow, the note is **sustained and exposed** the whole time, so any bump, scratch, or
pitch wobble during the swap is fully audible. That's what makes this genuinely harder than it
looks on paper.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play a long, held D-string E with your 1st finger. Partway through — bow still moving evenly —
  silently bring your 2nd finger down onto the exact same spot and lift the 1st finger away.
  Listen closely for any change in pitch or a click at the moment of the swap. Repeat until it's
  genuinely silent.
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

Rushing the swap. A fast substitution is far more likely to bump the pitch or scrape the
string than a slow, deliberate one — there's no reward for speed here, only for the swap being
inaudible.
