You will get lost. Everyone does — a run collapses, you skip a bar, suddenly you have no idea
where you are. The skill isn't *not* getting lost; it's **getting back in, fast, without
stopping.**

## The downbeat is the lifeline

When it falls apart:

1. **Keep the pulse going** — in your head, or with your foot. The beat doesn't stop just
   because your hands did. This is why `the-never-stop-rule` matters: as long as the pulse
   survives, you have somewhere to come back to.
2. **Look ahead to the next strong beat** — usually the next **downbeat** (beat 1 of the next
   bar). Or, if a section marker or a repeat sign is close, aim for that. These are your
   **regrouping points**.
3. **Re-enter there, in time.** Drop everything you missed in between. Land on that downbeat
   with the right note and the right beat, and you are *back* — reading again, not scrambling.

## Skilled sight-reading is mostly skilled *faking*

You know the shapes now (`the-patterns-in-real-music`) — scales, arpeggios, sequences. So
even when you can't read every note, you can play *something close* in the right rhythm and
land the important notes. That's not cheating; it's what fluent reading is. "Get the gist,
keep the pulse, nail the strong beats."

## The strong-beat priority

When you can't get everything: **get the downbeat right. Then the other strong beats. The
notes in between can be approximate.** A line with every strong beat correct and the weak
beats fudged sounds *musical*. The reverse sounds like a car crash. This also tells your eye
where to spend its attention — on the beats that carry the bar.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Take a line that's a little too fast for you and play it at that tempo, on purpose, so you
  *do* get lost. Each time you lose it, practise only the recovery: keep the count, find the
  next downbeat, re-enter. After a few goes, getting lost stops being scary — it's just "drop
  a bit, land on one."
</TryThis>

## The usual mistake

When you get lost, **slamming on the brakes and hunting for your place note by note.** By the
time you've found it, four bars have gone by and the pulse is dead. Don't hunt — *aim*: pick
the next downbeat, keep counting toward it, arrive. The other mistake: trying to catch up by
cramming the missed notes in fast. Let them go.
