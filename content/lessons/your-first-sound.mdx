## The stroke

Set the bow on the **A or D string** at the middle, hair flat on the string, halfway between
bridge and fingerboard. Pull a slow down-bow to the tip, then push back up. Let the **weight of
your arm** rest into the string through a relaxed hand — you're not pressing, you're leaning.
Keep the bow moving in its straight lane, parallel to the bridge, the whole length.

You want three things: a clean start (no scratch before the note), one even sound the whole
stroke, and a clean stop.

## Reading the sound

- **Whistling or glassy** — too close to the bridge, or not enough weight for the speed.
- **Scratchy, crushed** — too much weight, or the bow crooked.
- **Thin, airy, hard to keep going** — too close to the fingerboard, or too light.

Adjust one thing at a time.

## Crossing strings

To move to the next string, lift or lower the **whole arm** to a new level — like stopping a
lift on a different floor. The hand and bow just come along. Small, smooth, from the shoulder.

<TryThis>
  Four slow down-up bows on each string, G to E and back to G. Full bow, middle lane, arm
  weight. Listen for one unbroken sound per stroke. Do this at the start of every practice for
  a while.
</TryThis>
