No left hand yet — just the bow, an open string, and a steady sound. This is the lesson Stage 0
has been building toward.

## The stroke

Stand, hold the violin, hold the bow. Set the **flat hair** on the open **D** string, at the
**middle of the bow**, in **lane 3** (halfway between bridge and fingerboard). Pull a slow
down-bow to the tip, then push back up.

Let the **weight of your arm** settle into the string through a relaxed hand. You are not
pressing — you're **leaning**. Keep the bow moving in its straight lane, parallel to the
bridge, the whole length.

You want three things from every stroke: a **clean start** (no scratch before the note), **one
even sound** the whole way, and a **clean stop**.

## Reading the sound

If it isn't right, the sound tells you which dial to move — bow **speed**, arm **weight**, or
**contact point**:

| You hear | Usually means | Try |
|---|---|---|
| Whistling, glassy | too near the fingerboard, or too little weight for the speed | a little more arm weight; move toward lane 3; slow down |
| Scratchy, crushed | too much weight, too near the bridge, or a crooked bow | lighten; move toward the fingerboard; check the bow is straight |
| Thin, airy, hard to keep going | too light, or the bow tilted onto its edge | flatten the hair; let the arm settle in |

Change **one thing at a time.**

## Crossing strings

To move to the next string, raise or lower the **whole arm** to a new level — like stopping a
lift on a different floor. The bow hold stays exactly as it was.

## The usual mistake

- **Pressing** instead of leaning — the sound chokes and scratches;
- **racing the bow** — thin and whistly;
- a **crooked bow** — the sound wanders and thins toward the tip;
- a **tilted bow** (only part of the hair down) — weak, surface-y;
- **holding your breath** or hunching — everything stiffens.

<TryThis>
  Four slow whole bows on each string, G to E and back to G. Then loop the D–A–D crossing ten
  times. Do this at the start of every practice session for the next few weeks — it's the
  five minutes that trains your tone.
</TryThis>

<Cite source="Leopold Mozart, Violinschule (1756)">

  tone rests first on **bow speed** and a freely vibrating string, not on pressure.
</Cite>
