The course is called *from zero to Mozart*. The Mozart is the **Violin Concerto No. 3 in G
major, K.216** — and its solo violin entrance is the note this whole stage has been quietly
preparing you to read.

## Everything on that page, decoded

| What's printed | What Stage 4 lets you say |
|---|---|
| **one sharp** in the key signature | G major **or** E minor — and since the orchestra has been in G and the tune rests on G, it's **G major** *(Movements B, D)* |
| the **stacked chord** — three noteheads, one stem | a **G major triad** — scale degrees ^5, ^3, ^1 — rolled bottom to top because the bridge is curved *(Movement G; Stage 3)* |
| the **whole opening gesture** | the **G major arpeggio**, the tonic triad broken — *a shape you practise every day* *(Movement J)* |
| the **"2"** printed by the G | **second finger**, first position. Not "2nd position" (that's a Roman "II"). Not "the 2nd string." *(Movement J — the four numberings)* |
| the **F♯ → G** dip | **^7 → ^1** — the leading tone leaning home; realised as a short Classical turn *(Movements A, F; Stage 3)* |
| **f** at the entrance | loud, *relative to the orchestra* — a dynamic, not a decibel level *(Stage 3)* |

## Read versus play

A Stage-4 graduate can **read** all of that — name every note, string, finger, degree, chord
and marking. **Playing** it — in tune, with vibrato on the G, a controlled roll, the Classical
taste — is Stage 6, Stage 7, and the full Mozart module, which stays a long way off.

Those are different milestones, and you should feel the first one land. **"I can't even read
the first note of Mozart" is no longer true.** That was the whole point of this stage.

## Try it

<TryThis>
  Play the simplified entrance: roll the chord bottom to top (open D, B on the A string, G on
  the E string), holding that high G. Then the dip — F♯ (1st finger, E string) and back to G.
  Slowly, no vibrato, just clean. You are reading and playing the opening gesture of a Mozart
  concerto. Sit with that for a moment.
</TryThis>
