How you practise decides how fast you improve — far more than how long. The core of it is a
small loop you run on every single thing you work on.

## The loop

1. **What** — pick *one* thing to fix. Not "the piece" — the bow staying straight through the
   string change; the left hand not squeezing; two notes that aren't even.
2. **Why** — know what's wrong and what "right" sounds and feels like. If you can't tell, you
   can't fix it.
3. **Slow** — play it at a speed where you can get it right, in the **smallest useful chunk** —
   two notes, one bar.
4. **Repeat** — loop that chunk until it's *easy*, then a little faster. Speed is what happens
   when accuracy stops being hard; it's never the goal.
5. **Rest** — shake the hands out. Then the next thing, or the next chunk.

<Margin>
  If you're missing more than about **one attempt in five**, you've taken on too much — slow
  the tempo or shrink the chunk. Repeating a mistake just teaches the mistake.
</Margin>

## A 30-minute session

| Minutes | Work |
|---|---|
| 5 | **Tone** — open strings, straight bow, an even relaxed sound |
| 7 | **Reading** — 5 to 10 notes: name → string → finger |
| 7 | **Scale or arpeggio** of the week — listen for the pitch |
| 7 | **A piece or étude** — one to four bars, not the whole thing |
| 4 | **Review** — one weak rhythm or bowing pattern |

With 45–60 minutes, don't just double everything: add a second short piece block and a
dedicated rhythm-and-ear block.

## The rules that matter

- **Short and often beats long and rare.** Fifteen to twenty focused minutes most days beats
  two hours on Sunday. Two or three short sessions in a day is better still.
- **Warm up.** Open strings, slow bows, a scale you know. Cold hands learn badly.
- **No pain, ever.** Aching, pinching or tingling means stop, shake out, check the setup. Mild
  muscle tiredness is fine; sharp or nerve pain is not.
- **Stop before it falls apart.** Tired, frustrated practice is worse than none. End on
  something that worked.
- **Sort the rhythm first.** Before you pick the violin up, clap and count the rhythm of what
  you're about to play.

## The usual mistake

Playing tunes start to finish, over and over — that just rehearses the mistakes. Also:
practising fast before it's clean; practising through pain; hour-long grinds; sitting down
with no plan.
