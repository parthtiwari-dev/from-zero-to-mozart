How you practise decides how fast you improve, more than how long you practise.

## The rules that matter

- **Short and often beats long and rare.** Twenty focused minutes most days beats two hours on
  Sunday.
- **Warm up.** Open strings, slow bows, a scale you know. Cold hands learn badly.
- **One small thing at a time.** Four bars, not the whole piece. A single shift. One bow
  stroke. Fix it, then widen.
- **The 20% rule.** If you're missing more than about one attempt in five, you've bitten off
  too much — slow the tempo or step back a level. Repeating mistakes just teaches them.
- **Stop before it falls apart.** Tired, frustrated practice is worse than none. End on
  something that worked.

Hilary Hahn talks about "100 days of practice" — the point isn't the number, it's that small,
regular, honest sessions compound.

## How this course is tagged

Every page carries one tag:

- **NOW** — build this skill now.
- **NEXT** — add it once your current work is stable.
- **LATER** — it matters for real repertoire and Mozart, but it's premature today.
- **REFERENCE** — know what the word means so a score doesn't look alien; don't drill it.

And one distinction runs through everything: being able to **read** a passage — name every note,
symbol and rhythm — is a different achievement from being able to **play** it. You'll reach the
first long before the second, and that's exactly the plan.
