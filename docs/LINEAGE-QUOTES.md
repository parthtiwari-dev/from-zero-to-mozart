# Lineage quotes

Verified quotes and close paraphrases for **beat 6** ("where it comes from"). Each concept
lesson pulls one from here so the citation is written from checked material, not memory
(`RULES.md` #6, #9). Add a line when you verify a new one; note the source and how sure we are
of the exact wording.

Format: **exact quote** in quotes, or *close paraphrase* in italics, then the source, then a
confidence note.

## Leopold Mozart — *Versuch einer gründlichen Violinschule* (1756)
English: *A Treatise on the Fundamental Principles of Violin Playing*, tr. Editha Knocker,
OUP 1948/1985. Public-domain German + facsimile on IMSLP; first digital critical edition at
mozarteum.at.

- *"A beginner should always play firmly, with all his strength, energetically and sonorously —
  never weakly and timidly."* — on first tone production. Source: maiteiglesias.com summary of
  the treatise; **paraphrase, close** — confirm exact Knocker wording before it ships in
  `your-first-sound`.
- *"Only with time and patience will the harshness of the sound disappear and a clean sound be
  achieved."* — same section. Same source; **paraphrase, close.**
- *A young student should be made to beat the time carefully — keeping the quarter-notes in
  mind — before he takes the violin in his hand.* — the rhythm-first principle. Source: widely
  cited across pedagogy literature (stringsmagazine.com, CSUN thesis, violinist.com); **stable
  paraphrase**, exact Knocker wording to be confirmed for `how-to-practise` / Stage 2's
  `rhythm-before-the-instrument`.
- *He describes two ways of holding the violin and two of holding the bow, and shows the
  correct position of each.* — Ch. II. Source: Wikipedia (Violinschule), scholarworks CSUN;
  **factual, well-attested.**
- *The treatise opens by describing the string instruments known to him and the parts of the
  violin, before any playing.* — Introduction / Ch. I. Source: Wikipedia; **factual.**
- *He valued steady, assured, tasteful playing, free of gaudy effects and tricks that disguise
  poor technique.* — his stance on virtuosity/ornament. Source: stringsmagazine.com
  ("A Reexamination of Leopold Mozart's Famous Violin Pedagogy"); **paraphrase, close.**

## Ivan Galamian — *Principles of Violin Playing and Teaching* (1962)

- *Tone is governed by three things the player controls together: bow speed, bow weight
  (pressure into the string), and the sounding point — where the bow crosses the string
  between bridge and fingerboard.* Source: violinist.com pedagogy blogs, stringvisions
  (Ovation Press), simonfischeronline; **well-attested paraphrase of Galamian's model.**
- *The space between bridge and fingerboard divides into five sounding points — point 1 at the
  bridge, point 5 at the fingerboard — each about the width of the bow hair (~10 mm). Nearer
  the bridge is louder with more core; nearer the fingerboard is softer and flautando.* Source:
  Flesch/Galamian as summarised by simonfischeronline, violinlounge; the ff–f–mf–mp–p "lanes"
  labelling from violinist.com (Brian Lewis pedagogy class). **Well-attested.**
- *He taught a modified Franco-Belgian bow hold — a flatter wrist, the index a little apart
  from the middle finger.* Source: violinspiration, CSUN Franco-Belgian thesis; **factual.**
  His lineage: Capet → Galamian → DeLay → Perlman, Zukerman, Kyung-Wha Chung.

## Carl Flesch — *The Art of Violin Playing* (1923/1930)

- *He distinguishes the "old" German, the "newer" Franco-Belgian, and the "newest" Russian bow
  holds, and illustrates each.* Source: sarahwallinhuff bowhold comparison, violinist.com;
  **factual.**

## Simon Fischer — *Basics* (1997), *Practice* (2004), *The Violin Lesson* (2013)

- *"The single most important factor in positioning the left hand is which part of the
  fingertip contacts the string."* Source: thestrad.com (Fischer / Rachael Ridge guidance),
  simonfischeronline "Positioning the left hand"; **close paraphrase of Fischer.**
- *If the thumb presses sideways against the neck the fingers counter-press, and it feels like
  squeezing; keep the thumb as light as possible.* Source: same; **close paraphrase.**
- *The most open, freely-speaking, resonant tone is built first on bow speed.* Source:
  simonfischeronline; **close paraphrase.**

## Kató Havas — *A New Approach to Violin Playing* (1961)

- *Her method is built on balance and the release of tension — the violin and bow are
  balanced, not gripped.* Source: general pedagogy literature; **paraphrase**, exact wording

## Hilary Hahn — interviews, #100DaysOfPractice (2017–)

- *"I invited people to do their own #100daysofpractice, for the process rather than the
  results."* Source: violinist.com, stringsmagazine.com; **exact quote (Hahn, Instagram/
  interview).**
- *Even at her level, practice is basic finger patterns, a few bars at a time, under tempo,
  slow long bows on open strings, then shaking out the hands — "light, meandering practice."*
  Source: violinist.com "100 Days of Practice with Hilary Hahn"; **close paraphrase, "light,
  meandering practice" is her phrase.**

## Shinichi Suzuki — Suzuki Method

- *Teach one string completely, and put a real tune in the child's hands almost immediately,
  before mixing strings.* — the mother-tongue / sequence principle. Source: Violinspiration
  Suzuki overview, method literature; **paraphrase of the method's approach.**

## Standards / facts (not a person, but citable)

- **A = 440 Hz** is the modern international concert-pitch standard (ISO 16, 1955; adopted
  1939). The violin's four strings — **G3, D4, A4, E5** — are a stack of **perfect fifths**,
  inherent to the instrument's design.
- The **"rule of the down-bow"** (Baroque): the first beat of the bar takes a down-bow. Source:
  general performance-practice literature.
