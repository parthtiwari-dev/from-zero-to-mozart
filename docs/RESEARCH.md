## The pedagogical spine — teaching in a real lineage

### Leopold Mozart — *Versuch einer gründlichen Violinschule* (1756)
Literally the method Mozart was raised on (published the year Wolfgang was born). A textbook
"written as much for the teacher as the student". Covers rudiments of theory and technique
through difficult shifts, positions up to 7th, and double stops. **Three chapters on
ornamentation** (all the 18th-century embellishments). Draws heavily on the Italian school
(Tartini). Its philosophy is our north star:

- *solid technique + a developed sense of musicianship valued above feats of virtuosity;*
- *"a young student should be taught to beat the rhythm carefully, keeping the quarter-notes in
  his mind, before taking a violin in hand"* — **rhythm before the instrument**, which also
  plays straight to this user's strength and the konnakol idea.

Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versuch_einer_gr%C3%BCndlichen_Violinschule) ·
[Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/A-Treatise-on-the-Fundamental-Principles-of-Violin-Playing) ·
[IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Versuch_einer_gr%C3%BCndlichen_Violinschule_(Mozart,_Leopold))
(primary source to read in Phase 0).

### The French school → the modern canon
Baillot / Rode / Kreutzer (*Méthode du Conservatoire*, 1803, and the étude bibles still used
today) → **Auer** *Violin Playing as I Teach It* (1921) → **Flesch** *The Art of Violin Playing*
+ *Scale System* → **Galamian** *Principles of Violin Playing & Teaching* (tone-production and
bow model). Auer/Flesch/Galamian (1921–1962) are the three central modern texts; each builds on
the French conservatoire tradition (Galamian is a third-generation Baillot student via Capet).
Source: [comparison of Auer/Flesch/Galamian pedagogy (UT Arlington thesis)](https://www.deviolines.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/septiembre/umi-uta-1292.pdf) ·
[JMU: treatises of Leopold Mozart, … through time](https://commons.lib.jmu.edu/diss201019/92/).

### Suzuki + Hilary Hahn
Suzuki = "mother-tongue" method: daily listening, simple repertoire that plants future skills,
a fixed repertoire sequence. **Hilary Hahn:** started Suzuki (~age 3–4, ~18 months at Peabody) →
**Klara Berkovich** from ~age 4, *two lessons a week*, practical and clear, "did many études,
especially Wohlfahrt", phrasing present from the start → **Curtis / Jascha Brodsky** from age 10
(Kreutzer, Ševčík, Gaviniès, Rode, Paganini Caprices). Hahn credits Suzuki with lifelong
listening habits and has said "every note began with the Twinkle Variations". Her public "100
days of practice" ethic → the practice-science library.
Sources: [The Strad — Hahn & the Suzuki method](https://www.thestrad.com/playing-hub/planting-seeds-for-my-relationship-with-music-hilary-hahn-and-the-suzuki-method/13504.article) ·
[Interlude — Berkovich & Hahn](https://interlude.hk/sorcerers-apprentice-klara-berkovich-hilary-hahn-tell-forget-teach-will-remember/) ·
[WRTI — Hahn's earliest influences](https://www.wrti.org/post/violinist-hilary-hahn-remembers-her-earliest-influences).

### Modern reference
**Simon Fischer** — *Basics*, *Practice*, *The Violin Lesson*, *Tone*. Violinspiration
(structured beginner pedagogy, good scaffolded note-reading). ABRSM / RCM / Trinity graded
syllabi as external sanity checks (not as the curriculum).

## Graded-curriculum research

### Where K.216 sits
Technically upper-intermediate / early-advanced (positions 1–5, shifting, vibrato, Classical
bowing, 3-octave scales, trills, some double stops). Musically diploma-level — "technically
accessible at a relatively modest level, musically accessible at a very high level." It's on
the ABRSM diploma (ARSM / DipABRSM) repertoire list; roughly RCM Level 9–10 / Eastman Level V /
end of Suzuki Book 7 + a student-concerto ladder. So the full journey ≈ **absolute beginner →
ABRSM Grade 8 → then K.216**, a 7–9 year road for a child, compressible with adult focus.
Sources: [Wikipedia — Violin Concerto No. 3 (Mozart)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin_Concerto_No._3_(Mozart)) ·
[violinist.com — is Mozart 3 difficult?](https://www.violinist.com/discussion/archive/21891/) ·
[IMSLP — K.216](https://imslp.org/wiki/Violin_Concerto_No.3_in_G_major,_K.216_(Mozart,_Wolfgang_Amadeus)).

### Eastman Community Music School violin curriculum (Levels I–VI) — the most useful skeleton
- **I (beginning):** posture, coordination, first-position bow strokes (détaché, staccato,
  legato, slurs). Applebaum / Whistler scale collections. Suzuki 1–2, Wohlfahrt Op.38.
- **II (early intermediate):** double stops, natural harmonics, shift to 3rd position, colle &
  martelé. Hřímalý, Sitt, Ševčík Op.1 pt.1, Schradieck. Kayser Op.20, Wohlfahrt. Küchler Op.15,
  Rieding Op.21/34/35, Seitz 2 & 5, Suzuki 2–4.
- **III (intermediate):** vibrato coordination, positions 1–3 solid + intro 4–5, chords &
  trills. Flesch Scale System begins. Mazas Op.36 Bk1, Trott. Handel Sonata No.3 in F, Vivaldi
  A minor & G minor, Fiocco Allegro.
- **IV (advanced):** first five positions, continuous vibrato, 3-octave scales + 7-arpeggio
  sequences, 2-octave double-stop scales. Dont Op.37, Kreutzer 42. Accolay No.1, Bach A minor,
  Monti Csárdás, Handel Sonatas, Haydn G major, Kreisler.
- **V (upper advanced):** 3-octave scales to ±3 sharps/flats with fine intonation, seven
  positions. Kreutzer, Rode Caprices. Bach E major, Bach S&P movements, **Mozart Concerto in G
  major (with cadenza)**, Vivaldi Spring, Viotti No.23, de Bériot *Scène de Ballet*.
- **VI (pre-professional):** all keys, Dont Op.35, Gaviniès. Bach S&P complete, Bruch, Lalo,
  Mendelssohn, Mozart 4–5, Wieniawski 2.
Source: [ESM Community Music School violin curriculum](https://www.esm.rochester.edu/community/faq/student-curriculum/violin/).

### Suzuki Violin School — full sequence (referenced, not reproduced)
- **1:** Twinkle Variations · Lightly Row · Song of the Wind · Go Tell Aunt Rhody · O Come
  Little Children · May Song · Long Long Ago · Allegro · Perpetual Motion · Allegretto ·
  Andantino · Etude · Minuet 1–3 (Bach) · The Happy Farmer (Schumann) · Gavotte (Gossec).
- **2:** Chorus from Judas Maccabaeus · Musette · Hunter's Chorus · Long Long Ago (theme+var) ·
  Waltz (Brahms) · Bourrée (Handel) · The Two Grenadiers · Theme from Witches' Dance (Paganini) ·
  Gavotte from Mignon · Gavotte (Lully) · Minuet in G (Beethoven) · Minuet (Boccherini).
  *New: low 1st/2nd finger, extended 4th, up-bow staccato.*
- **3:** Gavotte (Martini) · Minuet (Bach/Petzold) · Gavotte in G minor (Bach) · Humoresque
  (Dvořák) · Gavotte (Becker) · Gavotte in D (Bach) · Bourrée (Bach). *New: 3rd position,
  wider patterns, first double stops, vibrato exercises.*
- **4:** Lullaby (Schubert) · Lullaby (Brahms) · **Seitz Concerto No.2 (1st)** · **Seitz No.5
  (1st & 3rd)** · **Vivaldi A minor (1st & 3rd)** · Perpetual Motion (Bohm) · **Bach Double
  (1st mvt)**. *New: positions 2–4, vibrato development.*
- **5:** Gavotte (Bach) · **Vivaldi A minor (2nd mvt)** · **Vivaldi G minor** · Country Dance
  (Weber) · German Dance (Dittersdorf) · Gigue (Veracini) · Bach Double. *New: 5th position,
  3-octave G major/minor + arpeggios, spiccato & collé.*
- **6:** La Folia (Corelli/Suzuki) · Handel Sonata No.3 in F · Fiocco Allegro · Gavotte
  (Rameau) · Handel Sonata No.4 in D. *New: 6th & 7th positions, frequent shifting.*
- **7:** Minuet (Mozart) · Courante (Corelli) · Handel Sonata in A · **Bach Concerto in A
  minor** · Bach Gigue & Courante · Allegro (Corelli). *New: 3-octave A major/minor & C major.*
- **8:** Eccles Sonata in G minor · Tambourin (Grétry) · Bach Largo (C major sonata) · Bach
  Preludio (E minor) · Pugnani/Moffat Largo Espressivo · Veracini Sonata in E minor.
- **9:** Mozart Violin Concerto in A major, K.219 (Joachim/Suzuki). **10:** Mozart Concerto in
  D, K.218.
Source: [Violinspiration — violin pieces in the Suzuki books](https://violinspiration.com/violin-pieces-suzuki/).

### Étude order
Consensus: Wohlfahrt precedes Kreutzer; the middle order varies by teacher. A common path:
Wohlfahrt Op.45 Bk1 → Kayser & Trott → Mazas *Études spéciales* → Dont Op.37 → Kreutzer → (Rode,
Gaviniès, Dont Op.35). Beginner set also names Sassmannshaus, Dancla Op.84, Sitt, Ševčík,
Schradieck.
Sources: [Violinspiration — violin etudes by difficulty](https://violinspiration.com/violin-etudes/) ·
[violinist.com — étude order threads](https://www.violinist.com/discussion/thread.cfm?page=5460).

### Bowing-technique taxonomy
Smooth (beginner): détaché, legato, tenuto, portato, (double stops, chords). Accented: staccato,
up/down-bow staccato, martelé, sustained martelé, spiccato, flying staccato, flying spiccato,
ricochet, collé, sautillé, fouetté, jeté, chop, pizzicato, marcato, arco. Orchestral/colour:
tremolo (+ measured), sul ponticello, sul tasto, col legno, au talon.
Source: [Violinspiration — bowing techniques, terms & symbols](https://violinspiration.com/violin-bowing-techniques-terms-symbols-and-definitions/).

### Music-theory scope (grades 1–8 shape)
Rhythm & notation → keys & scales (major, 3 minors, later modes/chromatic/whole-tone/pentatonic)
→ intervals (number + quality, inversion, compound) → triads & inversions → diatonic chords,
I–IV–V, dominant 7th → cadences (perfect, plagal, half, deceptive) → phrase/period, form (binary,
ternary, rondo, theme & variations, sonata) → ornament conventions by era → foreign terms →
transposition. Sources: ABRSM & Trinity theory syllabi (structure only).
