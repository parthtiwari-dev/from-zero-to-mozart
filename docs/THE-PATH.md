# THE PATH — what this course actually is

The single source of truth for the *shape* of the journey. Read this before planning any
stage. It sits above `PLAN.md` (phasing), `CURRICULUM.md` (the stage map) and `REPERTOIRE.md`
(the song bank) and tells all three what they're for.

Written 2026-09-02 from the user's own words after Stage 0 shipped.

---

## 1. The real goal

"Zero to Mozart" is shorthand. The real goal is **the whole violin canon, in order** — every
era, every major name, from the first nursery tune to the summit works — presented as a **set
path with set practice sessions**, so that every step is a specific piece you can actually
play at a level you have actually reached. This is how real violin study works: Suzuki Book 1
is 17 pieces, each teaching one thing; a conservatoire student moves through a known ladder of
concertos and studies.

K.216 is the **named finish line** because it's musically deep but technically reachable —
diploma-level musicianship in upper-first-position hands. But the path passes through, and the
end state includes being able to **read** (and slowly play, and rewrite in our own annotated
line) the rest of the literature.

### The journey, era by era

*(Researched against the standard repertoire canon — sources at the bottom. "▶ listen" = kept
alive as a listening + "what level is this really" study long before it's playable.)*

**0 · First sounds.** Open strings, the bow path, the D–A–D crossing.

**1 · First tunes (first position).** Hot Cross Buns · Twinkle · Lightly Row · Song of the
Wind · Go Tell Aunt Rhody · Mary Had a Little Lamb · Au Clair de la Lune · French Folk Song ·
Long Long Ago · Merrily We Roll Along · **Ode to Joy** · When the Saints · folk & fiddle
tunes.

**2–3 · The Baroque & early-Classical first-position world.** Bach *Minuets*, *Musette*,
*Gavotte*; Handel *Bourrée*, chorus from *Judas Maccabaeus*; Gossec *Gavotte*; Boccherini
*Minuet*; Beethoven *Minuet in G*; Pachelbel *Canon* (simplified); *Eine kleine Nachtmusik*
theme; Bach *Air on the G String* (simplified); Vivaldi *Spring* theme.

**4 · Student concertos & Baroque sonatas (positions 1–3).** Rieding Op.35 / Op.21 · Küchler
Op.11 / Op.15 · Seitz Concertos 2 & 5 · Accolay No.1 in A minor · Vivaldi Concerto in A minor
RV 356 · Bach *Double* Concerto (2nd, then 1st violin) · Fiocco *Allegro* · Handel Sonatas ·
Corelli *La Folia* · Vivaldi *Spring* / *Winter* (full first movements). Encore-lite: Monti
*Csárdás* · Dvořák *Humoresque* · Elgar *Salut d'Amour*.

**5 · The Romantic concerto canon (positions 1–5, 3-octave scales, real style).**
**Bach** A minor & E major Concertos · **Mozart** Concertos No. 3 (**K.216 — the destination
module**), then No. 4 & No. 5 · **Haydn** G major · **Viotti** No. 22 & 23 · **Kabalevsky** ·
**Bruch** No. 1 in G minor · **Mendelssohn** in E minor · then the ▶ listen-first giants:
**Beethoven**, **Brahms**, **Tchaikovsky**, **Dvořák**, **Sibelius**, **Lalo** *Symphonie
espagnole*, **Saint-Saëns** No. 3, **Wieniawski** No. 2, **Vieuxtemps** No. 4 & 5, **Glazunov**,
**Elgar**, **Khachaturian**, **Barber**, **Korngold**, **Prokofiev** 1 & 2, **Shostakovich**
1 & 2, **Bartók** No. 2, **Berg**, **Walton**, **Nielsen**, **Szymanowski**.

**6 · The virtuoso showpieces & concert encores.** **Kreisler** — *Liebesleid*, *Liebesfreud*,
*Schön Rosmarin*, *Caprice viennois*, *Praeludium and Allegro*, *Tambourin Chinois*.
**Sarasate** — *Zigeunerweisen*, *Carmen Fantasy*, *Introduction & Tarantella*, *Navarra*,
*Caprice basque*. **Wieniawski** — *Scherzo-Tarantelle*, *Légende*, *Polonaises*. **Massenet**
*Méditation* from *Thaïs*. **Saint-Saëns** *Introduction & Rondo capriccioso*, *Havanaise*.
**Chausson** *Poème*. **Ravel** *Tzigane*. **Ernst** — *Last Rose of Summer*, *Erlkönig*
(the hardest of all).

**7 · The great sonatas.** Beethoven (the ten — *Spring*, *Kreutzer*) · Brahms (three) ·
Franck · Debussy · Ravel · Fauré · Grieg · Prokofiev · Bartók.

**8 · The unaccompanied summits** — the three peaks of solo-violin literature:
**Bach** *Sonatas & Partitas* BWV 1001–1006 (the **Chaconne**) · **Paganini** *24 Caprices* ·
**Ysaÿe** *Six Sonatas* Op. 27. Plus **Bartók** *Solo Sonata*, **Hindemith**, **Bach**'s
predecessor **Biber** (*Passacaglia*, *Rosary Sonatas*).

**9 · The end state.** You can open *any* of the above and **read it** — name every note,
string, finger, rhythm and marking — and rewrite it in our own annotated line, even where the
technique to perform it in tempo is still years away.

### The lineage of players behind the path

The course teaches "the way the great players learned," so their names run through it as the
pedagogical spine: **Corelli → Tartini → Viotti → the French school (Baillot, Rode, Kreutzer)
→ Paganini → Joachim → Ysaÿe → Auer → Flesch → Galamian & DeLay → Suzuki → Heifetz, Oistrakh,
Milstein, Menuhin, Stern, Grumiaux, Szeryng, Perlman, Zukerman, Mutter, Bell, Chang, Vengerov,
Kavakos, Ray Chen — and Hilary Hahn**, whose Suzuki-to-Curtis path is our model for the early
years (`LINEAGE-QUOTES.md`).

*Sources for the canon: violinist.com "essential standard repertoire" & "Big Five concertos";
theimaginativeconservative / Musiq360 concerto lists; Eastman Community Music School graded
curriculum (Levels I–VI); Mimi Zweig / Dorothy DeLay repertoire sequences; Wikipedia
(Bach S&P, Ysaÿe Op.27); Strings Magazine (Ysaÿe, Kreisler); udiscovermusic / Classic FM
"greatest violinists"; talkclassical "20th-century violin concertos".*

## 2. Every stage ends at real music

**Each stage's end goal is: you can play 10–30 melodies tied to that stage's skills.**

- The melodies live **in the curriculum** — woven into the lessons where a skill is taught,
  and/or clustered at the end of the stage — **and** on a **separate practice page** ("The
  pieces") so you can go straight to playing what you've unlocked.
- The same melody appears in both places; it is authored once.
- Melodies may also be released **mid-stage**, not only at the end — a tune the moment its
  notes are covered is more motivating than a wall of tunes at the finish.

## 3. How a melody is presented

**Always our format. Never a scanned or PDF-style sheet — not once, anywhere.**

- Our **annotated note-line**: the staff, plus **name / string / finger under every note**,
  one-press playback, adjustable tempo. (`AnnotatedScore`, see `CONTENT-STANDARD.md`.)
- If the user hands over a real score (a Mozart concerto, a Vivaldi arrangement), the job is
  to **read it note by note and rewrite it** in this format. The PDF is a source, never a
  thing we display.
- **Every line gets a short how-to-play note** — the minute, thought-through things:
  *where to start the bow* (frog / middle / tip), *which direction*, *which part of the bow*,
  *how much weight*, *what to listen for*. Example: *"Start at the frog with a short down-bow
  to the middle; then keep going in the upper half with a light hand — this line should sound
  like one long breath."*

## 4. Every musical symbol gets explained

The user will meet symbols in a drill before fully understanding them. **That is fine — as
long as every note-value, symbol and marking is explained somewhere in the curriculum.**

- Keep **`docs/NOTATION-COVERAGE.md`**: every symbol → the lesson that teaches it.
- A symbol may appear in an example before its own lesson **only** with a pointer
  ("you'll meet this properly in Stage N"). Nothing is left unexplained forever.

## 5. Stages go deep — one skill at a time

Adding 30 songs is not the fix on its own. Each stage is (re)built to:

- teach **one skill at a time**, raw and detailed, with the **why** — not a stated fact;
- carry **many small play-around exercises per lesson** — patterns, variations, contrasts —
  not a single four-note fragment;
- **end at the melody set** that proves the skill.

## 7. Sheet-music sourcing — the answer to "you fetch or I provide?"

- **Public-domain Tier-0/1 tunes** (nursery, folk, hymn, Suzuki-vein): **Claude notates them**
  in ABC, first position, verified against the known melody. No file needed.
- **Simplified arrangements we already hold:** `docs/source/vivaldi-spring-easy.pdf`,
  `mozart-k216-violin-part.pdf`, `mozart-k216-full-score.pdf` — **Claude transcribes from these**

- **A specific arrangement the user wants** (a Violinspiration / YouTube teacher's version, a
  particular edition): **the user sends the PDF/photo, Claude transcribes it.** Still never
  displayed as-is.
- **Modern pop / film:** targets only — link to a licensed arrangement, never reproduce.

## 8. The practice page

`/practice` (or `/pieces`) — a flat, filterable list of every melody unlocked so far:
title · key · lowest position · the one skill it drills · "was in the original PDF". Tap one →
its annotated line + per-line how-to-play notes + playback. It's the "just let me play"
entrance; the curriculum is the "teach me why" entrance. Same content.
