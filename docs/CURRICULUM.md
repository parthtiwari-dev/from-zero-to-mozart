# Curriculum content map

The full 0 → Mozart K.216 path. Ten stages. Each stage carries five parallel tracks —
**Reading / Left hand / Right hand (bow) / Ear & musicianship / practised repertoire** — and
tags technique **NOW / NEXT / LATER / REFERENCE** (NOW = practise it now · NEXT = the next
layer · LATER = real repertoire, premature today · REFERENCE = recognise the term, don't drill).

Two hard gates: **Foundation complete** (end of Stage 5) and **Intermediate complete** (end of
Stage 8). The giants (Paganini, Bach, big Vivaldi, Mozart) come *after*.

> "Read" and "perform" are separate milestones and are labelled separately everywhere. You can
> understand a Mozart page long before your hands can play it.

Source mapping to the original PDF is in `PDF-MAP.md`. Repertoire lists are in `REPERTOIRE.md`.

### Stage 0 — Orientation & setup (before a note)
*New content (PDF assumed some of this). **✔ WRITTEN — 11 lessons** + `<Diagram>` SVGs.*

- **The instrument:** every part of the violin and bow, named, with why it matters.
- **Gear:** sizing, shoulder-rest & chin-rest fit, rosin, case, strings (gut/synthetic/steel),
  care, humidity, when to see a luthier.
- **Body:** posture standing & sitting, alignment, weight, breathing, tension audits.
- **Violin hold:** collarbone + jaw, scroll height, *no left-hand support*.
- **Left-hand frame:** thumb placement, knuckle line, contact points, no squeezing.
- **Bow hold:** Franco-Belgian (default); Russian & German noted. Bent thumb, curved pinky,
  flexible fingers; the prep games (rocket, spider, bunny).
- **Tuning:** how pegs vs fine tuners work, A = 440, tuning in 5ths, using a clip-on tuner,
  string names G–D–A–E, why it drifts.
- **Bow geography:** frog / tip / middle / lower & upper half; down-bow ⊓, up-bow V; contact
  point / sounding point (Galamian's lanes); the straight-bow "highway".
- **First sound:** open-string bows, weight vs pressure, a clean start, no neighbour string;
  string-crossing arm planes.
- **How to practise:** warmup, short focused blocks, the 20%-miss rule, how long / how often,
  injury & hearing care.
- **How this course works:** the 5 tracks, NOW/NEXT/LATER/REFERENCE, "read vs perform",
  "when you're confused, jump here".

### Stage 1 — First position on the map + the staff
*PDF Parts I & II. **✔ REBUILT — `stage-plans/stage-1.md`**, Movements A–F, ~43 lessons + tunes.*

- **The staff:** 5 lines / 4 spaces; treble (G) clef; lines & spaces; higher = higher.
- **Landmarks first:** the four open strings written on the staff; ledger lines (the G string
  lives *below* the staff — violinists meet low ledger lines immediately).
- **Pitch vs duration:** vertical position = pitch, note shape = duration.
- **Note names:** the musical alphabet A–G cycles; octaves; **"which B? which C?"** — a letter
  alone is ambiguous on the violin; the staff's vertical position disambiguates. C4/C5 are
  *later* labels, not first-reading language.
- **Fingers, not pitches:** 0–4 = open/index/middle/ring/pinky. Always pair a finger with a
  string ("A string, high 2, C♯" — never just "2").
- **Low & high fingers:** no frets, a finger moves a semitone; low1/1, low2/high2, 3/high3;
  the geometry.
- **Every first-position note, string by string (G, D, A, E):** natural-note ladder + full
  chromatic placement table per string.
- **Same pitch, more than one fingering:** open D vs G-string 4, etc. **Verified same-pitch
  checks: G-str 4 = open D · D-str 4 = open A · A-str 4 = open E.** (A D-string 3rd-finger G is
  an octave *above* open G — not the same pitch. This was a correction in the original chat.)
- **Accidentals** ♯ ♭ ♮ — meaning, and which way the finger moves (D string: F is low 2, F♯ is
  high 2).
- **Key signature** — the ♯ after the clef; applies to every octave; order of sharps/flats;
  accidental inside a bar lasts to the bar-line, then resets.
- **Intonation, first pass:** ear + ringing-tone resonance + external check (tuner/drone). No
  vibrato while learning where a note lives. Tapes can help geography; the ear decides.
- **Sa ↔ letters bridge (new, for this user):** tonic / Sa as a *movable* function vs G-A-B as
  *fixed* names; why Western notation fixes the letters and moves "Do"; movable-do solfège as
  the bridge between the two ways of hearing.
- **Reading scaffold:** Stage A (name + string + finger) → B (finger only) → C (plain staff).
- **Drills, each a try-page + an answer-page:** open strings; G string; D string; A string;
  E string; mixed G/D; G major incl. F♯; D + A; all first-position landmarks.

### Stage 2 — Rhythm: when & how long
*PDF Part III. **✔ REBUILT — `stage-plans/stage-2.md`**, Movements A–I, 33 skill lessons + tunes.*

- **Rhythm before the instrument** (Leopold Mozart): keep the pulse in your head first.
- Pulse / beat / tempo. Note anatomy: head, stem, flag, beam.
- **Values:** whole → half → quarter → eighth → sixteenth (→ 32nd). **Dots:** a dot adds half
  again; dotted quarter = 1½; **duration-dot vs staccato-dot** (same shape, different place &
  job). Double dots (reference).
- **Rests** of every value; a rest is not "nothing to read" — keep the pulse, prepare the next
  bow/finger.
- **Beams vs flags;** one beam = eighths, two beams = sixteenths.
- **Beamed notes vs double stops** — the exact Vivaldi confusion: notes side-by-side connected
  by a beam = play them one after another; noteheads stacked vertically = play together.
  **Tremolo** is a third case (repeated / alternating, not measured subdivision).
- **Ties vs slurs** — same curve: tie joins identical pitches (hold as one); slur groups
  different pitches (on violin, usually one bow).
- **Time signatures:** 4/4, 3/4, 2/4, 2/2 (₵), 3/8, 6/8, 9/8, 12/8; simple vs compound;
  the **C** and **₵** symbols (don't read C as the note C).
- **Counting:** "1 & 2 &", "1 e & a" — **and konnakol / tabla-bol counting as a first-class
  option** (leans on the user's strength).
- Subdivision; syncopation; anacrusis / pickup; triplets & other tuplets; hemiola;
  swing vs straight (reference).
- Bar lines, measures, 1st/2nd endings, repeat signs, D.C. / D.S. / Coda / Fine, multi-bar
  rests.
- **Rhythm worksheets** (try + answer): quarter + eighth; eighth + sixteenth; dotted; mixed.

### Stage 3 — Everything else on the page: score symbols
*PDF Part IV. **✔ BUILT 2026-09-03 — 46 lessons, Movements A–J** (3a Expression / 3b the rest

The list below is the original scope; the built stage covers all of it and more (editions as

- **Tempo words** (Italian / German / French) + modifiers (molto, poco, meno, più, assai,
  ma non troppo); rit. / rall. / accel. / stringendo / a tempo / rubato / tempo primo;
  metronome marks.
- **Dynamics:** pppp–ffff, mp / mf; crescendo & diminuendo (words + hairpins); sf / sfz / fp;
  subito; morendo, calando, niente.
- **Articulation:** staccato, staccatissimo, tenuto, accent (>), marcato (^), fermata,
  breath mark, caesura (//).
- **Bow-direction marks:** ⊓ down (frog→tip), V up (tip→frog), retake.
- **String words:** arco, pizz., col legno, con / senza sordino, sul G/D/A/E, sul ponticello,
  sul tasto / flautando.
- **Repeats & navigation, in full:** simple repeat, 1st/2nd endings, D.C. al Fine,
  D.S. al Coda, segno, coda, "to coda", rehearsal marks.
- **Ornaments** — with **Baroque vs Classical realisation** (grounded in Leopold Mozart's three
  ornament chapters): trill (tr, with/without accidental, from above or below), mordent
  (upper / lower), turn (+ inverted, delayed), grace notes (acciaccatura vs appoggiatura),
  tremolo. Don't improvise ornaments from the symbol until you know the period.
- **Double stops & chords** notation; rolled / broken chords; divisi.
- **Harmonics** notation (small ° / diamond notehead); left-hand pizzicato (+).
- **Editions:** editorial vs composer markings; finger numbers 1–4 near notes = suggestions;
  **roman-numeral positions ≠ finger numbers**.
- Glissando / portamento marks; 8va / 8vb / loco; clef changes (reference).
- **Score-language quiz + answers** (from the PDF, expanded, with the treble clef rendering
  properly this time).

### Stage 4 — Scales, keys, major & minor: musicianship I
*PDF Part V. **✔ BUILT 2026-09-03** — `stage-plans/stage-4-research.md` (20 passes) +
`stage-4.md` (4a/4b/4c, Movements A–P, **70 lessons** = 48 concept + 7 repertoire). Build
green (231 pages). Pays the K.216 first-note debt (`the-first-note-of-mozart`). `PRD.md` §6.1: the harmony/ear thread is load-bearing for K.216 — the
research budgets real lesson count (intervals + a dedicated ear thread, triads → diatonic
harmony → the dominant 7th, cadences, phrase/period). Owns the **K.216 first-note debt**
(`LESSONS-FROM-MOZART.md` §2): after Stage 4, "I can't read the first note of Mozart" must be
impossible. The list below is the original PDF scope; the built stage will cover all of it and
much more (the ear thread, the physics, transposition, the modes, an accurate raga bridge, a
`decode`-style harmony capstone).*

- **What a scale is:** an ordered set of pitches heard from a tonic; it teaches the pitch
  vocabulary of a key, not just a finger exercise.
- **Whole & half steps;** the major formula W–W–H–W–W–W–H; build any major scale.
- **Why G major has one sharp** — derived from the formula (E–F♯), not memorised.
- **Circle of fifths** (interactive): sharps clockwise, flats anticlockwise; key-signature
  order and *why*.
- **Minor:** natural / harmonic / melodic; **parallel** (same tonic, e.g. G major vs G minor —
  different key signature) vs **relative** (same key signature, different tonic — G major's
  relative is E minor).
- **The 12 major keys** — practical spellings; the natural-minor reference set; enharmonic keys.
- **Intervals:** number + quality (M / m / P / A / d); inversion; consonance / dissonance;
  compound intervals — with the ear-training hook (Stage 8).
- **Triads** (major / minor / dim / aug), inversions; the diatonic triads of a key
  (I ii iii IV V vi vii°); primary chords I–IV–V; the dominant 7th.
- **Cadences:** perfect / authentic, plagal, imperfect / half, deceptive. Phrase & period;
  antecedent / consequent.
- **Arpeggios:** chord tones vs scale steps; G / D / A major arpeggios; broken thirds.
- **Other scale families (reference):** chromatic, whole-tone, pentatonic (major / minor),
  blues, the modes (Ionian…Locrian), octatonic / diminished. There is no finite "all scales".
- **Your scale practice order:** NOW G / D / A / C major 1-octave → NEXT E natural minor,
  2-octave G, F, B♭ → LATER the full set, 3-octave, broken thirds, chromatic, double-stop scales.
- **The physics:** harmonic series, overtones, why open strings ring, sympathetic resonance,
  just vs equal temperament, "ringing tones" as an intonation check.
- **Transposition** basics; concert pitch.
- **Scale-reading pages** (G / D / A / C major) with one practical beginner fingering each.

### Stage 5 — The bow: core strokes & tone  →  **FOUNDATION GATE**
*PDF Part VI (bowing portion). **BUILT 2026-09-04 — 36 lessons** — `stage-plans/stage-5.md`
(5a Tone 9 · 5b The strokes 16 · 5c Reading & shaping + the gate 10 · P 1). Adds the
**reading-and-planning-a-bowing** thread (`LESSONS-FROM-MOZART.md` §1) and the first
**play-by-ear** lesson (`PRD.md` §3). 4 gate tunes deferred pending PD sources.*

- **Bow physics in plain language:** tone changes with bow **speed × weight × contact point**
  (+ hair amount, bow angle, straightness); the trade-offs; Galamian's model. Near the bridge
  needs a different speed/weight balance than near the fingerboard.
- **Tone:** *son filé* — one steady sound frog → tip → frog; whole-bow control; bow
  distribution; the resonance point.
- **String crossings:** arm planes / levels; smoothing with wrist & fingers; bariolage;
  double crossings.
- **Core NOW:** détaché (+ grand / accented / lancé / porté), legato / slurred bowing, martelé.
- **NEXT:** staccato (on-string, and one-bow up-/down-bow staccato), portato / louré, collé,
  brush stroke, hooked bowing, retakes.
- **NEXT / LATER:** spiccato (controlled off-string), sautillé; the spiccato ↔ sautillé
  continuum.
- **LATER (reference-first):** ricochet / jeté, flying staccato, flying spiccato, fouetté,
  viotti stroke, chopping.
- **Colour:** tremolo (measured / unmeasured), sul ponticello, sul tasto / flautando,
  col legno (battuto / tratto), au talon.
- **Bow-stroke atlas:** one card per stroke — name · stage · notation · sound · how to practise
  · what NOT to confuse it with · a video reference. **Every card is real** (the original PDF
  repeated one identical "Practice: start on open A or D…" paragraph across ~6 pages — do not).

## Phase 2 — Stages 6–8 (PDF Part VII + new)

### Stage 6 — Left hand beyond first position
*Mostly new (PDF gestured at positions/shifting). ~18–24 lessons.*

- First-position map revisited; 4th-finger security; extensions & contractions.
- **What a "position" is** — the location of the whole left hand, not a finger. Finger number
  alone is never a complete pitch address.
- **Shifting:** release thumb pressure, arm leads, guide / ghost notes, same-finger vs
  new-finger shifts, shifting on the old bow vs the new bow, Classical vs Romantic portamento,
  making shifts silent.
- **Positions one at a time:** 3rd (the usual first shift — 1st finger goes where 3rd was),
  2nd, **4th** (why K.216 uses it — it keeps a fast line on one string), 5th, 6th, 7th;
  half position; a 1st-finger table per string per position.
- **Double stops:** open+open → open+stopped → stopped+stopped; 3rds, 6ths, octaves, fingered
  octaves, 10ths (reference); tuning the interval; the bow plane between two strings.
- **Chords:** 3- and 4-note; breaking / rolling; the curved bridge makes sustained 4-string
  contact impractical.
- **Trills & finger action:** relaxed lift / drop, accurate base pitch before speed, trill from
  above vs below, Baroque vs Classical starts, trill chains.
- Ornament execution, physically (mordents, turns, grace notes).
- **Harmonics:** natural (nodes — half / third / quarter points), artificial (touch-fourth).
- Left-hand pizzicato; glissando as effect; finger substitution.

### Stage 7 — Vibrato & expression
*PDF Part VI (vibrato portion) + new. ~12–16 lessons.*

- **What vibrato is:** a controlled oscillation *below* the pitch and back to centre, for
  expression; width vs speed; continuous vs terminal. **Not** a way to make an uncertain note
  sound musical — learn the centre of the pitch first.
- **Readiness gate:** in tune in first position, without tension. (Violinspiration's gate.)
- **Types:** arm / forearm, wrist / hand, finger — the anatomy of each. Teachers differ on
  which to learn first; this course teaches **arm and wrist**, no dogma.
- **Preparation:** silent rocking away from the instrument, "polishing", metronome pulses
  (1 → 2 → 3 → 4 per beat), all fingers, on every string, the "knock", ghost vibrato.
- **A 6-week micro-plan** (from the PDF) + the honest caveat: vibrato often takes months.
- **Integrating:** start and stop deliberately; vibrato on the long notes of an easy melody;
  vary width & speed for character; keep the pitch centre.
- **Expression more broadly:** phrasing (shape, direction, breath, arrival & departure notes),
  dynamics as a line, agogics / rubato, tone colour, character words, listening & imitation.

### Stage 8 — Sight-reading & ear training  →  **INTERMEDIATE GATE**
*PDF Part VII (thin) + a large new ear-training track. **RESEARCHED 2026-09-04** — `stage-plans/stage-8-research.md` (22 passes). Split 8a (reading fluency + ear gym, position-independent, builds next) / 8b (positions at sight + the Intermediate gate). ~34 concept lessons + card/drill ladders. Functional ear training (sargam bridge) is the core.*

- **The 20-second pre-read:** clef → key → time → tempo → lowest/highest pitch → accidentals →
  rhythm traps → slurs / bow marks → repeats → then hear & count beat 1 before the bow moves.
- **The four-layer rescue:** when a line fails, isolate the layer — say note names (no rhythm) /
  clap rhythm (no pitch) / finger silently or pizz slowly / air-bow the rhythm & slurs — then
  combine. Don't replay the whole line ten times.
- **Progressive scaffold removal** A → E, formalised (A: name+string+finger+staff … E: normal
  score, you choose fingering & bowing).
- **Reading by pattern:** steps / skips / leaps, sequences, arpeggic & scalar shapes,
  landmarks, intervals — not note-by-note. Reading ahead. "Keep going; recover on the downbeat."
- **Graded sight-reading cards** (20+ levels), each a try + an answer + a pass condition
  (steady pulse, ≥ 8/10 pitches, no panic pause > 1 beat).
- **Ear-training gym** (the user's weak axis — the biggest new investment):
  - pitch matching; drone work (sing / play against a drone); relative pitch
  - interval recognition (ascending / descending / harmonic), with reference-song anchors
  - **functional / movable-do scale-degree ear training** — bridges directly from Sa-thinking
  - chord quality & cadence recognition; major vs minor
  - short melodic dictation; rhythmic dictation (their strength — build confidence here)
  - "which note did the violin play?"; play-back-what-you-hear
  - tuning your own note against open strings and drones
- Transcription mini-projects (learn a short tune by ear).

### Mozart K.216 deep-study module
*PDF Parts VIII & IX, expanded.*

- **The work:** G major, 1775, Salzburg ("Strassburg" concerto); movements Allegro / Adagio /
  Rondeau: Allegro; the form of each (ritornello-sonata; lyrical slow movement; rondo with the
  Andante & Allegretto surprises); orchestration; **cadenza traditions** (Joachim, Auer, …).
- **Editions** and why they differ — fingerings, bowings, cadenzas, ornaments, slurs; identify
  yours before comparing.
- **Full first-page decode:** every mark categorised — pitch instruction / rhythm instruction /
  bow & articulation / dynamic / navigation / editorial text.
- **First-system annotated decoder** with a *real* legend (the PDF's was circled numbers with
  no key).
- **Milestones:** (1) category-only pass, (2) pitch landmarks + apply F♯, (3) rhythm-only clap,
  (4) technique-triage table — *can you read it now? can you perform it now?* per passage type.
- **Passage studies:** the opening solo; the scalar runs; the **fourth-position passage** (why
  4th position smooths it); the lyrical second theme; ornaments & trills; the development;
  double stops; the recap; into the cadenza.
- **Practice plan** for the movement — slow, chunked, rhythms, a tempo ladder; the honest
  "read now, perform over months / years".
- **Listening study:** Hahn, Grumiaux, Oistrakh, Perlman, Szeryng, Mutter, Zukerman —
  comparative tempo, ornament and cadenza choices; what "your own interpretive choice" means
  vs recomposition.
- **Stylistic context:** Classical-era bowing, messa di voce, vibrato as ornament vs
  continuous, gut vs steel strings, Mozart's own violin playing and his letters.
