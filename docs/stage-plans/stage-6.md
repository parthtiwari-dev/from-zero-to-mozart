# Stage 6 — Shifting & the higher positions — the build list

Research: `stage-6-research.md` (21 passes + a re-verification pass that caught and fixed real
note-map errors before anything was built on top of them). This is the build list: the
decisions, the movement-by-movement lesson table, the wiring, and the bar every lesson clears.

## What this stage is for

Every stage built so far stays inside first position. Stage 6 is where the fingerboard opens
up — the entire left-hand half of the "Intermediate" gap between the Foundation gate (end of
Stage 5) and the Intermediate gate (end of Stage 8b). Concretely it pays two debts flagged
back in `LESSONS-FROM-MOZART.md`: the **left-hand side of a chord/double-stop** (Stage 4 built
the theory, Stage 3 the notation, Stage 5 the bow-roll — nobody has built the hand shape yet),
and making the **roman-numeral position reading** from Stage 3 physical instead of abstract.

Nothing here duplicates an earlier stage. The inheritance table in the research doc's intro is
the checklist: reading, notation, theory, and ear training are all already built — Stage 6
only adds the **motor skill** of finding a position, shifting into it, holding a stopped
double stop or chord there, trilling, and touching a harmonic.

## The decisions (Claude's call, per the research doc's flagged items — see below to redirect)

1. **Position order: 1st (recap) → half → 3rd → 5th → 2nd → 4th, with 6th/7th as REFERENCE.**
   This corrects the placeholder order that was in `CLAUDE.md` before this research pass
   (it said 3rd→2nd→4th→5th). Backed by two independent pedagogy sources plus the Suzuki
   book-sequence cross-check (research doc Pass 2). Movements B–D below use the corrected order.
2. **6th/7th position and artificial harmonics are REFERENCE, not performed.** Both are
   genuinely advanced, past what Suzuki introduces before Book 7, and past this course's
   Intermediate-gate scope. A card each: what's up there, the note map (7th = the octave
   harmonic node, on every string), why it's hard.
3. **Pivoting / creeping fingering is a named REFERENCE concept, not a drilled technique.**
   It's a refinement on shifting fluency — useful to know exists, not something this stage
   demands mastery of.
4. **The end-of-stage self-check does not gate anything** (`RULES.md` #4). It's a self-audit
   like `the-foundation-check` and `the-first-position-reading-check`, not a pass/fail wall.
5. **Movement P (repertoire) is deferred, not invented.** Stage 6-appropriate pieces are
   scouted in `REPERTOIRE.md` Tier 2 (Vivaldi A minor RV 356 1st mvt, Suzuki-vein
   3rd-position gavottes), but none has a verified, clean PD source in hand yet — the same
   discipline that deferred Bach *Air* and Gossec *Gavotte* in Stage 5 (`RULES.md` Mistake #4:
   never reconstruct a tune from memory). Sourcing is a separate task, done before Movement P
   is built, not guessed at now.

### Movement A — What a position is, and the first shift (6, NOW)

| # | Slug | Teaches | Cite |

| A2 | `how-the-arm-shifts` | the shift is an arm move, not a finger slide: lower-arm-leads below ~3rd, hand-leads above it; the thumb travels *before* the finger on a downward shift; "no squeeze" in motion (recap Stage 0 `left-hand-frame`, now tested moving) | research Pass 3 (The Strad, citing Fuchs on Heifetz) |
| A3 | `the-four-kinds-of-shift` | Galamian's four shift types (classical/French, combination, romantic/Russian, delayed) + the guide note/finger — what it is and why the ear tracks it even in a "silent" shift | research Pass 4 (Galamian, cross-checked) |

## Build order & wiring

1. **Movement by movement, in the order above: A → B → C → D → E → F → G → H.** A is the
   mechanics every later movement assumes; B/C build the two landmark positions before D fills
   in the landmark-free ones; E/F (double stops, chords) need at least 3rd position in hand;
   G is mostly independent and could interleave; H closes the stage.
2. **The position-note-map table is canon.** Every lesson's fingering must be copied from the
   verified table in `stage-6-research.md` Pass 20 (also reproduced in `D3`), not re-derived —
   that table is the one place this research caught and fixed a real error, so it's the one
   place later lessons must defer to rather than re-guess.
3. Each lesson: `content/lessons/<slug>.mdx` (pure body, no frontmatter) +
   `content/lessons/registry.ts` entry + `lib/curriculum.ts` `LESSONS` entry (`stage: 6`,
   `track: "left-hand"` unless noted, `tag`, `published: P`). **`grep` the slug in
   `curriculum.ts` + `registry.ts` first** (`CLAUDE.md` Mistake #11).
4. **No new components** (`RULES.md` #17) for most of this — `<NotatedExample>` (+ `labels`,
   `reveal`, `followPlayback`, `howToPlay`) and `<Diagram>`-family SVGs cover everything. One
   possible exception worth flagging, not deciding yet: a **fingerboard-map diagram** spanning
   multiple positions (for `the-full-fingerboard-map`, D3) may want a purpose-built `<Diagram>`
   rather than a very long single-line `<NotatedExample>` — decide when D3 is actually written,
   default to reusing `<ViolinDiagram>` if it can be adapted, only add new SVG if it truly can't.
5. **`python tools/abc-barcheck.py` before every commit** — bar sums *and* the label-count
   check (added during Stage 8) both apply here unchanged. Extra care on this stage
   specifically: **cross-check every fingering against the Pass-20 table**, the same way Stage
   4 learned to cross-check octaves (`CLAUDE.md` Mistake #2) — a position-note error is the
   Stage-6 equivalent of an octave error, and just as silent until caught.
6. **Chord/double-stop playability**: every stopped chord or double stop notated must satisfy
   `RULES.md` Mistake #3 (3+ different strings, or an honest caption if it must be rolled/
   broken) — now doubly important because these are chords the reader is meant to actually
   *hold*, not just read.

## The bar every lesson clears

Same six beats as every other stage (`CONTENT-STANDARD.md`): idea → see it → hear it → try it
→ the usual mistake → a cited lineage aside. Given this stage is the most purely
physical/kinesthetic content built so far — and given the accuracy conversation that preceded
this research (2026-09-05: the user asked directly whether the site's claims can be trusted at
face value) — two extra standards apply here that didn't carry the same weight in earlier
stages:

- **Every "try it" beat must be something checkable by the reader's own ear or hand**, not
  just "does this feel right" — the open-string check (3rd position), the thumb/body touch
  (5th), the "wiggle to lock" beating-tone check (double stops). A Stage-6 lesson that can't
  point to a self-check is under-built.
- **State plainly, in-lesson, where a real teacher's eye would catch something this course
  can't.** Not on every lesson — but on the ones asking for a genuinely new physical motion
  (the first shift, the first stopped double stop, the first stopped chord), one honest line
  that a teacher watching the hand can catch things a self-check can't. This isn't hedging;
  it's the same honesty the user was given directly about this stage's research (research doc
  Pass 20's closing note) carried through into the content itself.

## What must not be cut (the load-bearing set)

- **Movement A in full** — nothing later in the stage makes sense without the mechanics,
  timing, and vocabulary it establishes.
- **`third-position` (B1) and `fifth-position` (C1)** — the two landmark positions everything
  else triangulates from (research Pass 2's whole argument for the teaching order).
- **`holding-a-stopped-chord` (F1)** — this is the specific debt `LESSONS-FROM-MOZART.md` §2
  named; skipping it leaves that debt unpaid a second time.
- **`how-to-practise-a-position` (H2) + `the-position-check` (H3)** — Stage 6 only sticks if
  the reader has a repeatable practice method and an honest way to audit themselves, the same
  reason Stage 5/8 both end the same way.
